# From proof plans to a non-proof-plan IR

## Origin

The initial question was whether there is a size limit on turning a coherent document into a decomposed proof plan, and whether this limit is eased when an LLM has already learnt the work during training. *Philosophical Investigations* (PI) seemed an interesting test case: its numbered remarks are not a linear proof, but neither are they an arbitrary collection of aphorisms.

The existing Exegenius argument plan models a source as a global chain of `show`/`infer` intentions. This raises the question of whether the same representation can capture a work whose structure is more fragmentary, or whether a different intermediate representation is needed.

## The three-part idea

A separate practical experiment in Craftseer provided the more immediate motivation. Annotating an LLM chat demonstrating a language game in a “Cliff's notes” style made it possible to generate a similar simulation from scratch, while also producing a useful summary of each section.

This suggested extracting three kinds of information for each part of a document:

- **Content** — what the part says: its concepts, claims, examples and distinctions.
- **Narrative** — what happens in the exposition: how the author introduces, develops, revisits or reframes an idea.
- **Argument** — what the part is doing in the reasoning: what it establishes, challenges, presupposes or motivates.

Only the argument dimension currently has a corresponding proof-oriented plan representation. The proposed `content` and `narrative` dimensions would provide additional ways of representing source structure.

## Why this might be useful

Part-localised drafts need to know more than the subject of a section. They need enough context to explain the section in relation to the surrounding work, without requiring every page to reproduce the whole document.

The three-part representation could therefore serve two purposes:

1. Provide context for drafting individual wiki pages, preserving their relationship to the source's wider exposition.
2. Provide an intermediate representation from which a more global plan can be constructed when the source admits one.

PI makes the second purpose particularly interesting. Its preface explicitly acknowledges the difficulty of making the work cohere. A representation that assumes a single global intention to prove a result may impose the wrong structure from the outset.

## Open question

Does a fragmentary philosophical work have a global structure that can be reconstructed as a plan, or is its coherence better represented through relations between local movements of thought?

The proposed non-proof-plan IR is a hypothesis about how to investigate this, not yet a settled account of PI's structure.

The important distinction is between recovering the source's actual organisation and imposing a plausible organisation that makes it easier to summarise.

One further thought: I'd avoid making the three dimensions mutually exclusive categories. A passage can simultaneously introduce a concept, change the direction of the exposition, and perform an argumentative function. The IR should probably represent these as separate projections of the same source part, rather than forcing each passage into exactly one category. That seems consistent with your aim of preserving both local context and global structure.
