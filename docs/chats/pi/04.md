I think the thread wants to return to the question you started with: is there enough structure in PI to model, and if so, what kind of structure? Rather than introduce more terminology, I'd continue in your existing register, extending the contrast between a glossary and a proof plan.

I'd suggest:

I think the question is whether PI has sufficient structure to model, rather than merely summarise its individual remarks.

I want to model the writer’s *narrative* as well as what the reader needs to know: why a remark occurs where it does, what it is doing, and how it contributes to a larger line of thought.

A prerequisite graph captures one kind of structure (what depends on what). An argument plan captures another: what the author is trying to establish, and how each step advances it.

I’m unsure whether PI admits the latter kind of structure globally, or whether its structure is better modelled as a network of interrelated local arguments.
