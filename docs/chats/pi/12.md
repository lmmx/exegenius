Yes — and that makes the connection to Exegenius more concrete than simply saying that both projects preserve structure across a change of representation.

The Craftseer exchange contained a sequence of show steps through which a language game became intelligible. You weren't just observing a performance and subsequently narrating it. In trying to name the game, you were having a realisation about what the interaction amounted to; in spelling out its rules, you were making the game progressively explicit.

There may be three things happening together:

- Performance: the interaction enacts the language game.
- Realisation: something about the game becomes apparent to you as you try to identify it.
- Showing: you articulate rules that make the game recognisable and potentially reproducible by someone else.

These aren't necessarily three separate stages. The realisation might happen through the attempt to articulate the rules, and the rules might themselves form a sequence of show steps.

That gives you a more interesting link to Exegenius. In the original proof-plan model, a show step contributes to establishing an intended result, while an infer step expresses a relation of inference. But a show step needn't necessarily be a step in a mathematical proof. In Craftseer, spelling out the rules could be a way of showing someone what the language game is — making its organisation intelligible without deducing a theorem.

Perhaps the common thread is the reconstruction of an intention through steps that make something intelligible. In a mathematical paper, those steps might establish a result. In Craftseer, they might make a language game recognisable. In Philosophical Investigations, they might help a reader see a conceptual distinction or recognise what is happening in an example.

That last case is particularly relevant to your problem: a sequence of show steps need not constitute a linear proof, but it may still have an intelligible direction. The challenge is to represent that direction without forcing it into a proof-shaped structure.

One question I'd keep open is whether the Craftseer steps were already a sequence of shows in your original exchange, or whether annotating the exchange made that sequence visible retrospectively. That distinction could matter to Exegenius: are you extracting an existing structure, or constructing an explanatory structure that captures what the source enabled you to realise?
