> **AI systems are getting better at having good research taste:**\
> *…The better their taste, the higher the chance of recursive self-improvement…*\
> AI  systems are developing more of the creative intuitions which human  researchers ascribe to ‘good scientific taste’, according to a new  benchmark from P-Zero Research called TasteVal.
>
> **What TasteVal tests:** The benchmark sees how well AI  systems can conduct 8 tasks representative of frontier AI development,  “spanning pretraining data curation, pretraining and fine-tuning  language models, preference modeling, and robustness to adversarial  prompts”.\
> &#x20;   The goal here is to “define research taste as the  ability to pick interesting problems to solve, design experiments, and  interpret experimental results.” The benchmark works by measuring “the  experimental component of research taste; given a fixed research  problem, we measure how well a model iteratively designs experiments and  draws conclusions from their outcomes”.\
> &#x20;   The model being analyzed  designs experiments and interprets their results. Results are checked  against a human baseline “drawn from 24 experts (at least two per task)  who have recently worked at organizations including OpenAI, Google  DeepMind, NVIDIA and Microsoft Research”.
>
> **Results:&#x20;**“The experimental research taste of  frontier models has doubled every 3.0 months since December 2025 (95% CI  1.7-5.0 months). The best model, Opus 5.5, significantly exceeds our  human baseline with a compute multiplier of 2.30x (95% CI 1.15-4.37x),  at roughly 1/30 of our expert baseliners’ average cost per attempt.”  Research taste appeared to grow a lot more slowly in prior years with  the slope changing around the end of 2025 and beginning of 2026.
>
> **Why this matters – RSI:** In my analysis of recursive self-improvement (*AI Systems Are About To Start Building Themselves,&#x20;*[Import AI 455](https://importai.substack.com/p/import-ai-455-automating-ai-research?utm_source=post-email-title\&publication_id=1317673\&post_id=196354615\&utm_campaign=email-post-title\&isFreemail=true\&r=854n6\&triedRedirect=true\&utm_medium=email)),  I repeatedly noted that creativity seemed like a key variable for how  fast AI systems could develop the capacity to improve themselves. I think  subsequent to writing that we’ve seen both a tremendous advancement in  the ability for AI systems to make creative-seeming math breakthroughs, *and* with benchmarks like this we see some signs they’re becoming increasingly good at AI research as well.\
