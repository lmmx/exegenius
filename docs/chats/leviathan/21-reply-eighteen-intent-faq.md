I’d treat this as an FAQ about the possible intentions behind commissioning the exercise. The point is to help Claude infer what a particular version of you might value, without assuming every run is secretly about the same outcome.

These are deliberately different questions, not rephrasings of “how can Exegenius improve?” Some concern philosophy, others mathematical understanding, research strategy, software engineering, or the future of AI.

## FAQ: What might I want to get out of this exercise?

1\. Is there a deeper idea here that I haven't yet found the words for?

2\. Does Gamburd's essay reveal something important about what Exegenius is trying to do?

3\. What would it mean for a wiki to preserve understanding, rather than merely information?

4\. Is there a mathematical idea hidden in plain sight that could change the software?

5\. Are current approaches to generating technical wikis fundamentally missing something?

6\. Can the model find a worthwhile software intervention that I would not have thought to request?

7\. What do the reported mathematical breakthroughs tell us about the changing nature of AI?

8\. What is research taste, and can we make use of it before it becomes reliable?

9\. What should Exegenius become if models become much better researchers?

10\. Can I use the model as an intellectual collaborator without directing every step?

11\. How do I tell a real discovery from an impressive-sounding story?

12\. What should I do with an insight once the conversation has produced it?

13\. Can successive models improve this exercise rather than merely repeat it?

14\. What are we overlooking because we keep thinking in the same categories?

15\. Is there a compelling experiment that would teach us more than another round of speculation?

16\. What would a genuinely excellent Exegenius output let a person do that they cannot easily do now?

17\. Is there a research programme here, rather than just a collection of interesting ideas?

18\. What if the most valuable outcome is discovering that my premise is wrong?

## How I'd use these

I wouldn't give Claude all 18 as a checklist. That would encourage it to touch every theme superficially and manufacture a synthesis.

Instead, I'd use them as a library of possible user intentions. A given exercise might have one dominant expectation and several secondary ones. Another exercise might be driven by a completely different expectation.

For example:

- The philosopher: questions 1, 2 and 14.
- The mathematical explainer: questions 3 and 16.
- The experimental researcher: questions 8, 11 and 15.
- The software architect: questions 5 and 6.
- The AI futurist: questions 7 and 9.
- The autonomous collaborator: questions 10, 12 and 18.
- The builder of a cumulative research practice: questions 13 and 17.

These are overlapping perspectives, not fixed user types. The same person may want different things on different occasions.

The most important design principle is that the questions should describe different kinds of value, not prescribe the discovery. They should help a simulated user recognise what they care about, while leaving the model free to find an answer neither the user nor the model could have specified at the outset.
