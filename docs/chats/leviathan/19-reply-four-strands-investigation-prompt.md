# An open-ended investigation: Exegenius, Enlightenment, mathematical creativity, and research taste

I want you to investigate a set of ideas in relation to Exegenius, with the possibility of arriving at a genuinely new insight and carrying its consequences into the software.

There are several distinct strands to this investigation. Treat each seriously in its own right before considering what they might reveal about one another. Do not force them into a unified thesis prematurely.

## 1. Gamburd's essay and the implicit philosophy of Exegenius

Read Alexander Gamburd's *The Siren Call of Silicon Leviathan: Reflections on blowup and Aufklärungsdämmerung*:

[https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.28591)

Understand the essay on its own terms, including its mathematical and philosophical arguments and its use of Enlightenment as an intellectual frame.

Then study Exegenius. Look at the actual software, documentation, design decisions, experiments, and the ambitions that seem to motivate the project. Do not restrict yourself to what I have explicitly said I want it to achieve.

What, if anything, does the essay illuminate about Exegenius? Might the software be understood as implicitly pursuing a philosophical project that its current framing does not adequately express? Does the essay expose a tension between what Exegenius produces and what it is really trying to accomplish? Does it suggest an objective, distinction or possibility that is currently missing?

Take the comparison seriously enough to discover where it fails, too. Do not simply map philosophical language onto existing features.

## 2. What follows for the actual software?

Independently of whether the connection in the first strand proves fruitful, investigate what its strongest insights would imply for Exegenius.

I do not want a list of features that might be nice to have, a philosophical essay with a software recommendation attached, or a conversation that ends with suggestions for me to implement.

If you find something worth pursuing, work out what follows from it. The consequence might be a change to the conceptual framing, documentation, evaluation, architecture, code, or something not yet contemplated. It might instead be a test that determines whether a promising interpretation is true.

Pursue the most consequential implication you can justify. Where appropriate, make the intervention and gather evidence that it helps. Do not manufacture an intervention merely to ensure that the investigation produces a code change.

## 3. The reported mathematical breakthroughs: what kind of capability is emerging?

Consider the reports collected by prinz concerning the mathematical work of OpenAI's internal model:

[https://hraness.com/reading/mathematician-reactions-to-openais-372-math-results](https://hraness.com/reading/mathematician-reactions-to-openais-372-math-results)

In particular, examine the reactions to work on Barnette's Conjecture, the chromatic number of the plane, and the Navier–Stokes problem. The reported reactions suggest that some of the methods are not merely applications of familiar techniques or combinations of known results, but may involve unexpected conceptual moves and the invention of new mathematical mechanisms.

Investigate these reports critically. What do the examples actually suggest about the nature of the model's capabilities? What would distinguish genuine conceptual invention from sophisticated synthesis, and what evidence would let us tell the difference? What remains uncertain, especially where the mathematical work, its novelty or its relationship to prior literature has not been independently established?

Then consider the implications for Exegenius. If models increasingly become capable of discovering mathematical ideas that their users would not have anticipated, what changes about the role of software that helps people understand mathematical papers?

Do not assume the answer is simply to put a more capable model into the existing pipeline. Consider what the changing capability means for the problem Exegenius is trying to solve, for the kinds of output that matter, and for what the software might eventually enable.

## 4. Jack Clark, TasteVal, research taste, and the near future of models

Read Jack Clark's *Import AI 476: Creativity and RSI; worse-than-China open weight models; AI goes to space*, especially the section on research taste and the TasteVal benchmark:

[https://jack-clark.net/2026/10/12/import-ai-476-creativity-and-rsi-worse-than-china-open-weight-models-ai-goes-to-space/](https://jack-clark.net/2026/10/12/import-ai-476-creativity-and-rsi-worse-than-china-open-weight-models-ai-goes-to-space/)

Examine the distinction between mathematical creativity and experimental research taste. TasteVal addresses the latter: selecting promising research directions within a problem, designing experiments, and interpreting their outcomes. Clark discusses reported rapid improvement on this benchmark and the implications he draws for recursive self-improvement.

Assess what the benchmark measures, what its reported results support, and what they do not establish. Consider Clark's forecast in that light rather than treating it as a certainty.

Now consider the implications for this exercise itself.

What happens when models become better at identifying valuable questions, inventing approaches, testing hypotheses, interpreting evidence and deciding what to pursue next? Could a model do more than respond creatively to a question supplied by a human? Could it identify an important connection, recognise that it changes the problem, devise an intervention, and establish whether the intervention works?

Which parts of that process can already be elicited and evaluated with current models, and which depend on capabilities that may improve substantially in the near future?

How might we turn the investigation described here into a repeatable exercise that benefits from those improvements, rather than a one-off conversation whose value depends on the particular model that happens to conduct it?

## 5. Bring the strands into contact

Only after investigating the strands independently, consider their possible interplay.

Does Gamburd's account of Enlightenment help us understand why mathematical discovery and research taste matter for Exegenius? Do the reported breakthroughs change the significance of the essay's concerns? Does the distinction between inventing an idea and judging what to investigate next reveal something important about what Exegenius should do? Does the software's potential role change as models become more capable of both?

Look for connections that are not obvious from any one strand in isolation. Equally, preserve distinctions that turn out to matter. A connection is valuable only if it explains something, changes a judgement, opens a useful possibility or leads to a testable consequence.

Do not assume that the best outcome is a single grand synthesis. You may find several independent insights, one particularly important connection, a productive tension, or a reason that these ideas should remain separate.

## 6. Pursue the discovery

I have deliberately not specified the conclusion I expect, the connection you should find, or the intervention you should make.

Use your own informed judgement. Develop promising lines of thought, challenge your initial interpretations, inspect the evidence, and follow unexpected leads. Do not merely enumerate possibilities and leave the interesting work to me.

If a promising insight emerges, determine whether it changes how Exegenius should be understood or developed. Then pursue its consequences far enough to establish whether it is worth taking seriously. Make a proportionate intervention in the repository where justified; otherwise, produce the evidence or analysis needed to decide what should happen next.

Keep the reasoning traceable. Distinguish the essay's claims, the reports about model capabilities, your own inferences, and what you establish through work on the software. Record important alternatives and negative findings, not just the path that ultimately looks successful.

The outcome I want is not a predetermined deliverable. It is the possibility of an intellectual discovery that changes how I understand Exegenius, what I think it should become, or how I develop it—and a concrete consequence when that discovery warrants one.

Finally, consider how this investigation itself could become a repeatable research practice: one in which future models can independently rediscover, challenge or surpass earlier insights, and in which the quality of the discoveries and their consequences can be evaluated rather than judged solely by how persuasive the resulting prose sounds.

The key is that the repeatable unit is the entire intellectual journey: interpreting an external idea, understanding your software, investigating emerging model capabilities, discovering connections, and following the implications. The prompt doesn't assume that every strand must contribute equally or that every session will produce a breakthrough.

The TasteVal piece matters especially because it raises a question about the method itself: can you set up an exercise in which a model's ability to find and pursue worthwhile ideas becomes more useful as that ability improves? Clark's reported benchmark results are motivation to investigate that possibility, not proof that the whole process can already be automated.&#x20;
