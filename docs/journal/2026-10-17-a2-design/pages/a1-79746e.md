Fast interchange of address fields is a fixed-tape procedure that swaps two disjoint contiguous address fields of an array, and the multiplication construction uses it to speed up its address moves. The procedure acts on arrays laid out as in [Address fields and array layout on tapes](address-fields.md), so each field is a block of $u$ contiguous bits. Write $H$ and $D$ for the two fields, each viewed as a vector of entries over a common residue ring; for the bit procedure this ring is $\mathbb F_2$, where subtraction and addition coincide. Here $\tau$, the fixed exponent below 1 in the cost, is the exponent in $O(Vu^\tau)$ <cite data-source="paper">contains $V/W$ bits</cite>. (On the address-shear page, $\tau$ is a column index; here it means only this exponent.)

Swapping takes three updates, applied componentwise:

$$D\leftarrow D-H,\qquad H\leftarrow H+D,\qquad D\leftarrow H-D.$$

Starting from $(H,D)$ they give $(H,D-H)$, then $(D,D-H)$, then $(D,H)$, since $H+(D-H)=D$ and $D-(D-H)=H$. For example, with $(H,D)=(1,0)$ over $\mathbb F_2$ the states are $(1,1)$, $(0,1)$, $(0,1)$, which is $(D,H)$.

The network performs only the middle update, which is the costly one. The procedure is built from the [Address shear and lower-triangular factorization](address-shear-factorization.md): the network's endpoint operator is the shear $(H,D)\mapsto(H+D,D)$ over $\mathbb F_2$, with rank reduced by a lower-triangular factorization. Two more linear-cost address updates complete the interchange. Both change only $D$, each combining $D$ with the earlier field $H$ in linear time.

The cost bound follows from [Recursive power saving from s<Wm](recursive-power-saving.md). The network has $W$ roles, and each of its $s$ smaller calls acts on one role stream of volume $V/W$. For the bit network, $s<W$ <cite data-source="paper">the total number $s$ of smaller calls satisfies $s<Wm$</cite>; the paper's proof computes $s$ as $Wm$ minus a positive constant fixed by the construction. Hence $s/W<m$. Since $m^\tau\to m$ as $\tau\to1$, fix $\tau<1$ with $s/W<m^\tau$.

Let $Q_k$ bound the time per unit of volume for interchanging fields of $m^k$ bits, where $k$ is the recursion level. A level-$k$ call makes $s$ calls at level $k-1$, each on volume $V/W$, plus $O(V)$ outside work whose normalized cost is at most $C$. Hence
$$Q_0=O(1),\qquad Q_k\le \frac{s}{W}\,Q_{k-1}+C.$$
Unrolling gives $Q_k\le (s/W)^k Q_0+C\sum_{j<k}(s/W)^j$. The first term is $O(m^{k\tau})$ because $s/W<m^\tau$. The geometric sum is also $O(m^{k\tau})$: if $s/W\le1$ it is at most $k$, and if $s/W>1$ it is $O((s/W)^k)$. Both are $O(m^{k\tau})$, since $m\ge2$. A field of $u=m^k$ bits therefore has $Q_k=O(u^\tau)$, and the interchange costs $O(Vu^\tau)$.

The final effect is an exact permutation, although the network uses only pointwise XOR gates, so its intermediate values are XOR combinations of data. Over $\mathbb F_2$ each update is a pointwise XOR, since addition and subtraction are the same operation; after the first update $D$ holds the XOR of $D$ and $H$. The shear is applied to every row, including scratch rows with arbitrary contents. Exactness is secured by the [Restoring auxiliary scratch values](scratch-restoration.md) schedule, which subtracts the initial register value, computes, adds the result back, and reverses; the initial contents cancel, so the final permutation does not depend on them. <cite data-source="paper">The final permutation is exact for arbitrary initial auxiliary values</cite>.
