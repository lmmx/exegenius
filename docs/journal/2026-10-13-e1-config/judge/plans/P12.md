# Plan P12

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: reduce to convolution, accelerate transforms by two network-based tape procedures, then recover the product
  - motivation (reconstructed): Beating n log n needs a power saving in the log factor, which requires cheaper transform levels (S3) inside the standard multiplication-by-convolution reduction (S2, S4) with exact recovery (S5).
  - relies on: multitape-tm, big-o
  - **S1** (infer, page `multiplication-history`): Schönhage and Strassen conjectured that $n\log n$ is the optimal order of multiplication cost, and Harvey and van der Hoeven reached $O(n\log n)$, so only a power saving in the log factor can beat it.
    - quotes line 41: "proposed $n\log n$ as the optimal order of growth"
  - **S2** (infer, page `digit-convolution-reduction`): Splitting both integers into radix digits reduces multiplication to convolution of short integer coefficient lists.
    - relies on: convolution, binary-representation-of-integers, integer-addition-multiplication-and-carry-propagation
    - quotes line 76: "Splitting the inputs into radix digits reduces multiplication to"
  - **S3** (show): Two tape procedures, a fast address-field interchange and a simultaneous butterfly layer, cheapen both data movement and arithmetic of the transform levels.
    - method: supply two fast procedures from one linear-network construction
    - motivation (explicit): Plain movement and arithmetic cost n log n over log n levels, so a power saving needs both to be cheaper; one network idea provides both.
    - **S3.1** (infer, page `address-interchange-bottleneck`): Moving data between $\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels costs $\Theta(n\log n)$ by plain scanning, so both movement and arithmetic must be made cheaper.
      - relies on: tape-streams, sorting-and-linear-tape-scans, multitape-tm
      - quotes line 91: "$\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$."
    - **S3.2** (show): Both tape procedures come from a fixed linear network whose total number $s$ of smaller calls is less than $Wm$, and the recursion on it saves a power of the parameter.
      - method: build a finite network with a rank deficit, then recurse on it
      - motivation (reconstructed): Each procedure is a network of pointwise gates on $W$ role streams; making the gates' edge changes cost fewer smaller calls than the trivial bound gives a recurrence with contraction, as one needs a strict saving in each recursion level.
      - **S3.2.1** (infer, page `linear-network-roles`, reported out of order): A fixed linear network exchanges two banks of values with signs and restores auxiliary values; each wire has a role and invertible frames $D_v$ change its stored representation, cancelling across the network.
        - relies on: matrices-rank-and-invertibility, vector-spaces-subspaces-and-linear-maps
        - quotes line 643: "We construct two fixed linear networks that exchange two banks of values"
      - **S3.2.2** (infer, page `finite-subset-network`, reported out of order): A fixed finite network with nested subspaces attached to gates, built from three-element subsets with gather/scatter and cancelling auxiliary wires, makes the total number $s$ of smaller calls satisfy $s<Wm$.
        - relies on: linear-network-roles, sets-subsets-functions-and-permutations, vector-spaces-subspaces-and-linear-maps
        - quotes line 997: "\begin{lemma}\label{lem:motif-residuals}"
      - **S3.2.3** (infer, page `recursive-saving`, reported out of order): A call at parameter $mf$ uses $s$ calls at parameter $f$ on volume $V/W$ each, so cost per volume grows by $s/W<m$ per multiplication of the parameter by $m$, a power saving.
        - relies on: finite-subset-network, linear-network-roles, recurrence-relations-and-the-master-theorem
        - quotes line 1317: "\subsection{Explicit rational bounds for the two recurrences}"
    - **S3.3** (show): Two disjoint contiguous address fields of $u$ bits in an array of volume $V$ can be interchanged in time $O(Vu^\tau)$ with a fixed $\tau<1$.
      - method: instantiate the bit network with rational subspaces and implement edges by address shears
      - motivation (recoverable): The address interchange is a permutation whose cost can be cut by the network recursion, and the edge operators are address shears that a factorization makes cheap.
      - relies on: recursive-saving, controlled-shifts
      - **S3.3.1** (infer, page `address-shear-factorization`, reported out of order): Every rational matrix factors as lower-triangular times partial permutation times lower-triangular, so an address shear $(H,D)\mapsto(H+D,D)$ costs by rank.
        - relies on: controlled-shifts, matrices-rank-and-invertibility, gaussian-elimination
        - quotes line 1366: "Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ has a factorization"
      - **S3.3.2** (infer, page `fast-address-interchange`, reported out of order): Interchanging two address fields via XOR combinations of data and address shears costs $O(Vu^\tau)$ with $\tau<1$, the final effect being an exact permutation.
        - relies on: recursive-saving, xor-and-bitwise-operations
        - quotes line 1481: "Choose a rational number $\tau\in(0,1)$ with $s/W<m^\tau$."
    - **S3.4** (show, page `fast-butterfly-layers`): Applying $H_0(u,v)=((u+v)/2,(u-v)/2)$ simultaneously to selected coordinate bits of numerical arrays costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with a fixed $\lambda'<1$.
      - method: instantiate the complex phase network and bound its numerical layout and precision
      - motivation (recoverable): A butterfly layer is a linear pointwise operation, so the same network trick that cheapens the address interchange applies, provided the dyadic values stay exact and the packed address changes are cheap.
      - relies on: recursive-saving, fast-address-interchange
      - **S3.4.1** (infer, page `fixed-point-dyadics`, reported out of order): Intermediate values of a butterfly layer are exact Gaussian dyadics and a single truncation follows the completed contractive layer.
        - relies on: complex-numbers, fixed-point-and-finite-precision-arithmetic, norms-error-bounds-and-triangle-inequality
        - quotes line 111: "Intermediate values are exact Gaussian dyadics,"
      - **S3.4.2** (infer, reported out of order): Phase frames express a butterfly layer on selected bits through smaller groups of selected bits separated by binary changes of basis.
        - quotes line 1809: "Hold the unselected address bits fixed and regard the stored complex"
      - **S3.4.3** (infer, page `packed-selected-bit-addition`, reported out of order): Adding selected bit positions across rectangles of packed records on tapes costs linear time with a deterministic repair of exceptional addresses.
        - relies on: controlled-shifts, fast-address-interchange, xor-and-bitwise-operations
        - quotes line 1974: "\begin{lemma}[Packed selected-bit addition on one rectangle]"
      - **S3.4.4** (infer, reported out of order): The simultaneous normalized butterfly layer on at most $d$ chunks costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with $\lambda'<1$.
        - quotes line 2588: "\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous normalized butterfly layer]"
  - **S4** (show): The digit convolution reduces, by a chain of changes of problem and representation, to convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$.
    - method: chain of reductions: prime cyclic axes, power-of-two axes, polynomial ring
    - motivation (reconstructed): The fast tape procedures and synthetic transforms apply only to power-of-two axes with negacyclic ring coordinate, while the digit convolution has no such shape.
    - relies on: convolution, dft-fft
    - **S4.1** (infer, page `cyclic-crt-axes`, reported out of order): The radix-digit list is placed on a product of cyclic axes of distinct prime lengths by a Chinese-remainder address map that preserves convolution.
      - relies on: chinese-remainder-theorem, convolution, multidimensional-arrays-and-mixed-radix-indexing, cyclic-groups-and-products-of-groups
      - quotes line 4195: "\subsection{An explicit cyclic-convolution layout}"
    - **S4.2** (infer, page `gaussian-resampling`, reported out of order): Gaussian resampling expresses each prime-length Fourier transform through a power-of-two tensor transform with source and target permutations retained.
      - relies on: dft-fft, multidim-transform, cyclic-crt-axes, roots-of-unity
      - quotes line 3548: "\begin{lemma}[Resampling without executing the two permutations]"
    - **S4.3** (infer, page `bluestein-chirp`, reported out of order): Bluestein's chirp identity turns the middle transform into a normalized cyclic convolution multiplied by chirp phases.
      - relies on: dft-fft, convolution, gaussian-resampling
      - quotes line 3911: "\begin{lemma}[Chirp and permutation cancellation]"
    - **S4.4** (infer, page `phase-twist`, reported out of order): A last-coordinate phase twist turns a cyclic coordinate into a polynomial coordinate modulo $y^r+1$; removing the twist restores the cyclic wrap.
      - relies on: poly-ring-negacyclic, roots-of-unity, bluestein-chirp
      - quotes line 4298: "\subsection{Complex convolution by a coefficient twist}"
    - **S4.5** (show): Convolution over the remaining axes is computed over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ by forward synthetic transforms, pointwise ring products and the opposite transform.
      - method: transform, multiply pointwise, transform back
      - motivation (recoverable): This is the convolution theorem: transforms turn convolution into pointwise products, and the ring makes twiddle multiplications shifts.
      - relies on: convolution, dft-fft
      - **S4.5.1** (infer, page `synthetic-transforms`, reported out of order): Synthetic polynomial transforms use powers of $y$ as roots of unity, so twiddles are signed shifts, and two forward transforms retain one frequency order through the pointwise product.
        - relies on: poly-ring-negacyclic, dft-fft, multidim-transform
        - quotes line 2929: "\begin{lemma}[Transform layout and cost]"
      - **S4.5.2** (infer, page `normalized-ring-products`, reported out of order): Pointwise polynomial products in the ring are computed by signed packing (Kronecker substitution) and the standard $O(n\log n)$ multiplier as an ordinary subroutine.
        - relies on: polynomials, poly-ring-negacyclic, integer-addition-multiplication-and-carry-propagation
        - quotes line 3221: "\begin{lemma}[Signed packing and normalized ring multiplication]"
  - **S5** (show): The approximate transforms recover the exact integer product, and the total cost is $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$.
    - method: bound the rounding error below 1/2 and then add up costs
    - motivation (recoverable): Rounding to the nearest integer is exact only once the error is below 1/2; the cost is a sum over steps controlled by the chosen parameters.
    - **S5.1** (infer, page `precision-exact-recovery`, reported out of order): The known normalizations and digit scales are removed, the recovered coefficient error is strictly below $1/2$, and carries are propagated to give the exact binary product.
      - relies on: fixed-point-and-finite-precision-arithmetic, norms-error-bounds-and-triangle-inequality, floor-ceiling-logarithms-and-rounding, integer-addition-multiplication-and-carry-propagation
      - quotes line 4546: "\subsection{Precision and exact recovery}"
    - **S5.2** (infer, reported out of order): A compatible choice of rational parameters makes all terms in the cost table fit $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$.
      - relies on: constants-asymptotic-functions-and-polylog-factors, big-o
      - quotes line 4808: "\subsection{The complete time bound}"
  - **S6** (infer): The power saving gives a limit of zero for $T_A(n)/(n\lg n)$, so the $n\log n$ lower bound conjecture fails in this model.
    - relies on: multiplication-history
    - quotes line 386: "The power saving proved here gives a limit of zero through all input lengths,"
