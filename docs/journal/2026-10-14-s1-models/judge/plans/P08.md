# Plan P08

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): One deterministic fixed-alphabet multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: reduce the product to a cyclic convolution of radix digits, move it by a Chinese-remainder map and Gaussian resampling to power-of-two axes, compute that convolution with synthetic polynomial transforms accelerated by two network-based tape procedures, recover the exact coefficients by rounding, and bound every cost term
  - motivation (explicit): The Harvey and van der Hoeven route already reaches $O(n\log n)$; its remaining cost is a full scan per transform level for both address rearrangement and butterflies, so a power saving in those two tape operations lowers the whole bound.
  - relies on: nlogn-conjecture, hvdh-multiplier, multitape-turing-machine, deterministic-algorithm, worst-case-running-time, big-o-notation, logarithms-and-exponentials
  - **S1** (show): Two tape procedures save a power of their parameter: chunk interchange in $O(Vu^\tau)$ and a simultaneous butterfly layer in $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, with $\tau,\lambda'<1$.

    - motivation (explicit): Scanning $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so both data movement and butterfly arithmetic must be cheaper than one scan per level.
