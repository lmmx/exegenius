# 2026-10-13: E1, the planner definition against a general-purpose subagent at a fixed model and effort

**The finding.** At the same model (Sonnet) and the same effort (high), plans made by `exegenius-planner` subagents and by general-purpose subagents are indistinguishable by the content judge:
- **both arms:** a median of 19 of 19 core results stated in substance, no major errors, and wide variation within each arm;
- **the gap of 2026-10-13-content-judge is therefore not the agent definition's;**
- **the planner at high, against the planner at medium:** on a near-identical binary and configuration, the planner at high states, derives and follows the paper far more than the same planner at medium did. The medium plans are the depth experiment's section-rule arm.

**Effort is the leading explanation of the earlier gap.** It is not yet shown by a run made for the purpose: the two efforts were run at different times and judged in different batches.

## Design

Proposed in 2026-10-13-model-allocation, with effort fixed as 2026-10-13-effort-dimension requires. The protocol is `EXPERIMENT.md` on wiki branch `131026-exp-e1-config` (9afb736). The results, the runner's report and every transcript are at 85611ce.

**The arms:** ten planning-only runs, P1–P5 and G1–G5.
- **The same for both:** binary `cc70355`, configuration (`coverage = "reject"`, `coverage_depth = "sections"`), definitions and settings written by `exegenius init`, and the prompts `exegenius next` printed, word for word.
- **What differed:** every planning task in the P runs went to `exegenius-planner`, and in the G runs to `general-purpose`.
- **Overrides:** every Agent call passed `model: "sonnet"` and `effort: "high"`.
- **Verified from the transcripts** (`effort.tsv`, by `2026-10-13-effort-dimension/effort.py`): all 30 subagents ran on `claude-sonnet-5-5` at `effort` high, every turn. The runner ran on Opus at medium.

**The judge:** that of 2026-10-13-content-judge, unchanged.
- **Inputs:** the same pack (byte-identical) and the same instructions, with general-purpose Opus subagents as judges.
- **Plans:** the ten E1 plans and two anchors from the first batch (`sonnet1`, `C2`), rendered by the same `prepare.py` and shuffled with seed 20261014.
- **Blinding:** the key was held outside the judges' directory until every verdict was in.
- **Second judgements:** three plans (P03, P09, P12), drawn by `random.Random(20261014).sample`, were judged twice, independently.

## Interpretation, not shown

- **The definition does not matter at high effort.** The general-purpose arm's slightly higher medians for derived (10 against 8) and faithful (5 against 3) are within the spread of either arm with five runs.
- **Effort is the likeliest cause of the planner's early stops and shallow plans at medium.** The C-arm comparison changes little else, and the stricter batch would, if anything, understate the gap.
- **What it does not exclude:**
  - an interaction between effort and the one changed sentence of the task file, which is unlikely to touch reading;
  - drift in the model's serving between the runs;
  - a difference between batches that favours E1. The anchors argue against that: `sonnet1` scored about the same, and `C2` scored lower.
- **High effort fixes reading, not the hardest derivation.** The saving $s<Wm$ is still derived faithfully by no plan, so a further gain, if any, lies there. A higher effort, Opus, or a planning task aimed at that construction could each be tried, one at a time.

## What follows from E1

**Step 3 of the author's plan does not apply.** It investigates the definition's differences if the general-purpose arm wins, and it did not. No ablation of the definition is run.

**Proposed next, in order of expected value for cost:**

1. **E3, a bounded effort comparison on planning: `exegenius-planner` on Sonnet at medium against high.**
   - **Design:** five runs each, in one runner, on `cc70355`, judged in one batch with the same anchors.
   - **Why:** it replaces the C-arm comparison with a run made for the purpose.
   - **Cost:** about 10 USD for the medium arm and 28 for the high arm (by the runner reports above), plus 12–15 Opus judgements.
   - **Criterion, fixed in advance:** if high exceeds medium by at least 4 in median core derived and at least 2 in median faithful, the planning profile should run at high. Planning is 3–7 of a full run's roughly 212 tasks, so its added cost is small against writing and auditing.
   - **No xhigh or max yet,** as the author asked.
2. **A full run on `master`, with planning at high**, otherwise as the planner and handoff run.
   - **Why:** this is the measurement 2026-10-13-convergence-before-after lacks. Does a plan that states and derives the paper's results reduce what rewrites must add, and so the renewal of step gaps across audit rounds?
   - **Cost:** the largest here, a full run.
   - **Measures:** the convergence tables of that entry, and the content judge on its plan.
3. **The writers' and auditors' effort and model** (E4, E5 of 2026-10-13-model-allocation).
   - **Why:** they dominate a run's cost.
   - **What it needs first:** a page judge, and audit variance measured on fixed pages.
4. **The hardest derivation.**
   - **The question:** whether a higher effort or Opus on the argument task derives the $s<Wm$ construction faithfully.
   - **When:** only after E3, and only with the author's agreement, since it introduces effort levels he asked to hold back.

**The planner's definition** could carry `effort: high` in its frontmatter, so that it no longer takes the orchestrator's effort. That is a change of allocation, and it stays unimplemented until reviewed, with the profiles of 2026-10-13-model-allocation.
