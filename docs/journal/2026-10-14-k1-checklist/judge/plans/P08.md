# Plan P08

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): One deterministic Turing machine with a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed number of one-dimensional tapes outputs $\operatorname{bin}_{2n}(\operatorname{val}(x)\operatorname{val}(y))$ for every $n\ge1$ and all $n$-bit inputs $x,y$, in worst-case time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: Reduce multiplication to a cyclic convolution evaluated by Fourier transforms on power-of-two axes, accelerate the transforms' address rearrangements and butterfly layers by two tape procedures built from fixed linear networks with a rank saving, and bound every cost divided by the volume $V=Tp=\Theta(n)$.
  - motivation (explicit): A transform-based multiplier that scans its $\Theta(n)$-bit array once per level of a $\Theta(\log n)$-level transform already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$; beating the conjecture requires making both the data movement and the butterfly arithmetic of the levels cheaper than full scans.
  - relies on: integer-multiplication-conjecture, multitape-tm, big-o, logarithms-and-iterated-logarithm-notation, big-omega-theta-and-little-o-notation, finite-alphabet-encodings-and-constant-factor-simulation
  - **S0.1** (infer): To refute the eventual $\Omega(n\lg n)$ lower bound in the fixed-tape model it suffices to give one machine with $\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_A(n)/(n\lg n)=0$, and a bound $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ gives limit zero through all input lengths.
    - relies on: integer-multiplication-conjecture, big-omega-theta-and-little-o-notation
    - quotes line 384: "Its negation requires one machine satisfying
$\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_A(n)/(n\lg n)=0$."
  - **S1** (show, page `address-chunk-interchange`): Two disjoint contiguous address chunks of $u$ bits in an arbitrary bit array on $[P]\times[2^u]\times[G]\times[2^u]\times[B]$ can be interchanged, $(p,h,g,d,z)\mapsto(p,d,g,h,z)$, in $O(Vu^\tau)$ steps with a fixed $\tau<1$, where $V=PGB\,2^{2u}$.
    - method: Prove the bound for chunks of $e=m^k$ radix-$q$ digits with a row field divisible by $W^k$ through the bit network, then manufacture that row field from a few high digits, split the remaining width in base $m$, and pad binary chunks to radix $q$.
    - motivation (explicit): The network recursion needs a row field long enough to be split $k$ times into $W$ role streams; borrowing $O(\log e)$ high digits of the chunks supplies it at a cost below the target bound.
    - relies on: rank-saving-recurrence, tape-address-model, elementary-stream-ops, permutations-and-cartesian-products, big-o
    - **S1.1** (show, page `rank-saving-recurrence`): For $e=m^k$, two disjoint chunks of $e$ radix-$q$ digits preceded by a row field whose length is divisible by $W^k$ can be interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time, because the time per volume obeys $F_k\le(s/W)F_{k-1}+O(1)$ with $s/W<m^\tau$.
      - method: Deal whole rows cyclically to $W$ role streams, run the fixed bit network on them pointwise with address-shear frames whose endpoint differences are the identity, implement each edge's change of frame by as many smaller interchanges as the rank of its matrix, and solve the resulting recurrence.
      - motivation (explicit): Dividing cost by volume, one level of recursion multiplies the parameter by $m$ but the recursive contribution only by $s/W$; the strict inequality $s<Wm$ is exactly what turns the trivial cost linear in $e$ into $e^\tau$ with $\tau<1$.
      - relies on: linear-network, common-frame-principle, subset-intersection-labels, address-shear, elementary-stream-ops, tape-address-model, recurrences-and-induction, big-o
