The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): There is one deterministic fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape Turing machine that multiplies $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.
  - method: Reduce the product to a convolution evaluated by Fourier-type transforms over a polynomial ring, speed up the transforms' address movement and butterfly arithmetic with two recursive tape procedures that each save a power of the width, then assemble the reduction and balance the parameters so that every cost row stays a power below $\log n$.
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning the data at each of the logarithmically many transform levels already costs $n\log n$, so both movement and arithmetic must be made cheaper.
  - relies on: fixed-tape-model, nlogn-conjecture, nlogn-multiplier, big-o, asymptotic-notation-beyond-big-o, determinism-and-exact-versus-approximate-computation

    - motivation (explicit): Because the total rank is below the wire count times the dimension, the recurrence saves a power of the chunk width.
    - relies on: fixed-tape-model, array-order, binary-field-f2-and-xor
