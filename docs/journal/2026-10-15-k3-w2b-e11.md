## K3: pairwise judging validates (`calibration/plans/K3.md`)

Blind pairwise judging, with both orders and Opus judges, was validated against known orderings from the E7–E9 plans. It passes all four preregistered criteria:
- **Known orderings:** 16 of 16 verdicts on known pairs are correct.
- **Primary items:** 37 of 37 items that the primary batches separate beyond noise are matched, with 0 reversals.
- **Order and judges:** 0 reversals across orders or between judges, overall or on items.
- **Position:** the item position share is 0.50.

It also resolves a single removed constant-level sub-step. In all 4 verdicts it prefers the intact plan (`slight`), locating the difference at `label-residual-budget`.

So it is adopted for comparisons above the primary rubric's ceiling. It cost 114.5 M cache read for 20 judgements.

## W2b: Opus `xhigh` writes the hard key-step pages better (`calibration/plans/W2b.md`)

On a fixed 19/19 plan, two hard pages were judged with both runs pooled and the W2 pages re-judged in the same batch.

| Page | Opus `xhigh` (each judge) | Sonnet `high` | Major errors, Opus vs Sonnet |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0015, 8 items | 8, 8, 8 and 8, 7, 8 | 7, 6, 5 and 5, 5, 5 | 0 vs one run with a major error in all three judgements |
| 0016, 4 items | 4, 4, 4 | 3, 3, 3 | 0 vs 0 |

**By the preregistered table, a split writer policy goes to the author:** Opus `xhigh` for key-step pages, Sonnet `high` elsewhere.
- **The added cost:** about 2–3× Sonnet's tokens on those pages, +1.4–4.2 M cache read each.
- **Why W2 missed it:** W2's one-run reading was "within noise". It did not survive a second Sonnet run and a shared batch. The same Sonnet page drifted from 8, 6, 7 (J22) to 7, 6, 5 (J24), while Opus stayed at 8, 8, 8.
