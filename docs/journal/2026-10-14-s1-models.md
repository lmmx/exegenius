# 2026-10-14: S1, screening Opus and Haiku for planning

**What it shows, at the screening stage only.** Two runs per arm support only a decision to stop or go on spending (`calibration/PROTOCOL.md` §6). They are no claim of reliability.
- **Opus reconstructs the counting core.** At medium or high effort, `exegenius-planner` on Opus reconstructs the counting core of the paper's central saving, which Sonnet at high effort rarely does.
- **The Research gate:** 1 of 2 plans passes in each Opus arm. This is the first configuration to pass it at all. Sonnet at high passed 0 of 5.
- **Haiku at high** reaches none of the three calibres.

**The decision:** both Opus arms advance. Opus at medium is the cheaper of the two, and the author's own hypothesis ("Opus might be equally capable at medium"), so it is the arm worth estimating (stage 2) against Sonnet at high.

## Interpretation, not shown

- **The counting core seems to need model capability, not only effort.** Sonnet at high reads the paper fully (E3) yet rarely reconstructs it (1–2 in 5). Opus reconstructs all four of C3–C6 in 2 of 4 plans (O2, Q1), and C3, C5 and C6 in a third (O1), at medium as at high. Sonnet at high reconstructed all four in 0 of 5 (E3).
- **Two runs per arm cannot separate Opus at medium from Opus at high.** The medium arm used fewer tokens and less time.
