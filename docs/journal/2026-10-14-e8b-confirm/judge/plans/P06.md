The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): A single deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ worst-case time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.
  - method: Build two accelerated tape primitives (fast address-chunk interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers) from fixed linear networks with a rank saving, use them to speed up synthetic transforms over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, place these in a Gaussian-resampling reduction of the digit convolution, recover the exact product by rounding and carries, and add up the cost against an explicit slack.
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning an array of $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so savings are needed in both data movement and arithmetic.
  - relies on: n-log-n-conjecture, fixed-tape-model, big-o
