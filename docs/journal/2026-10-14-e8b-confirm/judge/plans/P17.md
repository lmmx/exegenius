# Plan P17

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): There is a deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplying two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, which disproves the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.
  - method: Build the multiplier bottom up: locate the $n\log n$ barrier, obtain two tape procedures with power savings from one rank-saving network recursion, use them to evaluate synthetic transforms and ring convolutions, turn the integer product into such convolutions by Chinese remaindering, Gaussian resampling and chirps, then assemble the algorithm, prove exact recovery and total the cost.
  - motivation (explicit): Plain transform levels over $\Theta(n)$ stored bits cost $\Theta(n\log n)$ by scanning, so a power saving needs savings in both movement and arithmetic, and each later page supplies one saving or accounts for a change of representation.
  - relies on: multitape-turing-machine, big-o-notation, subroutines-and-composition-of-algorithms, asymptotic-notation-beyond-big-o
  - **S1** (show, page `n-log-n-barrier`): Multiplication costs $O(n\log n\log\log n)$ by Sch\"onhage--Strassen and $O(n\log n)$ by Harvey--van der Hoeven, and a transform with $\Theta(\log n)$ levels that scans $\Theta(n)$ bits per level costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so beating $n\log n$ needs savings in data movement and in arithmetic.
    - method: Recall the known upper bounds and the conjecture, then identify the scan-per-level cost of a transform as what must be reduced.
    - motivation (recoverable): The conjectured optimum and the best known bound coincide at $n\log n$, and the transform structure used here has exactly that scanning cost, so any power saving has to attack the per-level scan; this is the framing the later pages rely on.
    - relies on: big-o-notation, asymptotic-notation-beyond-big-o, schoolbook-and-karatsuba-multiplication, multitape-turing-machine
