- **R** (show, page `main-theorem`): A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: Reduce the product to a convolution evaluated by Fourier-type transforms, make the transforms' address rearrangement and butterfly arithmetic cheaper than a scan per level by two tape procedures that each save a power of the logarithmic factor, assemble the exact multiplier, and add up all costs
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning an array of $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so both movement and arithmetic must be made cheaper.
  - relies on: schonhage-strassen-conjecture, hh-multiplier, multitape-turing-machine, big-o-notation, asymptotic-notation-beyond-big-o, limits-and-limit-inferior, convolution-of-sequences, subroutines-and-reductions-between-problems

    - method: Obtain the interchange for power widths with enough rows from the rank-saving recursion, then remove the width and row restrictions by padding, base-$m$ pieces and single-bit moves
    - motivation (explicit): An edge matrix of rank $a$ requires $a$ smaller interchanges, and because the ranks sum to less than $Wm$ the recurrence saves a power of the chunk width.
