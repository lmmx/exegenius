# 2026-10-13: E3, the planner at medium effort against high, judged in one batch

**The finding.** With everything but effort held fixed, `exegenius-planner` on Sonnet at **high** effort makes plans that state, derive and follow the paper far more than at **medium**. By the preregistered rule (R1), high effort is preferred for planning on this paper.

**What it does not reach.** At either effort, no plan reconstructs the paper's central saving well enough to pass the central-derivation gate (R2). The high-effort plans carry the chain's ends (frames, the endpoint identity, the recursive calls, the recurrence). They rarely carry its counting core: labels, rank as dimension change, the budget, the strict saving.

**How strong the evidence is.** It is stage-2 evidence (`calibration/PROTOCOL.md` §6): provisional, scoped to this paper and these versions. The comparison is sequential and partly re-used.

**Rubric validity (addendum A4):**
- **Agreement between judges:** each of the three paired plans agreed on 8 of 9 steps. All three are medium plans near the floor, where agreement is easy, so this says little about agreement on strong plans.
- **Spread:** within the high arm the count runs from 3 to 8, so the rubric separates plans.
- **Against the item counts:** it is not redundant with them. P1 derives 6 items and reconstructs 5 central steps, while P3 derives 10 and reconstructs 4.

## Interpretation

- **Effort is the lever for coverage and correctness of planning on this paper.** The setup check leaves effort, time and session as the only differences. The size of the effect (stated 7 against 19, derived 1 against 7) dwarfs the variation between batches seen in the anchors. The effort effect is not called "isolated": the arms ran an hour apart in different runner sessions, and the high arm was re-used.
- **Effort does not buy the central derivation.** The counting core is what neither arm plans, and it is what makes the saving work. The best wiki seen so far carried that core through writers' rewrites, not its plan (2026-10-13-chain-walk-baseline).
- **The next gain is therefore unlikely to come from more effort alone.** It may come from:
  - a higher effort level or a stronger model on the argument task, to be tested by screening;
  - asking the argument task for the counting core explicitly;
  - letting writers carry derivations the plan names but does not detail, instead of cutting them under the depth rule.

  These are hypotheses.

## Current State
- With binary, definition, model, configuration and prompts fixed, `exegenius-planner` on Sonnet at high effort states a median of 19 of 19 core results, derives 7 and follows 3 faithfully, against 7, 1 and 0 at medium (exact one-sided Mann–Whitney p = 0.004 and 0.024 for the primary measures), in batch J3
- No plan at either effort passes the central-derivation gate; at high effort the counting core (C3–C6) is reconstructed in 1–2 plans of 5
- High effort costs about 4.6–5.2 times the tokens read from cache and 3–4.4 times the seconds of medium on the extraction and argument tasks
