# Plan P01

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): One deterministic Turing machine with a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed number of one-dimensional tapes outputs the exact $2n$-bit product of any two $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ worst-case time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, so in this model the Schönhage–Strassen conjecture that $n\log n$ is the optimal order of multiplication cost fails.
  - method: Build two new tape procedures that save a power in address movement and in butterfly arithmetic; use them to compute convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ by synthetic transforms; reach that convolution from the digit convolution by exact changes of representation (Gaussian resampling with retained permutations and chirps, a Chinese-remainder map to prime axes, a phase twist); recover the product exactly by rounding and carries; and bound every cost per stored bit by $p^{1-2\kappa}$.
  - motivation (explicit): A transform-based multiplier that touches its $\Theta(n)$ stored bits once per transform level already spends $\Theta(n\log n)$, so going below $n\log n$ requires saving both on rearranging addresses and on butterfly arithmetic; the two tape procedures supply these savings, and the reductions place them inside an exact multiplication.
  - relies on: n-log-n-barrier, multitape-turing-machine, big-o-notation, convolution, tape-address-model
  - **S1** (show, page `address-chunk-interchange`): For a fixed $\tau<1$, two disjoint contiguous address fields of $u$ bits each, in an array of logical volume $V$ with arbitrary prefix, gap and suffix fields, can be interchanged in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time on a fixed number of tapes.
    - method: Prove the bound for chunks of $m^k$ radix-$q$ digits preceded by enough rows, by recursion on a fixed bit network, then reduce every width to that case by borrowing a few high digits as rows, splitting the rest into base-$m$ pieces, and padding binary ranges to powers of $q$.
    - motivation (explicit): Each edge matrix of rank $a$ costs $a$ smaller interchanges, and the ranks of the bit network sum to less than its wire count times $m$; since each smaller interchange acts on a $1/W$ share of the rows, the recursion saves a power of the chunk width instead of paying one pass per digit.
    - relies on: tape-address-model, multitape-turing-machine, binary-numerals-and-bit-strings, multidimensional-arrays-and-row-major-order
    - **S1.1** (show, page `power-width-interchange`): For every $e=m^k$, two disjoint chunks of $e$ radix-$q$ digits each, preceded by a row field whose length is divisible by $W^k$, can be interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time with $\tau=1-2^{-50}$, preserving every other address field.
      - method: Split each chunk into $m$ fields of $b=e/m$ digits ($H$ and $D$), obtain the shear $H\gets H+D$ by running the fixed bit network pointwise on $W$ row streams whose frames are address shears, implement each edge's frame change by as many recursive $b$-digit interchanges as its rational rank, convert the shear into the interchange with two linear-cost updates, and solve the volume-normalized recurrence.
      - motivation (explicit): Splitting rows among the $W$ roles makes every recursive call act on a $1/W$ share of the volume, while the network needs only $s<Wm$ calls on fields $m$ times narrower; per stored bit, multiplying the width by $m$ multiplies the recursive cost by $s/W<m$, which compounds into a power saving.
      - relies on: tape-address-model, divide-and-conquer-and-recurrences, matrices-vectors-and-linear-maps, modular-arithmetic
