**The measures** (section 1 of `table.md`), all from the accepted answer and its `log.jsonl` record:
- **Quotes:** quotes, located quotes, quotes in the first tenth, and the share past the introduction (line 431).
- **Lines:** 100-line blocks touched (of 54).
- **Sections:** developing top-level sections quoted (of 10), and body subsections quoted (of 35).
- **Results:** results quoted (of 66). These are the paper's theorem-like statements and labelled equations, as exegenius counts them.
- **Reference items reached:** core items (of 19) and supporting items (of 7) in the draft reference. An item is reached when a quote starts in its stated or derived lines, or one of its labels is a quoted result. Matching by label never adds an item that the lines miss.
- **Checklist:** checklist sub-steps whose paper lines hold a quote, and checklist markers (`markers.py`) in the extraction's own text.
- **Size:** concept counts and gloss length.
- **Cost:** cache read and seconds of the extraction subagent. These come from the registry, or for E6 from its transcripts.

## Finding 1: model and effort differences are visible without judging

| extraction seconds | 180 | 60 | 158 | 256 | 266 |

**Sonnet `medium` skims.**
- Four of five first attempts were sent back by the section rule: 1–3 of 10 developing sections, 0–2 results.
- The accepted answers reach the sections but stay shallow: 11 results, 10 core items and 2 in derivation lines. That is below Sonnet `high` beyond the spread within configurations (A ≥ 0.94, p ≤ 0.001).
- It has half the concepts (21 against 40) and half the gloss text, so the wiki would get half the pages.
- It is the cheapest: about 0.18 M cache read and one minute.

**Opus `medium` is front-loaded and shallower.**
- It puts 37 quotes in the first tenth, against 24 (A = 0.01, p < 0.001).
- Its depth is below Sonnet `high` beyond the spread: 9 results (A = 0.87, p = 0.003), 8 core items (A = 0.89, p = 0.002) and 2 in derivation lines (A = 0.82, p = 0.014).
- It is not cheaper in tokens (0.16–1.45 M), and its model is priced higher.

**Haiku `high`** quotes few results (3 and 11; A = 0.96, p = 0.026) and reaches 1–3 items in derivation lines (p = 0.032).
- K1 was accepted at 7 of 10 sections with 3 results and 4 core items. It is the shallowest accepted extraction in the set.
- Haiku's token use is not lower (0.47 and 2.06 M) and it is the slowest.

**Opus `high`** has the same depth as Sonnet `high` (A = 0.22–0.56, p ≥ 0.24).
- It has more concepts (52; p = 0.037) and more quotes in the introduction (p = 0.005).
- It is the only configuration that quotes the layer recursion's paragraph ("Unrolling the recurrence"). No Sonnet extraction of 23 does.

**Within Sonnet `high` the spread is wide:** 9–34 results and 8–18 core items.
- E1-P1 and E6-F8 are as shallow as the Opus `medium` median.
- So two runs per configuration (S1) can show a gross difference, like Sonnet `medium`'s, but cannot rank close configurations such as Opus `high` and Sonnet `high`.
- The planner definition and the general-purpose type do not differ in depth: 25 against 23 results, 16 against 15 core items.

## Finding 2: among Sonnet `high` extractions, the extraction does not move the plan's total

The data are E6's eight extractions, each planned under successive instructions: B, V (J7), W (J8), X (J16/J18) and Y (E9/E10, J18/J21). See `table.md` §4.

**No extraction effect is detectable in the plan totals.**
- **The two-way decomposition** is faithful counts over 4 arms × 8 extractions. It gives F = 0.55 for extractions (permutation p = 0.81), and on the checklist (W, X) p = 0.999.
- **The arms do not rank the extractions alike:** the faithful counts give ρ from −0.58 to +0.46. W's and X's checklist totals rank them in nearly opposite orders (ρ = −0.92).
- An extraction that helped every planner would show up as concordance. There is none.

**The variation in X is noise.** The "16 on F2, F3, F5, F6 against 17–19 on F4, F7, F8" spread is the size of run-to-run variance on one extraction:
- E8F4X against E8F4X2 differ by 1;
- S3F8OX against S5F8OX (Opus `xhigh`) differ by 3.

X's shared miss, `label-residual-budget`, is reached by all eight extractions, so it does not come from the extractions' reach.

**Under the combined instruction (Y), all six extractions tested score 19/19.** These are F2, F3, F4 (twice), F5, F6 and F8.
- They include F6 and F8, the two with the lowest reach (11 core items; 14 and 11 results).
- Of the 34 core results that those six extractions do not reach, all 34 are faithful in the Y plans.

**E1's planner extractions are the one exception, and only under the baseline instruction.**
- P4's extraction is the richest on every measure: 60 unknown concepts, 34 results, and 6 checklist markers, including L2 and L4.
- Under the baseline instruction its plans scored 14 (P) and 11 (E4 B), against 0–3 on P1, P2, P3 and P5.
- Under the key-step instruction (E4 V) it scored 13, below P3's 16.
- The decomposition over 3 arms × 5 extractions gives an extraction share of 71%, p = 0.063.

## Finding 3: item-level reach predicts item faithfulness under the weaker instructions only

The data are each core result in each plan on a fixed extraction. Strata are arm × result. The test is a Mantel-Haenszel odds ratio, with a permutation that reassigns the extractions' reach patterns (`table.md` §6).

**Across B, V, W and X:**
- A result is faithful in 0.56 of plans whose extraction reached it, against 0.41 where it did not.
- The odds ratio is 3.7, exact p = 0.0001.

**The result is robust.**
- Leaving out any one extraction gives an odds ratio of 3.2–5.1 (p ≤ 0.0012).
- Leaving out the two worst plans on low-reach extractions (E6F6V, E7F8W) gives 2.8 (p = 0.007).

**The weaker instructions carry it.**
- V and W show it (p = 0.03 and 0.04).
- X does not reach significance (p = 0.12).
- Y shows none: every result is faithful, reached or not.

So a gap in the extraction costs the plan that result when the instruction does not make the planner derive everything. The current candidate instruction makes it do so.

## Finding 4: no judge-free measure predicts the plan's total

**Across E6's eight extractions:** 133 rank correlations, between 19 measures and the arms' faithful and checklist totals and their mean rank. None reaches p < 0.05; chance alone would give about 7. The largest is markers against B: ρ = 0.75, p = 0.07 with ties.

**Across full-planning runs of different configurations:**
- Results quoted tracks faithful: ρ = 0.59, p = 0.001, over 26 runs.
- But the argument was planned by the same model as the extraction, so this cannot be credited to the extraction.

**The decisive gap:** no plan under Y has been made on an extraction from any configuration other than Sonnet `high`. The depth measures order the configurations as Sonnet `high` ≈ Opus `high` > Opus `medium` ≈ Haiku `high` ≈ Sonnet `medium`. Whether that order reaches the plan under Y is the open question.

The proposal is in `calibration/plans/X1-draft.md`.

## What these figures cannot show

**Where quotes fall is not capture.**
- A reached item is a quote somewhere in the draft reference's lines for it.
- For broad items this is weak. `label-residual-budget`'s lines span 845–1111; 17 of 18 Sonnet `high` extractions reach it, yet nearly every pre-Y plan misses it.

**Extractions do not quote derivations.**
- The checklist's sub-step lines are almost never quoted (median 0 of 26 in every configuration).
- The planner reads the derivation from the paper itself. This is why the extraction's reach matters little once the instruction asks for the derivation.

**The reference is an unreviewed draft,** and every figure rests on one paper.

**The extraction also fixes the wiki's pages, which no plan judgement measures.** There is one page per unknown concept. Sonnet `medium`'s extractions would give about 21 pages, against about 40.

**Batches differ.**
- Full-planning plans were read from the batch that judged two configurations together: J3 for P with M, J6 for G with N, J4 for S1.
- E4–E10 plans were read from their own experiment's batch. J21 (E10) was read from its verdicts, since the registry does not list it yet.
- The E1-P4 and E3-M3 anchors are stable across batches (faithful 10–14 and 0), but batch effects are not removed.
- W's and X's checklist figures pair the two plans in one batch: J15 for F1, F4 and F8, and J17 for the others.

## Current State
- `calibration/tools/extraction_stats.py` computes, from git objects and the registry alone, judge-free measures for 34 extractions in five configurations, and relates them to every judged plan made on them (primary judgements, checklist totals, per-item verdicts)
- Sonnet `medium`, Opus `medium` and Haiku `high` extractions quote fewer results and reach fewer reference items, and fewer in derivation lines, than Sonnet `high`'s, beyond the spread within configurations; Opus `high`'s are as deep, with more concepts and more introduction quotes
- Among E6's eight Sonnet `high` extractions, the extraction carries no detectable share of the plan's faithful or checklist total; X's 16-against-17–19 spread is within run variance; under Y all six extractions tested reach 19/19
- Item level: under instructions B–W, a core result is faithful more often when the extraction quoted where it is developed (Mantel-Haenszel OR 3.7, exact p = 0.0001); under Y the planner supplies every unreached result
