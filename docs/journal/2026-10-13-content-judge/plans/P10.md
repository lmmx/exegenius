# Plan P10

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **R** (show, page `main-result`): A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: Build two fast tape procedures (address interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers) with power savings, use them in synthetic transforms, and place those in the reduction of multiplication to convolution
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning the data at each of the log n transform levels already costs n log n, so savings are needed in both data movement and arithmetic.
  - relies on: nlogn-multiplier, big-o, multitape-tm, logarithms-floor-and-ceiling
  - **S1** (infer, page `tape-layout-streams`): A multitape machine with fixed alphabet and tapes can do elementary scans, permutations of digit pairs and controlled shifts on arrays in lexicographic order in time linear in the volume.
    - relies on: multitape-tm, deterministic-algorithms-and-tape-scans, multidimensional-arrays-and-tensors, mixed-radix-and-positional-digit-expansion, permutations
    - quotes line 435: "A machine has one fixed finite alphabet and one fixed finite number of"
  - **S2** (show, page `address-interchange`): Two disjoint address fields of $u$ digits in an array of volume $V$ can be exchanged in time $O(Vu^\tau)$ with $\tau<1$, the final map being an exact permutation.
    - method: Build a fixed linear exchange network with a rank saving, realize its edges by address shears, and recurse on smaller interchanges
    - motivation (explicit): A rank saving in the network makes the interchange recurrence save a power of the field width, instead of paying a full pass per level.
    - relies on: tape-layout-streams, permutations, big-o
    - **S2.1** (infer, page `linear-network-frames`): A fixed linear network exchanges two banks of values and restores auxiliary values; applied to arrays of volume $V$ with an invertible frame per gate, intermediate frames cancel and only an endpoint operator remains.
      - relies on: linear-algebra-over-a-field, permutations, tape-layout-streams
      - quotes line 643: "We construct two fixed linear networks that exchange two banks of values"
    - **S2.2** (infer, page `subset-intersection-network`): The three-element-subset construction gives a bit network with $W$ wires whose edge differences have total rank $s<Wm$.
      - relies on: basic-finite-set-combinatorics, linear-algebra-over-a-field, linear-network-frames
      - quotes line 1139: "The bit network has $W_{\rm b}$ wires, uses only pointwise XOR gates,"
    - **S2.3** (infer, page `recursive-power-saving`): A call at parameter $mf$ uses $s<Wm$ calls at parameter $f$ on streams of volume $V/W$, so the cost per volume grows like a power $m^\tau$ with $\tau<1$.
      - relies on: geometric-series-and-recurrences, big-o, logarithms-floor-and-ceiling, subset-intersection-network, linear-network-frames
      - quotes line 1326: "For both recurrences we may use the fixed rational exponent"
    - **S2.4** (infer, page `address-shear`): The address shear $(H,D)\mapsto(H+D,D)$ with a rational matrix is done in linear time per fixed matrix via lower-triangular (Bruhat-type) factorization in tape order.
      - relies on: linear-algebra-over-a-field, modular-arithmetic, primes-gcd-and-coprimality, tape-layout-streams, linear-network-frames
      - quotes line 1394: "\begin{lemma}[The cost of a rational matrix shear]"
    - **S2.5** (infer): Two disjoint address fields of $u$ digits can be interchanged for arbitrary width in time $O(Vu^\tau)$.
      - quotes line 1631: "\begin{lemma}[Arbitrary-width interchange]"
  - **S3** (show, page `simultaneous-butterfly-layer`): Applying $H_0$ on the same bit position of up to $d$ address chunks of polynomial records costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with $\lambda'<1$, with all intermediate values exact Gaussian dyadics and a single truncation.
    - method: Use the complex-valued network with phase frames, implement frame changes by packed selected-bit additions and chunk moves, and recurse
    - motivation (explicit): Doing the $d$ butterflies one axis at a time takes $d$ whole-array passes; the network recursion yields a power saving over that.
    - relies on: fixed-point-dyadic, butterfly, complex-numbers, linear-network-frames, recursive-power-saving, address-interchange, elementary-gaussian-integers-and-dyadic-rationals
    - **S3.1** (infer, page `packed-selected-bit-addition`): Selected bits (at positions $\rho+iK$) of grouped address chunks can be added (XORed) in one packed pass.
      - relies on: binary-representation-and-bit-operations, address-interchange, tape-layout-streams
      - quotes line 1974: "\begin{lemma}[Packed selected-bit addition on one rectangle]"
    - **S3.2** (infer): The butterfly $H_0$ applied simultaneously on the same bit of $d$ address chunks of polynomial records costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with $\lambda'<1$, exact until one truncation.
      - quotes line 2588: "\begin{proposition}[Simultaneous normalized butterfly layer]"
  - **S4** (show, page `synthetic-transform`): Convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ can be computed by synthetic transforms that use the simultaneous butterfly layers.
    - method: Do Fourier transforms over the ring with powers of $y$ as roots of unity, so roots act as signed shifts and the remaining work is butterflies, applied on a suitable layout
    - motivation (explicit): Powers of y are roots of unity acting by exact signed permutations of coefficient lists, so only butterfly layers cost arithmetic.
    - relies on: dft, negacyclic-ring, butterfly, address-interchange, convolution
    - **S4.1** (infer): Synthetic Fourier transforms over the ring modulo $y^r+1$ are laid out on tape so that the parallel layers apply, with a stored frequency order retained for the inverse.
      - quotes line 2929: "\begin{lemma}[Transform layout and cost]"
    - **S4.2** (infer, page `signed-packing`): Products of polynomials over the ring are obtained exactly from one integer product by signed packing, using the known $O(n\log n)$ multiplier as a subroutine.
      - relies on: negacyclic-ring, nlogn-multiplier, polynomials, binary-representation-and-bit-operations, norms-error-bounds-and-rounding
      - quotes line 3221: "\begin{lemma}[Signed packing and normalized ring multiplication]"
    - **S4.3** (infer): Convolution over the ring is computed from two forward synthetic transforms, a pointwise product and the opposite transform, with bounded error.
      - quotes line 3391: "\begin{proposition}[Convolution from the retained transform layout]"
  - **S5** (show, page `gaussian-resampling`): Fourier transforms of pairwise coprime lengths reduce to power-of-two tensor transforms with the permutations retained in the transform formula.
    - method: Gaussian resampling with the source and target permutations kept inside the formula
    - motivation (explicit): Resampling usually sorts twice per coordinate, which costs full passes; keeping the permutations in the formula avoids executing them.
    - relies on: dft, permutations, norms-error-bounds-and-rounding, roots-of-unity-and-euler-s-formula, synthetic-transform
    - **S5.1** (infer): Gaussian resampling expresses a Fourier transform of a prime-power-free length $t$ through a power-of-two transform without executing the two coordinate permutations.
      - quotes line 3548: "\begin{lemma}[Resampling without executing the two permutations]"
    - **S5.2** (infer, page `permutation-cancellation`): In the chirp identity the source permutation cancels across the two forward transforms, the pointwise product and the opposite transform.
      - relies on: gaussian-resampling, bluestein-chirp, permutations, dft, convolution
      - quotes line 3911: "\begin{lemma}[Chirp and permutation cancellation]"
  - **S6** (show): The product of two $n$-bit integers is reduced step by step to convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, recovered exactly, and its total cost is bounded.
    - method: Chain reductions (digits, prime axes, power-of-two axes, polynomial ring), then recover the product and account for every cost term
    - motivation (recoverable): Each reduction changes the problem to a shape the fast tape procedures serve; the chain ends in a ring convolution whose transforms are the cheap synthetic ones.
    - **S6.1** (infer, page `radix-digit-reduction`, reported out of order): Splitting each operand into radix-$2^b$ digits reduces the integer product to a convolution of digit lists, followed by carry propagation.
