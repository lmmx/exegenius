The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **ROOT** (show, page `main-theorem`): There is one deterministic fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape Turing machine multiplying $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: Reduce the product to a digit convolution computed with Fourier-type transforms on power-of-two axes, and accelerate the transforms' address rearrangements and butterfly layers by a power of the logarithm.
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning the array at each of the logarithmically many transform levels already costs $n\log n$, so a power saving needs the movement and the arithmetic per level to be cheaper than a scan.
