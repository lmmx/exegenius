The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, refuting the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.
  - method: Build the two accelerated tape procedures (address interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers), use them inside synthetic transforms and Gaussian resampling to compute a convolution, assemble the multiplication algorithm with exact recovery, and total the cost.
  - motivation (explicit): A transform-based product scans $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ levels and so costs $n\log n$; a power saving needs cheaper movement and cheaper arithmetic, which the two procedures supply, and everything else is built around them.
  - relies on: nlogn-history, fixed-tape-model, established-multiplier, big-o, turing-machine, asymptotic-notation-beyond-big-o, logarithms-exponentials-floor-and-ceiling
  - **S1** (show): Two tape procedures cost less than a pass per level: interchanging two disjoint $u$-bit address fields of a volume-$V$ array costs $O(Vu^\tau)$ and a simultaneous butterfly layer on $D\le d$ chunks costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, for fixed $\tau,\lambda'<1$.
