Fast address-chunk interchange swaps two disjoint equal-width chunks of an array, and the main algorithm uses it in its transforms.

Let $u$ be the number of bits in each chunk, and let $V$ be the number of bits stored in the array. Here $\tau$ is a fixed exponent with $0<\tau<1$. The procedure takes $O(Vu^\tau)$ time <cite data-source="paper">for a fixed $\tau<1$</cite>.

Let $e$ be the number of digits in each chunk, written in a fixed prime radix $q$.

The general case is reduced to the [Power-width address interchange](power-width-interchange.md) in three stages. That procedure swaps chunks of $e=m^k$ digits, where $k$ is the least integer with $m^k\ge e$, when a row field before them has length divisible by $W^k$.

Let $\rho$ be the least integer with $q^{2\rho}\ge W^k$, the number of top digits taken from each chunk. Call the first chunk $h$, the second $d$, and the field between them $g$. Write $h^+$ and $h^-$ for the top $\rho$ digits of $h$ and its remaining digits, and $d^+$ and $d^-$ for those of $d$. Interchange the top digits of $h$ and $d$ one pair at a time; then $d^+$ and $h^+$ are on top, and moving $h^+$ after $d^+$ gives a row field of $q^{2\rho}\ge W^k$ rows before both remaining parts. Appending zero rows makes its length a multiple of $W^k$, less than $2q^{2\rho}$. Each digit pair costs $O(V)$ by [Elementary stream operations](stream-ops.md), and the orders are $(h^+,h^-,g,d^+,d^-)\to(d^+,h^+,h^-,g,d^-)\to(d^+,h^+,d^-,g,h^-)\to(d^+,d^-,g,h^+,h^-)$. Write $e-\rho=\sum_{j=0}^{k} a_j m^j$ with $0\le a_j<m$, where $a_j$ counts the pieces of width $m^j$ and $j$ is the base-$m$ position. Split each remaining part into $a_j$ pieces of width $m^j$ for each $j$, and interchange corresponding pieces by the power-width procedure, all using one padded row field whose length is divisible by every $W^j$ with $j\le k$. The costs sum to $O(Ve^\tau)$ because $a_j<m$ and $m^k<me$. For binary chunks take $e$ to be the least integer with $q^e\ge 2^u$, pad each chunk range numerically from $\{0,\dots,2^u-1\}$ to $\{0,\dots,q^e-1\}$ with zeros, interchange, and scan the padding away. Radix-$q$ order is numerical, so padding keeps valid entries in order, and $e=O(u)$ bounds the cost.

Together these stages interchange any two disjoint chunks of $u$ bits, with no row restriction, in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time. Intermediate steps XOR-combine data, yet the [bit network](bit-network-interface.md) sends every input role to its exchanged role, so the result is exact.
