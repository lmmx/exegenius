Fast address-chunk interchange swaps two disjoint chunks in an array, a tape procedure of the main multiplication algorithm. $u$ counts the bits in a chunk, $V$ the bits in the array, and $\tau$ is a fixed exponent with $0<\tau<1$. Its time is $O(Vu^\tau)$ <cite data-source="paper">Its cost is $O(Vu^\tau)$ for a fixed $\tau<1$</cite>.

Let $q\ge 2$ be the fixed radix and $e$ the number of radix-$q$ digits in a chunk, the least integer with $q^e\ge 2^u$. Let $k=\lfloor\log_m e\rfloor$ be the largest integer with $m^k\le e$.

The [Power-width address interchange](power-width-interchange.md) result says that, for each $j\ge 0$, two disjoint chunks of $m^j$ radix-$q$ digits can be interchanged in $O(Vm^{j\tau})$ time when a row field, whose values are rows, of length divisible by $W^j$ precedes both. The chunks' own digits supply the missing row field. The fast address-chunk interchange is assembled from [Power-width address interchange](power-width-interchange.md) calls on pieces of width $m^j$.

Let $\rho=O(\log e)$ be the number of top digits moved first, with $q^{2\rho}\ge W^k$. Write $h=h^+h^-$ and $d=d^+d^-$, where $h^+$ and $d^+$ are the top $\rho$ digits, and $h^-$, $d^-$ the rest. Interchange the high digits pairwise, then move $h^+$ after $d^+$. These two fields form a row field of $R=q^{2\rho}$ rows before the remaining chunks. Digit moves cost $O(V)$ by [Elementary stream operations](stream-ops.md), so this costs $O(V\rho)$. Pad with zero rows to $R'=W^k\lceil R/W^k\rceil$ rows. Write $e-\rho=\sum_{j=0}^{k} a_j m^j$ with $0\le a_j<m$, and cut $h^-$ and $d^-$ into $a_j$ pieces of width $m^j$. Interchange corresponding pieces by the power-width result. Calls share the $R'$-row field, divisible by $W^j$ for $j\le k$, and keep entries in their rows, so padding affects nothing. The cost is $O(V)\sum_j a_j m^{j\tau}$, which is $O(Ve^\tau)$, as the top term $m^{k\tau}\le e^\tau$ dominates the geometric sum. Scan away the padding and reverse the joining moves, costing $O(V\rho)$. Finally, pad each binary range numerically from $[2^u]$ to $[q^e]$ with zeros, which enlarges the volume by a fixed factor. Radix-$q$ order is numerical, so padding keeps valid entries in order, and scanning it away recovers the binary result. As $e=O(u)$, the total is $O(Vu^\tau)$.

The exponent $\tau<1$ is the power-width one, dominating the other costs. The result is an exact permutation despite XOR-combined intermediate steps <cite data-source="paper">Although its final effect is an exact permutation</cite>. Moves and padding are exact, and each power-width call keeps entries in rows and cleans its work tapes, so its XOR combinations vanish.
