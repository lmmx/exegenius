The disk grid is the number format for the algorithm's complex coefficients: values lie on a binary grid in the unit disk, each step is computed exactly, then one truncation returns to the grid, so each step adds a known error and the stored width never grows.

**Stored values.** Let $p$ be the number of fractional bits kept. A coefficient is stored as $2^{-p}(a+ib)$, where the integer numerators $a$ and $b$ are each a signed two's-complement word of $w$ bits. Records are elements of [$\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$](polynomial-ring-mod.md), lists of $r$ such coefficients. For an array $z$ of records, $\|z\|_\infty$ is its largest coefficient modulus. A *disk array* has $\|z\|_\infty\le1$; a *$p$-bit grid array* has all real and imaginary parts in $2^{-p}\mathbb Z$. On the disk grid $|a|,|b|\le2^p$, so $w\ge p+2$ bits suffice <cite data-source="paper">The condition $w\ge p+2$ allows every permitted disk-grid component, including $-1$ and $1$.</cite>

**Exact intermediates.** A *Gaussian dyadic* is a complex number whose real and imaginary parts have power-of-two denominators. Sums, differences and halvings stay Gaussian dyadic, so a butterfly (half-sum and half-difference of two values) is exact in working words with *guard bits*: extra fractional bits for denominators, extra integer bits for growth. Let $s$ be the number of halvings since the last truncation. Values then lie on the $2^{-(p+s)}$ grid and temporary sums have modulus at most two <cite data-source="paper">A temporary sum has modulus at most two and needs only a constant number of extra integer bits.</cite>, so $p+s+O(1)$ bits per part suffice.

**One truncation.** Each step ends with $Q_p$, which truncates every real and imaginary part toward zero onto $2^{-p}\mathbb Z$: shift the absolute numerator right past the guard bits and restore the sign. Each part moves by less than $2^{-p}$ and its absolute value does not grow <cite data-source="paper">truncation cannot increase the absolute value of either component, and changes each by less than $2^{-p}$</cite>, so for every array $z$

$$\|Q_pz-z\|_\infty<\sqrt2\,2^{-p},\qquad \|Q_pz\|_\infty\le\|z\|_\infty.$$

Rounding to nearest could leave the disk: at $p=2$ it sends $0.92+0.38i$ to $1+\tfrac12i$.

The transform steps are contractions: they keep disk arrays in the disk and do not enlarge existing errors. With $Q_p$ after each, arrays stay disk-grid arrays in $w$-bit words, and each step adds less than $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ to the error <cite data-source="paper">A contraction does not increase the norm of the difference between the computed and exact arrays; the single truncation then adds less than $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$</cite>.

**Example.** Take $p=2$, $w=4$ and the pair $\tfrac34+\tfrac14i$, $\tfrac12-\tfrac12i$, stored as words $0011,0001$ and $0010,1110$. The butterfly gives exactly $\tfrac58-\tfrac18i$ and $\tfrac18+\tfrac38i$, on the $2^{-3}$ grid. Truncation gives $\tfrac12$ and $\tfrac14i$ (note $-\tfrac18\mapsto0$, not $-\tfrac14$). Both errors have modulus $\sqrt2/8<\sqrt2/4$; moduli fall from about $0.64,\,0.40$ to $0.5,\,0.25$.
