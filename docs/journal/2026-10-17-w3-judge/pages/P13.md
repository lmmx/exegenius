The ring $\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ holds polynomials in a formal variable $y$ with $r$ complex coefficients, multiplied under the rule $y^r=-1$; the algorithm stores one coordinate of its arrays in this ring so that powers of $y$ act as roots of unity applied exactly, by moving coefficients <cite data-source="paper">stored as the coefficient list of a polynomial modulo $y^r+1$, with $r$ a power of two.</cite>

The record length $r$ is a power of two with $r\ge2$. Write an element $f\in\mathcal R_r$ by its unique representative of degree less than $r$, $f=\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}z_ky^k$, where $z_k$ is the complex coefficient of $y^k$.

Sums are coefficientwise; a product is the ordinary polynomial product with each $y^{r+k}$ replaced by $-y^k$. This negative wrap is the only difference from [Cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md), where $y^r=1$ adds the wrapped coefficient instead.

## Powers of $y$

Multiplying by $y$ shifts every coefficient up one place, and the top one wraps to position $0$ with its sign flipped:
$$yf=-z_{r-1}+z_0y+z_1y^2+\dots+z_{r-2}y^{r-1}.$$
Repeating this, and using $y^{-1}=-y^{r-1}$, multiplication by $y^a$, where $a$ is any integer exponent, permutes the coefficients cyclically and changes some signs <cite data-source="paper">Multiplication by $y^a$, for any integer $a$, permutes the coefficients and changes</cite>. For example, with $r=4$,
$$y^3(1+2y+3y^2+4y^3)=y^3+2y^4+3y^5+4y^6=-2-3y-4y^2+y^3,$$
so the list $(1,2,3,4)$ becomes $(-2,-3,-4,1)$.

From $y^r=-1$ we get $y^{2r}=1$, while $y^a\ne1$ for $0<a<2r$, because $y^a$ is then plus or minus a monomial other than $1$. So $y$ is a root of unity of order $2r$, and $y^{2r/t}$ is a primitive $t$-th root of unity, where $t$, the length of a transform along one axis, is a power of two dividing $2r$.

These roots are exact: a complex root such as $e^{2\pi i/t}$ must be rounded, but multiplying by $y^a$ only moves and negates stored numbers, so it adds no rounding error and on tapes needs one split and merge of the list, with no sorting <cite data-source="paper">and no coefficient sort is required</cite>.

Measure an element by its largest coefficient modulus, $\|f\|_\infty=\max_{0\le k<r}|z_k|$. Multiplication by $y^a$ only rearranges and negates coefficients, so it preserves this norm <cite data-source="paper">consequently it is an isometry for this norm.</cite> Transforms whose outputs are averages of such images therefore never increase the norm <cite data-source="paper">Thus both transforms are contractions.</cite>

The ring has zero divisors (nonzero elements with product $0$), so identities about these roots are checked directly, not by division <cite data-source="paper">The ring has zero divisors, so we verify character cancellation</cite>.

The [Coefficient (phase) twist](phase-twist.md) moves a cyclic coordinate into $\mathcal R_r$, the [Synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md) uses $y^{2r/t}$ as its root of unity, and [Signed packing of polynomial products](signed-packing.md) multiplies two elements exactly by integer multiplication.
