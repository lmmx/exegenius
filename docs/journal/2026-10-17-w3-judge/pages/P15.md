Gaussian resampling rewrites the [Discrete Fourier transform](dft.md) $F_s$ of prime length $s$ through the transform $F_t$ of power-of-two length $t>s$ and two cheap Gaussian-weighted maps, so coprime lengths can use fast butterflies. Harvey and van der Hoeven's version applies two index permutations by sorting, a factor $\log t$ <cite data-source="paper">Its usual implementation sorts twice on each coordinate.</cite> Here both stay in the identity.

Let $p>100$ be the precision in bits, $s<t<2^p$ coprime lengths, and $\alpha$ an integer Gaussian width with $2\le\alpha<\sqrt p$ and

$$\theta:=t/s-1>p/\alpha^4.$$

The permutations multiply indices by $t$ modulo $s$ and by $-s$ modulo $t$

$$(P_su)_j=u_{tj},\qquad (P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}.$$

## One axis

Two Gaussian maps $\mathbb C^s\to\mathbb C^t$ are

$$(\mathsf Su)_k=\frac1\alpha\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi\alpha^{-2}(j-sk/t)^2}u_{j\bmod s},\qquad (\mathsf Wu)_k=\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}e^{-\pi\alpha^2(tj/s-k)^2}u_{j\bmod s}.$$

$\mathsf S$ averages inputs near $sk/t$ over width $\alpha$ while the narrow $\mathsf W$ sends $u_j$ mainly to the $k$ nearest $tj/s$. As Gaussians transform to Gaussians, Harvey and van der Hoeven prove the identity <cite data-source="paper">The exact Gaussian identity and inverse estimate proved in</cite>

$$\mathsf WP_sF_s=P_tF_t\mathsf S.$$

Let $q_j=\lfloor tj/s+1/2\rfloor$ be the integer nearest $tj/s$, let $\mathsf G:\mathbb C^t\to\mathbb C^s$ pick those coordinates by $(\mathsf Gv)_j=v_{q_j}$, and let the diagonal $\mathsf D$, with $(\mathsf Du)_j=e^{\pi\alpha^2(tj/s-q_j)^2}u_j$, cancel the Gaussian factor at $q_j$. Then $N=\mathsf G\mathsf W\mathsf D$ is the identity plus Gaussian tails, with max-norm operator norm $\|N-I\|<2.01e^{-\pi\alpha^2\theta/2}<2^{-\alpha^2\theta}$ <cite data-source="paper">The exact Gaussian identity and inverse estimate proved in</cite>.

Since $\alpha^2<p<\alpha^4\theta$ gives $\alpha^2\theta>1$ and hence $\|N-I\|<1/2$ the Neumann series $N^{-1}=\sum_{m\ge0}(I-N)^m$ converges. As $\mathsf G\mathsf W=N\mathsf D^{-1}$ the map $\mathsf DN^{-1}\mathsf G$ is a left inverse of $\mathsf W$, and applying it to the identity gives $P_sF_s=\mathsf DN^{-1}\mathsf GP_tF_t\mathsf S.$ Splitting $2^{-2\alpha^2}=2^{-1}\cdot2^{-1}\cdot2^{2-2\alpha^2}$ put

$$A=\tfrac12\mathsf S,\qquad B_0=\bigl(2^{2-2\alpha^2}\mathsf D\bigr)\bigl(\tfrac12N^{-1}\bigr)\mathsf G,\qquad\text{so}\qquad P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA.$$

The factors have norms below $3/4,\ 7/8,\ 1$ <cite data-source="paper">The proof of Proposition~4.7(i) in the cited paper gives</cite>, so $A$ and $B_0$ are contractions, never increasing the max norm. The resampling approximates the factors in [Fixed-point disk-grid arithmetic](fixed-point-truncation.md) with errors $16p,\ 3p^2/4,\ 4$ grid steps $2^{-p}$ in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ for any fixed $0<\delta<1/8$ <cite data-source="paper">Lemmas~4.8, 4.9 and 4.12 of</cite>. As $q_0<q_1<\dots<q_{s-1}<t$ one forward scan applies $\mathsf G$ exactly in time $O(tp)$. Errors add along contractions, so the computed $\widetilde A$ errs by $16p<p^2$ and $\widetilde B_0$ by $4+3p^2/4<p^2,$ all in increasing coordinate order.

## Many axes

Let $d$ count the axes, with lengths $s_i<t_i$ on axis $i$ meeting the hypotheses above. Tensor products multiply factorwise, so tensoring over the axes of a [Multidimensional (tensor) transform](multidimensional-dft.md) combines the one-axis identities into

$$RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^{\gamma}\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A,\qquad \gamma=2d\alpha^2,$$

where $R=\bigotimes_iP_{s_i},\ Q=\bigotimes_iP_{t_i},\ \mathcal A=\bigotimes_iA_i,\ \mathcal B_0=\bigotimes_iB_{0,i},\ F_{\boldsymbol s}=\bigotimes_iF_{s_i},\ F_{\boldsymbol t}=\bigotimes_iF_{t_i}$.

The array sits in a zero-padded box of $T=\prod_it_i$ entries. For $\mathcal A$, take each axis $i$ in turn: make it the innermost address field, run $\widetilde A_i$ on lines whose other coordinates are in range, and write zeros elsewhere; $\mathcal B_0$ compresses likewise. Each axis map is a contraction erring below $p^2$, so $d$ axes err below $dp^2$. Axis $i$ has at most $T/t_i$ lines, costing

$$\sum_{i=1}^d\frac T{t_i}\,O(t_ip^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)=O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha).$$

With $t_i\in\{r/2,r\}$ and $r=2^\ell$, moving an axis past a neighbour is a [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-chunk-interchange.md) of $\ell$- or $(\ell-1)$-bit fields, costing $O(Tp(1+\ell^\tau))$ for its fixed exponent $\tau<1$; $O(d)$ moves per axis give $O(Tpd^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
