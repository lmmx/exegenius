Multiplying two integers of $n$ bits each (so $n$ is the number of bits of each input) reduces to a convolution of short digit lists. An integer product is the product of two polynomials evaluated in the base $2^b$, where $b$ is the number of bits in one digit; with enough zero padding, a cyclic convolution of the digit lists computes that polynomial product without wraparound.

The algorithm takes $b=\lceil \log_2 n\rceil$, so $n\le 2^b$.

## Digits as a polynomial

Write each input in base $2^b$ with $q=\lceil n/b\rceil$ digits, where $q$ is the number of digits of one input. For the first input $u$ with digits $u_0,\dots,u_{q-1}$, each between $0$ and $2^b-1$,

$$u=\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}u_k\,2^{bk}=U(2^b),\qquad U(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}u_kx^k,$$

and likewise $v=V(2^b)$. Hence $uv=(UV)(2^b)$. The product polynomial $UV$ has coefficients $c_j=\sum_k u_kv_{j-k}$, where $c_j$ is the coefficient of $x^j$. Its integer values are not yet digits, so carries are propagated afterwards, as on [Rounding and carry propagation](exact-recovery.md).

## No wraparound

Let $S$ be the cyclic length, the number of positions in the zero-padded digit arrays, and $T$ the padded transform size, a power of two. The parameter choices make $2n/b\le T/2<S$, with $S$ a product of distinct primes just below the axis lengths ([Parameter choices and prime source lengths](parameter-choice.md)).

$U$ and $V$ have degree at most $q-1$, so $UV$ has degree at most $2q-2$. Since $q<n/b+1$,

$$2q-2<2n/b\le T/2<S.$$

The product polynomial therefore has all its terms in degrees $0,\dots,S-1$. Reduction modulo $x^S-1$ replaces $x^{S+m}$ by $x^m$, so it only moves terms of degree $S$ or more, and there are none. Cyclic and ordinary convolution then agree, and zero padding (digits $u_k=v_k=0$ for $q\le k<S$) lets the cyclic convolution compute the ordinary one. The integer product is computed by a [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md) of the two zero-padded digit lists, because no index wraps around.

## Size of the coefficients

The coefficient $c_j$ is a sum of at most $q$ products $u_kv_{j-k}$, and each factor is at most $2^b-1$. So

$$0\le c_j\le q(2^b-1)^2<2^{3b},$$

where the last step uses $q\le n\le 2^b$: then $q(2^b-1)^2<2^b\cdot 2^{2b}$. The coefficients are therefore integers of fewer than $3b$ bits <cite data-source="paper">0\le c_j\le q(2^b-1)^2<2^{3b},</cite>. This bound is what lets the later error analysis recover each $c_j$ exactly by rounding.
