Gaussian resampling expresses the [Discrete Fourier transform](dft.md) of a prime length $s$ through a power-of-two transform of a longer length $t$. The usual implementation sorts twice on each coordinate <cite data-source="paper">Its usual implementation sorts twice on each</cite>; here both coordinate permutations stay in the formula and no sort is executed.

## One dimension

Let $p$ be the number of bits of precision, $s<t<2^p$ coprime lengths, $\alpha<\sqrt p$ an integer width, $\theta=t/s-1$ the relative excess of $t$ (assumed $>p/\alpha^4$), and $0<\delta<1/8$ a fixed exponent. The permutations are $(P_su)_j=u_{tj}$ and $(P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}$. We want contractions (linear maps of norm at most 1) $A:\mathbb C^s\to\mathbb C^t$, $B_0:\mathbb C^t\to\mathbb C^s$ with
$$P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA.$$

Two Gaussian-weighted maps are
$$(\mathsf Su)_k=\tfrac1\alpha\sum_j e^{-\pi\alpha^{-2}(j-sk/t)^2}u_j,\qquad (\mathsf Gu)_k=\sum_j e^{-\pi\alpha^2(tj/s-k)^2}u_j,$$
and satisfy the exact identity $\mathsf GP_sF_s=P_tF_t\mathsf S$ (Harvey and van der Hoeven). Let $q_j=\lfloor tj/s+1/2\rfloor$ be the integer nearest $tj/s$, $\beta_j=tj/s-q_j$, let $C$ select coordinates $q_j$, and let $\mathsf D$ be diagonal with entries $e^{\pi\alpha^2\beta_j^2}$. In $N=C\mathsf G\mathsf D$, the summand of $(Nu)_j$ at index $j$ is exactly $u_j$ ($\mathsf D$ cancels the Gaussian) and the others are tiny: $\|N-I\|<2.01e^{-\pi\alpha^2\theta/2}<2^{-\alpha^2\theta}$ <cite data-source="paper">\|N-I\|<2.01e^{-\pi\alpha^2\theta/2}<2^{-\alpha^2\theta}.</cite> Since $\alpha^2\theta>p/\alpha^2>1$, $\|N-I\|<1/2$, so $N^{-1}=\sum_k(I-N)^k$ (Neumann series). As $C\mathsf G=N\mathsf D^{-1}$, $(\mathsf DN^{-1}C)\mathsf G=I$, and applying this left inverse gives $\mathsf DN^{-1}CP_tF_t\mathsf S=P_sF_s$.

Put $\mathsf S'=\mathsf S/2$, $\mathsf J'=N^{-1}/2$, $\mathsf D'=2^{-(2\alpha^2-2)}\mathsf D$, with norms below $3/4$, $7/8$, $1$. Set $A=\mathsf S'$ and $B_0=\mathsf D'\mathsf J'C$; both are contractions since $\|C\|=1$. The normalizations multiply to $2^{-(2\alpha^2-2)}2^{-1}2^{-1}=2^{-2\alpha^2}$, so $B_0P_tF_tA=2^{-2\alpha^2}P_sF_s$.

Its numerical maps are computed in [Fixed-point disk-grid arithmetic](fixed-point-truncation.md): $\widetilde{\mathsf S}'$ has error $16p$ and time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ <cite data-source="paper">\widetilde{\mathsf S}'&16p&O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)\\</cite>, $\widetilde{\mathsf J}'$ error $3p^2/4$ and the same time, $\widetilde{\mathsf D}'$ error $4$ and time $O(tp^{1+\delta})$. Since $q_0=0$, $q_{s-1}<t$ and $q_{j+1}>q_j$, $C$ is an ordered subsequence, generated by counters with $tj=sf_j+e_j$ at $O(p)$ per index: cost $O(tp)$, no error. So $\widetilde A=\widetilde{\mathsf S}'$ and $\widetilde B_0=\widetilde{\mathsf D}'\widetilde{\mathsf J}'C$ have errors $16p<p^2$ and $4+3p^2/4<p^2$, in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$.

## Several axes

Take $d$ axes, with pairs $(s_i,t_i)$ each satisfying the hypotheses, and $T=\prod_it_i$, the number of entries of the zero-padded box. Tensoring the one-dimensional identity over the axes gives a statement about the [Multidimensional (tensor) transform](multidimensional-dft.md): with $R=\bigotimes_iP_{s_i}$, $Q=\bigotimes_iP_{t_i}$, $\mathcal A=\bigotimes_iA_i$, $\mathcal B_0=\bigotimes_iB_{0,i}$,
$$RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A,\qquad \gamma=2d\alpha^2,$$
since tensor products multiply factorwise.

Axis by axis, each valid line is expanded from $s_i$ to $t_i$ entries, mapped, and compressed back. Each axis map is a contraction with error below $p^2$, so $d$ axes give error below $dp^2$ <cite data-source="paper">Composing the $d$ axis maps gives error less than $dp^2$.</cite> At most $T/t_i$ lines invoke it on axis $i$, costing $\sum_i\frac T{t_i}O(t_ip^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)=O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$.

Exposing an axis interchanges adjacent address fields of $\ell$ or $\ell-1$ bits, where $\ell$ is an address field's bit width, each costing $O(Tp(1+\ell^\tau))$ for a fixed $\tau<1$. An axis needs $O(d)$ interchanges, so all need $O(d^2)$ <cite data-source="paper">$O(d)$ adjacent interchanges, hence all axes need $O(d^2)$.</cite>, in total $O(Tpd^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
