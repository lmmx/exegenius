The multiplication algorithm is the pipeline from two $n$-bit integers to their exact product, a chain of reductions each giving the shape the next tool handles.

It takes its parameters from [Parameter choices and prime source lengths](parameter-choice.md): $b$ is the digit width in bits, $p=6b$ the precision in bits, $d$ the number of axes, $T$ the padded length, and $S=\prod_i s_i$ the product of distinct odd primes $s_i$, with $T/2<S<T$.

The product follows from [Integer product as digit convolution](radix-digit-convolution.md): a cyclic convolution modulo $x^S-1$ of digits. It maps its addresses by the [Chinese-remainder address map](crt-address-map.md) $\Phi$, giving a convolution on $d$ prime axes. Prime lengths defeat power-of-two transforms, so it computes its prime-length transforms by [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md), through lengths $t_i$ that are powers of two, and cancels its permutations by [Retained permutations and their cancellation](permutation-cancellation.md): they stay in the formulas, unsorted. It writes transforms as chirp convolutions by the [Bluestein chirp identity](bluestein-chirp.md). It twists the last axis by the [Coefficient (phase) twist](phase-twist.md) into $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, and convolves over that ring by [Convolution over the polynomial ring](ring-convolution.md), where roots of unity are signed shifts.

Chirp phases $-\sum_i s_ij_i^2(r/t_i)\bmod 2r$ are computed with the [established multiplier](established-multiplier.md). The chirp transform is $x_0=Q_p(\overline{\widetilde a}\cdot x)$, $y_0=\widetilde{\mathcal M}_{\mathbb C}(\widetilde a,x_0)$, $\widetilde{\mathscr C}(x)=Q_p(\overline{\widetilde a}\cdot y_0)$, with $\widetilde a$ the computed kernel and $Q_p$ truncation to $p$ fractional bits; exact phases and convolution give $QF_{\boldsymbol t}x$. The source transform $\widetilde{\mathscr F}$ applies resampling $\mathcal A$, $\widetilde{\mathscr C}$, resampling $\mathcal B_0$, then scale $2^\gamma$; its exact counterpart is $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$ <cite data-source="paper">\mathscr F=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A</cite>. The opposite-sign version conjugates input and output: $\widetilde{\mathscr F}^+(z)=\overline{\widetilde{\mathscr F}(\overline z)}$.

It stores digits $a_j2^{-b-2}$ exactly and applies $\Phi$ to get $u,v$ of norm below $1/4$. It computes $\widetilde{\mathscr F}(u)$ and $\widetilde{\mathscr F}(v)$, multiplies pointwise to $z$, computes $\widetilde{\mathscr F}^+(z)$ scaled by $S$, scales by $2^{2b+4}S$, rounds, applies $\Phi^{-1}$, and propagates carries by [Rounding and carry propagation](exact-recovery.md). The two factors $S$ undo different normalizations <cite data-source="paper">The two occurrences of $S$ in this algorithm undo different</cite>: the three transforms leave $(u*v)/S^2$, the first scale gives $(u*v)/S$, the second $2^{2b+4}(u*v)$.

Errors are in units of $2^{-p}$, with $L=\log_2T$; inserting the exact contraction at each computed input adds errors. The ring convolution has error $<5TL$ <cite data-source="paper">E_{\rm ring}:=M(3L+2)<5TL.</cite>. Twist errors $4+4+4$ give $<6TL$ for the complex convolution <cite data-source="paper"><(E_{\rm ring}+4+4+4)h_p<6TLh_p.</cite>. Kernel error $2$ and phases $4+4$ give $<8TL$ for the chirp transform <cite data-source="paper"><(6TL+2+4+4)h_p<8TLh_p.</cite>. Resampling errors $dp^2$ and scale $2^\gamma$ give $E_s=9\cdot2^\gamma TL$ <cite data-source="paper">E_s=9\,2^\gamma TL,</cite>. Norms stay inside the unit disk.
