With $y$ the formal polynomial variable and $r$ the number of complex coefficients in a record (a power of two), the ring $\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ is where the algorithm keeps one coordinate of its arrays, because there powers of $y$ behave as exact roots of unity and multiplying by them only rearranges coefficients.

Elements are complex polynomials in $y$, added and multiplied as usual and then reduced with the rule $y^r=-1$. Each element has a unique representative $\sum_{k=0}^{r-1}z_ky^k$ of degree less than $r$, where $z_k$ is its complex coefficient of $y^k$, so an element is stored as a list of $r$ complex numbers <cite data-source="paper">stored as the coefficient list of a polynomial modulo $y^r+1$, with $r$ a power of two</cite>. Its size is measured by $\|\sum_k z_ky^k\|_\infty=\max_k|z_k|$.

## Monomials act as signed shifts

Multiplying by $y^a$ sends $z_ky^k$ to $z_ky^{k+a}$. Write $k+a=qr+s$ with $0\le s<r$; since $y^r=-1$, we get $y^{k+a}=(-1)^q y^s$. So coefficient $k$ moves to position $(k+a)\bmod r$, and its sign flips once for each wrap past degree $r-1$. For $r=4$ and $a=1$,
$$y\,(z_0+z_1y+z_2y^2+z_3y^3)=-z_3+z_0y+z_1y^2+z_2y^3 .$$
This is a cyclic shift whose wrapped coefficients are negated (a negacyclic shift). Negative $a$ works the same way, since $y^{-1}=-y^{r-1}$. Multiplication by $y^a$ therefore permutes the coefficients and changes some signs <cite data-source="paper">permutes the coefficients and changes some signs</cite>. It leaves every $|z_k|$ unchanged, so it preserves $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ exactly, with no rounding.

## Exact roots of unity

From $y^r=-1$ we get $y^{2r}=1$. For $0<a<r$ the power $y^a$ is a nonconstant representative, and for $r\le a<2r$ it equals $-y^{a-r}$, so $y^a\ne1$. Thus $y$ is a primitive $2r$-th root of unity in $\mathcal R_r$. For a power of two $t$ dividing $2r$, $y^{2r/t}$ is a primitive $t$-th root, so a transform of length $t$ can use it as its twiddle factor. Over $\mathbb C$ such a root would be an inexact number like $e^{2\pi i/t}$; here multiplication by it is a signed shift <cite data-source="paper">multiplication by such a root is a signed shift of the coefficient list</cite>, which costs a pass over the record and introduces no error.
