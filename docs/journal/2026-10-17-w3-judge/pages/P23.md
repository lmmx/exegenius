Writing each input as a list of fixed-width blocks of bits makes the integer product a polynomial product of the two digit lists, and padding them with zeros reduces multiplication to a [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md), which the algorithm's fast transforms compute.

The transforms, like the FFT, compute cyclic convolutions, in which indices wrap around modulo the length, rather than ordinary polynomial products; padding to a length beyond the product's degree makes the two coincide.

## Digits as coefficients

Let $n$ be the number of bits of each input and $b=\lceil\log_2 n\rceil$ the width of one digit in bits. Cut each input into $q=\lceil n/b\rceil$ blocks of $b$ bits, its digits, padding the top block with zeros. Reading block $i$ as a number $u_i$ with $0\le u_i<2^b$, the first input is $U=\sum_{i<q}u_i2^{bi}$, the value at $x=2^b$ of the polynomial $u(x)=\sum_{i<q}u_ix^i$ in a variable $x$; the second input $V$ likewise gives digits $v_k$ and $v(x)$. Evaluation at $2^b$ respects products, so $UV=c(2^b)$, where $c(x)=u(x)v(x)=\sum_jc_jx^j$ and $c_j=\sum_{i+k=j}u_iv_k$, the ordinary convolution of the digit lists.

## No wraparound

The algorithm takes $T$, the padded size, to be the power of two in $[4n/b,8n/b)$ <cite data-source="paper">Take the power of two $T$ in $[4n/b,8n/b)$</cite>. The convolution length $S$ is a product of distinct primes with $T/2<S<T$ <cite data-source="paper">Together with $s_i<t_i$, this gives $T/2<S<T$.</cite>, chosen in [Parameter choices and prime source lengths](parameter-choice.md). Since $x^S=1$ modulo $x^S-1$, the product modulo $x^S-1$ puts at index $j$ every $u_iv_k$ with $i+k\equiv j\pmod S$, which is the cyclic convolution of the two digit lists padded with zeros to length $S$. If every $i+k$ that occurs is below $S$, the congruence forces $i+k=j$, so cyclic and ordinary convolution agree. Here $i+k\le 2q-2$; $q<n/b+1$ gives $2q-2<2n/b$, the choice of $T$ gives $2n/b\le T/2$, and $T/2<S$:
$$2q-2<2n/b\le T/2<S.$$
Hence no term wraps around <cite data-source="paper">Thus convolution modulo $x^S-1$ has no nonzero wraparound.</cite>.

## Coefficient size

Each $c_j$ is a sum of at most $q$ products $u_iv_k$, one for each $i$, and each product lies between $0$ and $(2^b-1)^2$. Since $q\le n\le2^b$ (because $b\ge1$ and $b\ge\log_2n$), this gives $0\le c_j\le q(2^b-1)^2<2^b\cdot2^{2b}=2^{3b}$, so every coefficient fits in $3b$ bits. This bound lets [Rounding and carry propagation](exact-recovery.md) recover each $c_j$ exactly from approximate transforms and turn them into the bits of $UV$.

## Example

Take $n=4$, so $b=2$ and $q=2$. The inputs $13=1+3\cdot4$ and $11=3+2\cdot4$ give $u(x)=1+3x$, $v(x)=3+2x$ and $c(x)=3+11x+6x^2$, and $c(4)=3+44+96=143=13\cdot11$. With $S=3$ nothing wraps; with $S=2$ the term $6x^2$ would fold onto the constant, giving $9+11x$, whose value at $4$ is $53$, not $143$.
