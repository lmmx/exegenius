Retained permutations keep the two coordinate permutations of [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md) inside the transform formula instead of executing them as sorts: the target permutation is absorbed into a chirp convolution and the source permutation cancels, so the multiplication gets its convolution without sorting.

The arrays live on $\mathbb Z/s_1\times\dots\times\mathbb Z/s_d$, where $d$ is the number of coordinates and the source lengths $s_i$ are pairwise coprime; each $s_i$ has a power-of-two target length $t_i>s_i$ with $\gcd(s_i,t_i)=1$. Write $\boldsymbol s=(s_1,\dots,s_d)$, $\boldsymbol t=(t_1,\dots,t_d)$, and let $S=\prod_is_i$ and $T=\prod_it_i$ be the numbers of entries in a source and a target array. Let $F_{\boldsymbol s}$, $F_{\boldsymbol t}$ be the normalized negative-sign [multidimensional transforms](multidimensional-dft.md) on these grids, and $F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$ the positive-sign version. Resampling writes $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$, not $F_{\boldsymbol s}$, as a Gaussian-weighted map, then $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$, then another such map, up to a power of two, where for a source array $x$ and a target array $w$ the permutations $R$ and $Q$ are

$$(Rx)_{\boldsymbol j}=x_{(t_1j_1,\dots,t_dj_d)},\qquad (Qw)_{\boldsymbol k}=w_{(-s_1k_1,\dots,-s_dk_d)},$$

with indices modulo the lengths. Executing each as a sort <cite data-source="paper">Its usual implementation sorts twice on each</cite> costs a factor $\log t_i$ more than a scan <cite data-source="paper">each costing $O(tp\log t)$</cite>.

The middle factor $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$ is computed by the [Bluestein chirp identity](bluestein-chirp.md):

$$QF_{\boldsymbol t}w=\overline a\cdot\frac{a*(\overline a\cdot w)}{T},\qquad a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp\Bigl(-\pi i\sum_i\frac{s_ij_i^2}{t_i}\Bigr),$$

where $a$ is the chirp and a dot is entrywise multiplication. The factor $s_i$ in the chirp's exponent performs the frequency change of $Q$, so no array is permuted <cite data-source="paper">The chirp identity below performs the frequency change</cite>.

Forward resampling outputs $RF_{\boldsymbol s}u$ and $RF_{\boldsymbol s}v$ for the digit arrays $u,v$, left in scrambled order. These are multiplied entrywise, then the opposite transform $RF^+_{\boldsymbol s}$ is applied, computed by the same algorithm with input and output complex-conjugated <cite data-source="paper">Complex conjugation of the input and output of an algorithm for</cite>. The cancellation applies to computing a [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md), which is all the multiplication needs:

$$RF^+_{\boldsymbol s}\bigl[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)\bigr]=\frac{u*v}{S^2}.$$

First, $R$ passes through $F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$. For a length $q$, a unit $c$ modulo $q$ (an integer invertible modulo $q$) and a vector $z$ of length $q$, let $(R_cz)_j=z_{cj}$. Substituting $h=ck$, which runs over every residue modulo $q$ once as $k$ does, gives

$$(F^+_qR_cz)_j=\frac1q\sum_{k=0}^{q-1}z_{ck}e^{2\pi ikj/q}=\frac1q\sum_{h=0}^{q-1}z_he^{2\pi ihc^{-1}j/q}=(F^+_qz)_{c^{-1}j}=(R_c^{-1}F^+_qz)_j,$$

so $F^+_qR_c=R_c^{-1}F^+_q$. On coordinate $i$, $R$ is $R_c$ with $q=s_i$ and $c=t_i$, a unit since $\gcd(s_i,t_i)=1$. Applying the identity on every coordinate gives $F^+_{\boldsymbol s}R=R^{-1}F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$, that is, $RF^+_{\boldsymbol s}R=F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$. For example, with $s_1=3$ and $t_1=8\equiv2$, $R$ swaps entries $1$ and $2$, and entry $(j,h)$ of $RF_3^+R$ is $e^{2\pi i(2j)(2h)/3}/3=e^{2\pi ijh/3}/3$, as in $F_3^+$.

Second, $R$ only moves entries, so $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)=R(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$, and hence

$$RF^+_{\boldsymbol s}\bigl[R(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)\bigr]=F^+_{\boldsymbol s}(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v).$$

The rest follows from the normalizations of the [Discrete Fourier transform](dft.md), tensored over the coordinates: the convolution rule $F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=S(F_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$ and the inversion $F^+_{\boldsymbol s}F_{\boldsymbol s}=I/S$ give

$$F^+_{\boldsymbol s}(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)=\frac1SF^+_{\boldsymbol s}F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=\frac{u*v}{S^2}.$$
