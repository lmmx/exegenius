Packed selected-bit addition XORs one address chunk's bits into another's at evenly spaced positions for the [Fast simultaneous butterfly layer](fast-butterfly-layer.md), using a constant number of cheap whole-chunk cyclic shifts, then repairing a bounded set of addresses <cite data-source="paper">The construction first uses a constant number of cyclic shifts of whole chunk ranges, then corrects an explicitly bounded set of addresses.</cite>

$M$ records of $R$ bits hold $V=MR$ bits. Chunks $x,y,z,$ with $y$ not the ring variable here, have $L=fK$ bits, where $f$ counts the selected positions $j_i=\rho+iK$ $(0\le i<f)$ spaced $K$ apart from offset $\rho\in[0,K).$ Goal: $y_{j_i}\gets y_{j_i}\oplus x_{j_i},$ restoring all else, even an arbitrary $z.$

For control bit $c,$ integers $u,w$ and indicator $[P]$ of $P,$ run in order:

$$\begin{array}{llll}(1)\ u\gets u-c[w\bmod2=0] & (2)\ w\gets w-c[u\bmod2=0] & (3)\ u\gets u+c[w\bmod2=0] & (4)\ w\gets w+c[u\bmod2=0]\\(5)\ w\gets w+c[u\bmod2=1] & (6)\ u\gets u+c[w\bmod2=1] & (7)\ w\gets w-c[u\bmod2=1] & (8)\ u\gets u-c[w\bmod2=1]\end{array}$$

For $c=1,$ label corners of squares $\{2a,2a+1\}\times\{2b,2b+1\}$ by parities of $(u,w);$ tests see only parities, so $a=b=0$ suffices. Lines (1)–(4) send $(0,0)\to(-1,0)\to(-1,0)\to(0,0)\to(0,1)$ and likewise give the cycle $(00\ 01\ 10);$ lines (5)–(8) give $(01\ 10\ 11).$ Composed, $00\mapsto01\mapsto10,$ $01\mapsto10\mapsto11,$ $10\mapsto00,$ $11\mapsto01:$ the product $(00\ 10)(01\ 11)$ moves corners horizontally, so

$$(u',w')=\bigl(u+c(1-2(u\bmod2)),\,w\bigr).$$

The parity of $u$ flips exactly when $c=1,$ both halves $\lfloor u/2\rfloor,\lfloor w/2\rfloor$ stay, and any $w$ is restored; four unit steps move each coordinate at most four.

For $i<f-1,$ the $K$-bit segment of $y$ at $j_i$ is $u_i=2g_i+a_i,$ $a_i=y_{j_i},$ with guard $g_i<2^{K-1};$ likewise $w_i=2h_i+b_i$ in $z;$ control $c_i=x_{j_i}.$ Position $j_{f-1}$ is XORed separately in $O(V).$ One line, of sign $\varepsilon$ testing parity $\beta,$ on all segments is the shift $y\gets y+\varepsilon\sum_{i=0}^{f-2}2^{j_i}[x_{j_i}=1][z_{j_i}=\beta]\pmod{2^L},$ whose offset ignores $y,$ so it is a bijection, as are lines updating $z.$ $S_0$ composes the lines; $T_0$ does the XORs at $j_0,\dots,j_{f-2}.$

Let $\mathcal B$ hold the addresses where some guard leaves $[10,2^{K-1}-11].$ Elsewhere segments start in $[20,2^K-21],$ so moves $\le8$ never carry between segments and $S_0=T_0.$ Each guard excludes twenty of $2^{K-1}$ values, all equally frequent, so a union bound over $2(f-1)$ guards gives $|\mathcal B|\le\delta M$ with $\delta=\min\{1,80(f-1)2^{-K}\},$ for any records <cite data-source="paper">There are exactly twenty excluded values for each guard in</cite>.

$T_0$ keeps guards, so $T_0(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B.$ Since $T_0$ and $S_0$ are bijections agreeing on $\mathcal B^c,$ also $S_0(\mathcal B)=\mathcal B,$ so the correction $T_0S_0^{-1}$ fixes $\mathcal B^c$ and permutes $\mathcal B.$

Each rotation target is swapped behind its controls and back by [Fast address-chunk interchange](address-chunk-interchange.md) in $O(VL^\tau)$ with fixed $\tau<1,$ and rotated by [Controlled cyclic shifts](controlled-shift.md) in $O(V);$ $S_0$ needs at most sixteen swaps and eight rotations <cite data-source="paper">There are at most sixteen swaps and eight rotations in $S_0$.</cite> Extracting $\mathcal B$'s records keyed by destination rank, radix-sorting them, and reinserting them in one scan gives $T_0,$ as keys equal the vacated ranks.

With rank length $A=\lceil\log_2(2V)\rceil,$ movement costs $O(V(L^\tau+1)),$ scans at $O(A^3)$ per record cost $O(MA^3+V),$ and at most $A$ radix passes cost $O(|\mathcal B|A(R+A)).$ Divided by $V,$ the extras are at most $A^3/R$ and $80(f-1)2^{-K}A(1+A/R),$ both $o(1)$ since $A=O(p)$ for precision $p,$ $R/p^C\to\infty$ for fixed $C,$ $f\le p$ and $K/\log p\to\infty,$ so the total is $O(V(L^\tau+1))$ <cite data-source="paper">The movement costs $O(V(L^\tau+1))$.</cite>

Hence, on $e=mf$ axis chunks in $m$ slots of width $L=(e/m)K,$ a fixed invertible binary $m\times m$ matrix and its inverse, each a fixed number of packed row additions, cost $O(V((eK)^\tau+1)).$
