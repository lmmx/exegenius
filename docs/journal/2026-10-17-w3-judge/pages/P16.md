Gaussian resampling rewrites a [discrete Fourier transform](dft.md) of prime length $s$ through a power-of-two transform of a longer length $t$, so that power-of-two transforms can serve the coprime lengths the integer product needs.

Let $p$ be the precision in bits, $2\le s<t<2^p$ coprime lengths, $\alpha<\sqrt p$ an integer width, $\theta=t/s-1>p/\alpha^4$ the relative gap, and $0<\delta<1/8$ fixed. Usual implementations sort twice on each coordinate <cite data-source="paper">Its usual implementation sorts twice on each</cite>; here the permutations $(P_su)_j=u_{tj}$ and $(P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}$ stay inside the formula, unexecuted.

## One dimension

Define $\mathsf S,\mathsf G:\mathbb C^s\to\mathbb C^t$ by $(\mathsf Su)_k=\frac1\alpha\sum_je^{-\pi\alpha^{-2}(j-sk/t)^2}u_j$ and $(\mathsf Gu)_k=\sum_je^{-\pi\alpha^2(tj/s-k)^2}u_j$, indices of $u$ read modulo $s$. Harvey and van der Hoeven prove the identity $\mathsf GP_sF_s=P_tF_t\mathsf S$ <cite data-source="paper">The exact Gaussian identity and inverse estimate proved in</cite>. To invert $\mathsf G$, let $q_j=\lfloor tj/s+\tfrac12\rfloor$ be the integer nearest $tj/s$ and $\beta_j=tj/s-q_j$. Let $C$ copy coordinate $q_j$ to position $j$, and $\mathsf D$ multiply coordinate $j$ by $e^{\pi\alpha^2\beta_j^2}$. In $N=C\mathsf G\mathsf D$ the $j$-th summand of $(Nu)_j$ is exactly $u_j$, because $\mathsf D$ cancels the Gaussian weight at $q_j$; the others are small, $\|N-I\|<2^{-\alpha^2\theta}$. Since $\alpha^2\theta>p/\alpha^2>1$, $\|N-I\|<1/2$, so $N$ is invertible by a Neumann series. As $C\mathsf G=N\mathsf D^{-1}$, the map $\mathsf DN^{-1}C$ is a left inverse of $\mathsf G$, and applying it to this identity gives $\mathsf DN^{-1}CP_tF_t\mathsf S=P_sF_s$.

Put $\mathsf S'=\mathsf S/2$, $\mathsf J'=N^{-1}/2$, $\mathsf D'=2^{-(2\alpha^2-2)}\mathsf D$; their norms are below $3/4$, $7/8$, $1$ <cite data-source="paper">\|\mathsf S'\|<\frac34,\qquad</cite>. Set $A=\mathsf S'$ and $B_0=\mathsf D'\mathsf J'C$, both contractions (norm at most one) as $\|C\|=1$. The removed factors multiply to $2^{-(2\alpha^2-2)}\cdot\frac12\cdot\frac12=2^{-2\alpha^2}$, so $B_0P_tF_tA=2^{-2\alpha^2}P_sF_s$, that is, $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$.

Each of $\mathsf S',\mathsf J',\mathsf D'$ is approximated on the [fixed-point disk grid](fixed-point-truncation.md) by their algorithms, with errors $16p$, $3p^2/4$, $4$ and times $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$, $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$, $O(tp^{1+\delta})$ <cite data-source="paper">\widetilde{\mathsf S}'&16p&O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)\\</cite>. Since $q_{j+1}>q_j$, $C$ is a forward scan generating $q_j$ by integer counters: cost $O(tp)$, no error. Taking $\widetilde A=\widetilde{\mathsf S}'$ and $\widetilde B_0=\widetilde{\mathsf D}'\widetilde{\mathsf J}'C$ gives errors below $16p<p^2$ and $4+3p^2/4<p^2$ <cite data-source="paper">Contraction of the exact maps yields errors less than $16p<p^2$ and</cite>.

## Many axes

The identity extends to the [multidimensional transform](multidimensional-dft.md) $F_{\boldsymbol q}=\bigotimes_iF_{q_i}$ over $d$ axes by tensoring: with every $(s_i,t_i)$ as above, $R=\bigotimes_iP_{s_i}$, $Q=\bigotimes_iP_{t_i}$, $\mathcal A=\bigotimes_iA_i$, $\mathcal B_0=\bigotimes_iB_{0,i}$ give $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ with $\gamma=2d\alpha^2$. Implement $\mathcal A$ one axis at a time on the zero-padded box of $T=\prod_it_i$ entries: expand each valid line from $s_i$ to $t_i$ entries, writing zeros elsewhere; $\mathcal B_0$ compresses likewise. Each axis map is a contraction with error below $p^2$, so $d$ axes give error below $dp^2$ <cite data-source="paper">Composing the $d$ axis maps gives error less than $dp^2$.</cite> At most $T/t_i$ lines call the one-dimensional algorithm on axis $i$, costing $\sum_i\frac T{t_i}O(t_ip^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)=O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$.

Let $t_i\in\{r/2,r\}$ with $r=2^\ell$. Exposing an axis as innermost field takes $O(d)$ adjacent interchanges of $\ell$- or $(\ell-1)$-bit address fields, each $O(Tp(1+\ell^\tau))$ by [fast address-chunk interchange](address-chunk-interchange.md) with fixed $\tau<1$. All axes need $O(d^2)$ interchanges <cite data-source="paper">$O(d)$ adjacent interchanges, hence all axes need $O(d^2)$.</cite>, in total $O(Tpd^2(1+\ell^\tau))$. Since $t_i\ge r/2$, at most $2dT/r$ lines occur, so polynomial setup per line is $o(Tp)$.
