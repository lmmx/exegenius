Retained permutations are the two coordinate permutations of Gaussian resampling, kept inside the transform formula rather than executed as sorts: the target one is absorbed into a chirp convolution and the source one cancels when two transforms are multiplied and inverted.

Here $d$ is the number of coordinates, $s_i$ is the source length in coordinate $i$, and $t_i$ is the even target length there, coprime to $s_i$. Also $S=\prod_i s_i$ and $T=\prod_i t_i$ are the total array sizes. The source permutation is $(Ru)_{\boldsymbol j}=u_{(t_ij_i)_i}$ with each index taken mod $s_i$, and the target permutation $Q$ reads frequency $-s_ik_i$. For a length $q$, $F_q$ is the normalized Fourier transform and $F^+_q$ its opposite-sign version, and $F_{\boldsymbol q}$, $F^+_{\boldsymbol q}$ are their tensor products over lengths $q_i$.

This is based on [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md), which gives $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$ equal to a power of two times $\mathcal B_0\,QF_{\boldsymbol t}\,\mathcal A$, with $\mathcal A,\mathcal B_0$ contractions. ## The middle factor

The page computes its middle factor $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$ by the [Bluestein chirp identity](bluestein-chirp.md): with $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_i s_ij_i^2/t_i)$, $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot(a*(\overline a\cdot u))/T$. So $Q$ is never applied to the array <cite data-source="paper">The chirp identity below performs the frequency change in $Q$ without applying $Q$ as a separate permutation of the array.</cite>

## Cancelling $R$

The cancellation follows from the [Fourier transform](dft.md) rules. Take a unit $c$ modulo $q$ and $(R_cz)_j=z_{cj}$. (For $q=5$, $c=3$: $(z_0,\dots,z_4)\mapsto(z_0,z_3,z_1,z_4,z_2)$.) Substituting $h=ck$, which runs once over all residues because $c$ is a unit, gives
$$(F_q^+R_cz)_j=\frac1q\sum_{k}z_{ck}e^{2\pi i kj/q}=\frac1q\sum_{h}z_he^{2\pi i hc^{-1}j/q}=(R_c^{-1}F_q^+z)_j.$$
Each $t_i$ is a unit modulo $s_i$ and $R$ is the tensor product of the maps $R_{t_i}$, so $F^+_{\boldsymbol s}R=R^{-1}F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$, hence $RF^+_{\boldsymbol s}R=F^+_{\boldsymbol s}$.

A coordinate permutation commutes with pointwise products, so $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)=R(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$ and, by the identity just shown,
$$RF^+_{\boldsymbol s}\bigl[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)\bigr]=F^+_{\boldsymbol s}(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v).$$
Now $F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=S\,(F_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$ and $F^+_{\boldsymbol s}F_{\boldsymbol s}=I/S$, so
$$F^+_{\boldsymbol s}(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)=\frac1SF^+_{\boldsymbol s}F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=\frac{u*v}{S^2}.$$
The result reduces to the [cyclic convolution](cyclic-convolution.md) $u*v$ of the arrays $u,v$, divided by $S^2$. The $R$ on each side of the product is never executed <cite data-source="paper">retained source permutation $R$ cancels when computing a convolution.</cite>

The opposite transform $RF^+_{\boldsymbol s}$ comes from an algorithm for $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$ by conjugating input and output.
