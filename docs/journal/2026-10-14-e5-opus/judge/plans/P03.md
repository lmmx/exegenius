# Plan P03

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): A single deterministic fixed-alphabet multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: speed up address movement and butterfly arithmetic by recursive linear-network procedures, plug them into Harvey and van der Hoeven's reduction of multiplication to synthetic polynomial transforms, and bound the total cost
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning Θ(n) bits at each of Θ(log n) transform levels already costs Θ(n log n), so beating n log n requires power savings in both data movement and arithmetic.
  - relies on: nlogn-conjecture, harvey-hoeven-multiplier, multitape-turing-machine, big-o
  - **A** (show): Address-field interchange costs $O(Vu^\tau)$ and a simultaneous butterfly layer costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, with fixed $\tau,\lambda'<1$, where $V$ is the stored bit volume.
    - method: turn a strict rank deficit of two finite linear networks into recursive power savings for both tape procedures
    - motivation (explicit): Each procedure reduces a call at parameter mf to s calls at parameter f on volume V/W; since s<Wm the normalized cost grows by less than m per level, which gives a power saving.
    - relies on: multitape-turing-machine, big-o
    - **S1** (show, page `tape-array-layout`): Arrays of logical volume $V$ stored in lexicographic order admit a toolkit of $O(V)$-time tape operations: digit permutations and moves, splits and merges, cyclic row distribution among $W$ streams, and controlled shifts of a later field.
      - method: collect the linear-time scans that every later procedure is built from
      - motivation (recoverable): Every later cost bound is a recurrence on time divided by volume, so the primitives must cost O(V) with constants independent of all field lengths; the paper isolates them first so later steps only count calls.
      - relies on: multitape-turing-machine, lexicographic-order, turing-machine-alphabet-and-simulation-constants
      - **S1.1** (infer, page `elementary-streams`): Permuting or moving a single radix-$q$ digit, splitting and merging on a digit, and distributing rows cyclically among $W$ streams each cost $O(V)$.
        - relies on: tape-array-layout, binary-counter, stacks
        - quotes line 493: "Each of the following operations on an array of volume $V$ takes $O(V)$ steps on a fixed number of one-dimensional tapes:"
      - **S1.2** (infer, page `controlled-shifts`): An update $y\mapsto y+a(\text{controls})\bmod q^b$ of a target field that follows its control fields, or multiplication of $y$ by a fixed rational unit, costs $O(V)$.
        - relies on: elementary-streams, modular-arithmetic, euclidean-algorithm
        - quotes line 552: "The next operations are linear because their target field follows their control fields."
    - **S2** (show, page `rank-deficit`): There are two fixed linear networks, one over $\mathbb F_2$ and one over $\mathbb Z[i,1/2]$, each with $W$ wires, that exchange two banks of values, restore all scratch values, and admit nested subspace labels whose edge changes need only $s<Wm$ smaller calls in total.
      - method: build the scalar networks from triple-intersection gather/scatter with scratch restoration, lift them to arrays by frames, and count the residual dimensions of nested subspace labels
      - motivation (explicit): The frame identity shows that only endpoint frames determine the array operator, so intermediate frames can be chosen freely to make each edge cheap; nested subspace labels make an edge's cost its dimension change, and the subset construction keeps the total dimension lost small.
      - relies on: linear-network, rank-of-a-matrix
      - **S2.1** (infer, page `scratch-restoration`): For arbitrary initial scratch values, the eight-row forward schedule changes $y_S$ to $y_S+x_S$ and restores every scratch wire.
        - relies on: linear-network
        - quotes line 748: "The net target change is $(\mathcal J\mathcal V+\mathcal R\mathcal G)x$, independently of $a,c$."
      - **S2.2** (infer, page `triple-intersection-construction`): Gather/scatter through central wires gives coefficients depending on $|S\cap T|$, side wires cancel the unwanted ones so each invocation is $y\gets y+x$, and three stages give $(X,Y)\mapsto(-Y,X)$.
        - relies on: scratch-restoration, binomial-coefficients-and-subsets, xor-and-arithmetic-modulo-2
        - quotes line 762: "Starting from $(x,y)$, the three stages give $(x,y+x)$, then $(-y,y+x)$, then $(-y,x)$."
      - **S2.3** (infer, page `frames`): With an invertible frame $D_v$ at every source, gate and sink, the network on arrays implements $D_{\rm out}\mathsf S_\Omega D_{\rm in}^{-1}$, so each route applies only its endpoint frames.
        - relies on: linear-network, linear-maps-and-invertibility, vectors-and-matrices
        - quotes line 812: "Thus the common frame commutes with the pointwise gate, and the invariant continues to the sinks."
      - **S2.4** (infer, page `subspace-labels`): Gates and terminals receive nondegenerate subspaces of an $m$-dimensional bilinear space, nested along every edge, with the only decreasing edges being the central-wire edges from time 3 to time 4.
        - relies on: triple-intersection-construction, frames, vector-spaces-subspaces-and-dimension, dot-product-and-orthogonality
        - quotes line 998: "Every edge joins comparable nondegenerate labels."
      - **S2.5** (infer): The total dimension lost on decreasing edges satisfies $L/N<1/2$ in both networks, which leaves a strict deficit $s<Wm$.
        - relies on: subspace-labels
        - quotes line 1110: "In particular, both are smaller than $1/2$."
    - **S3** (infer, page `recursive-saving`): Since $s/W<m^\tau$ with $\tau=1-2^{-50}$, a call at parameter $mf$ done by $s$ calls at parameter $f$ on volume $V/W$ gives normalized cost $O(f^\tau)$.
      - relies on: rank-deficit, elementary-streams, divide-and-conquer-recurrences, geometric-series
      - quotes line 1331: "Both exact deficits displayed above are greater than $20\cdot2^{-50}$."
    - **S4** (show, page `fast-address-interchange`): Two disjoint contiguous $u$-bit address fields of an array of volume $V$ can be interchanged in $O(Vu^\tau)$ time.
      - method: implement each edge's matrix shear by rank-many smaller interchanges via a triangular factorization, run the bit network on row-split role streams, recurse on power widths, then pad to arbitrary widths
      - motivation (explicit): An edge matrix of rank a costs a smaller interchanges, and the ranks sum to less than W m, so the recursion saves a power of the width.
      - relies on: recursive-saving, elementary-streams, controlled-shifts
      - **S4.1** (infer, page `triangular-factorization`): Every rational $m\times m$ matrix of rank $a$ factors as $E_1\Pi E_2$ with $E_1,E_2$ invertible lower triangular and $\Pi$ a partial permutation with $a$ ones.
        - relies on: gaussian-elimination, rank-of-a-matrix
        - quotes line 1366: "Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ has a factorization"
      - **S4.2** (infer, page `address-shear`): The address shear $(H,D)\mapsto(H+AD,D)$ over $\mathbb Z/q^b\mathbb Z$ with $A$ of rank $a$ takes exactly $a$ smaller field interchanges plus $O(V)$ work.
        - relies on: controlled-shifts, triangular-factorization
        - quotes line 1406: "can be performed with exactly $a$ interchanges of pairs of $b$-digit fields and $O(V)$ further work."
      - **S4.3** (infer, page `bit-network-interface`): The bit network with projection frames gives the shear $H\gets H+D$ on every role, with endpoint matrices differing by $I_m$ and edge ranks summing to $s_{\rm b}<W_{\rm b}m$.
        - relies on: rank-deficit, address-shear, frames
        - quotes line 1470: "Proposition~\ref{prop:bit-motif-interface} supplies these conditions with $W=W_{\rm b}$ and $s=s_{\rm b}$."
      - **S4.4** (infer): For widths $e=m^k$ with enough rows, the normalized cost satisfies $F_k\le(s/W)F_{k-1}+O(1)$, hence the interchange costs $O(Ve^\tau)$.
        - relies on: recursive-saving, bit-network-interface
        - quotes line 1557: "Hence $F_k=O(e^\tau)$."
      - **S4.5** (infer): Supplying rows from high digits, decomposing the width in base $m$ and padding binary chunks to radix $q$ extends the bound to $O(Vu^\tau)$ for every width $u$.
        - relies on: elementary-streams
        - quotes line 1733: "Since $e=O(u)$ for fixed $q$, the bound is $O(Vu^\tau)$."
    - **S5** (show, page `simultaneous-butterfly-layer`): Applying $H_0$ at one common bit position in each of $D\le d$ equal-width address chunks of polynomial records costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with $\lambda'<1$, with exact dyadic intermediates and one final truncation.
      - method: recurse through the complex network's phase frames, realizing binary basis changes by packed address moves and converting the C-layer to the H_0 layer by phases
      - motivation (explicit): Separate butterfly factors would need one full pass per selected axis; the phase-frame recursion shares the work across axes and saves a power of d.
      - relies on: recursive-saving, fast-address-interchange, polynomial-ring, normalized-butterfly
      - **S5.1** (infer, page `phase-frames`): With phase frames $\mathcal C_U$, each edge's frame change is a product of one two-point kernel $aI+bX_v$ per orthonormal residual vector, and telescoping gives $C^{\otimes e}$ on every role by $s_{\rm c}$ child calls on $e/m$ axes.
        - relies on: subspace-labels, rank-deficit, gaussian-dyadics, normalized-butterfly, hamming-weight
        - quotes line 1911: "Proposition~\ref{prop:complex-motif-interface} therefore gives $C^{\otimes e}$ on every role, including roles whose initial scratch values were arbitrary."
      - **S5.2** (infer): Diagonal phases and the scalar $b^D$ convert the exact $C^{\otimes D}$ layer into $H_0^{\otimes D}$ using only Gaussian dyadic operations.
        - relies on: normalized-butterfly, gaussian-dyadics
        - quotes line 1938: "Thus, once the physical binary basis changes and recursive stream layout are supplied, the complete parallel butterfly layer is exact up to its prescribed final truncation."
      - **S5.3** (infer): Packed changes of selected address bits and the recursion give total time $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ on fixed tapes.
        - relies on: fast-address-interchange, recursive-saving
        - quotes line 2736: "The row construction and the recurrence analysis above give total time $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ on fixed tapes, including all padded and auxiliary streams."
      - **S5.4** (infer, page `fixed-point-precision`): Since $H_0^{\otimes D}$ maps disk arrays to disk arrays, the final truncation $Q_p$ keeps values in the disk with error below $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$.
        - relies on: complex-numbers, gaussian-dyadics
        - quotes line 2752: "Thus $\mathcal H$ maps disk arrays to disk arrays, and componentwise truncation preserves that disk and has error less than $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$"
  - **B** (show): Cyclic convolution on distinct prime axes is computed from synthetic transforms over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, Gaussian resampling with retained permutations, and Bluestein chirps.
    - method: evaluate the needed transforms as synthetic polynomial transforms on a layout where the fast layers apply, and connect them to the prime-length transforms by resampling and chirps
    - motivation (explicit): In the polynomial-ring representation every transform step is an address rearrangement, a signed shift or a two-point butterfly, exactly the operations the two tape procedures accelerate.
    - relies on: fast-fourier-transform, polynomial-multiplication-via-evaluation-and-interpolation
    - **S6** (show, page `transform-layout`): Synthetic transforms over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ on $d-1$ power-of-two axes, with outputs kept in a stored frequency order, cost $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$ with error below $\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$.
      - method: compute Nussbaumer's decimation-in-frequency recursion, group each round into a parallel butterfly plus a monomial, and lay the selected bits out in common-offset chunks for the simultaneous layer
      - motivation (explicit): The simultaneous layer applies only to selected bits at a common offset in consecutive K-bit chunks, so a layout must be built and its movement charged.
      - relies on: synthetic-transform, simultaneous-butterfly-layer, fast-address-interchange
      - **S6.1** (infer, page `synthetic-transform`): With root $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$, decimation-in-frequency butterflies and signed shifts compute $B_tF_t^-$, writing the outputs directly in bit-reversed frequency order.
        - relies on: polynomial-ring, normalized-butterfly, discrete-fourier-transform, bit-reversal-permutation
        - quotes line 2869: "The recursion writes its outputs directly in this order; it does not move whole frequency indices."
      - **S6.2** (infer): Each round on several axes is a parallel butterfly $\mathcal H$ followed by one recordwise monomial $J_E$.
        - relies on: multidimensional-transform
        - quotes line 2899: "Consequently the individual axis steps may be grouped as a parallel butterfly $\mathcal H$, followed by one recordwise monomial operation $J_E$."
      - **S6.3** (infer): Moving prefix slots and interchanging chunks into the layout, and applying one simultaneous layer per round, gives the stated cost including layout construction and restoration.
        - relies on: simultaneous-butterfly-layer, fast-address-interchange, parameter-choice
        - quotes line 2986: "Both layout construction and final restoration are charged in this bound."
    - **S7** (show, page `synthetic-convolution`): The normalized cyclic convolution $\mathcal M_r(f,g)=(f*g)/T$ of arrays over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ is computed with error below $\sqrt2M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$, adding only $O(Tp\log(rp))$ to the transform cost.
      - method: transform both operands forward, keep their common stored order, multiply records by signed packing, and apply the opposite transform to that order
      - motivation (explicit): The opposite transform consumes the stored order directly, so the bit-reversal and layout permutations never have to be undone between the transforms and the pointwise product.
      - relies on: transform-layout, signed-kronecker-packing, cyclic-convolution
      - **S7.1** (infer, page `signed-kronecker-packing`): Two polynomial records are multiplied by packing signed coefficients into integers, multiplying with the $O(n\log n)$ multiplier and unpacking, returning $Q_p(fg/r)$ in $O(rp\log(rp))$ time.
        - relies on: harvey-hoeven-multiplier, polynomial-ring, fixed-point-precision, two-s-complement-and-signed-integers
        - quotes line 3228: "One can compute the exact Gaussian integer numerators of $fg$, and then return $Q_p(fg/r)$ in that same format, in time $O(rp\log(rp))$ on a fixed number of tapes."
      - **S7.2** (infer): Both forward transforms retain their common $LB$ order, the packed products keep it, and the opposite procedure consumes it.
        - relies on: transform-layout
        - quotes line 3415: "Perform both forward transforms and retain their common $LB$ order."
    - **S8** (show, page `gaussian-resampling`): The tensor of prime-length source transforms satisfies $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ with power-of-two lengths $t_i$, contractions $\mathcal A,\mathcal B_0$, and the permutations $R,Q$ kept in the formula.
      - method: apply Harvey and van der Hoeven's one-dimensional resampling identity with both coordinate permutations retained, then take tensor products line by line using chunk swaps
      - motivation (explicit): The needed transform lengths are pairwise coprime while synthetic transforms need powers of two; resampling connects the shapes, and keeping the permutations in the formula avoids two full sorts per coordinate.
      - relies on: multidimensional-transform, fixed-point-precision
      - **S8.1** (infer): For coprime $s<t$, $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$ with contractions $A,B_0$ computable with error below $p^2$.
        - relies on: discrete-fourier-transform, exponential-function-and-gaussians, prime-numbers-and-gcd
        - quotes line 3559: "There are linear contractions $A:\mathbb C^s\to\mathbb C^t$ and $B_0:\mathbb C^t\to\mathbb C^s$ satisfying"
      - **S8.2** (infer): Linewise tensoring gives the multidimensional identity with contractions $\mathcal A,\mathcal B_0$, and exposing lines costs $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$.
        - relies on: multidimensional-transform, fast-address-interchange
        - quotes line 3818: "Both $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B_0$ are contractions."
    - **S9** (infer, page `bluestein-chirp`): The middle factor $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u$ equals $\overline a\cdot(a*(\overline a\cdot u))/T$ for a chirp $a$, so it is a normalized cyclic convolution between two phase multiplications.
      - relies on: discrete-fourier-transform, cyclic-convolution, gaussian-resampling
      - quotes line 3901: "The factor $QF_{\boldsymbol t}$ in \eqref{eq:no-sort-tensor} can be computed from a cyclic convolution and two pointwise phase multiplications."
    - **S10** (infer): The retained source permutation $R$ cancels: $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+)[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)]=(u*v)/S^2$.
      - relies on: gaussian-resampling, cyclic-convolution, permutations
      - quotes line 3905: "The second identity shows how the retained source permutation $R$ cancels when computing a convolution."
  - **C** (show): The machine computes the exact $2n$-bit product of $n$-bit inputs in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time.
    - method: place the radix-digit coefficients on prime axes, reduce to ring convolution by a twist, run the transforms, recover exact coefficients, and sum the normalized cost table under fixed parameters
    - motivation (explicit): The known normalizations and an error strictly below 1/2 make rounding exact, and a compatible fixed choice of exponents puts every row of the cost table below the target power of p = Θ(log n).
    - relies on: multitape-turing-machine, deterministic-algorithm-and-worst-case-running-time
    - **S11** (infer, page `crt-address-map`): A Chinese-remainder map $x\mapsto\prod_ix_i^{\mu_i}$ preserves cyclic convolution and is implemented by triangular controlled shifts and axis moves.
      - relies on: chinese-remainder-theorem, controlled-shifts, fast-address-interchange, cyclic-convolution
      - quotes line 4219: "Hence $\Phi$ is an isomorphism and preserves cyclic convolution."
    - **S12** (infer, page `coefficient-twist`): The twist $x_d\mapsto\zeta y$ with $\zeta=e^{\pi i/r}$ turns cyclic convolution in the last coordinate into negacyclic arithmetic in $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$.
      - relies on: polynomial-ring, cyclic-convolution, complex-numbers
      - quotes line 4319: "Untwisting therefore recovers the cyclic coefficient with the correct sign."
    - **S13** (infer, page `radix-digit-convolution`): Splitting each input into radix-$2^b$ digits turns the product into a convolution of short coefficient lists, scaled into the disk and padded to $S$ records.
      - relies on: cyclic-convolution, binary-representation-of-integers, schoolbook-integer-arithmetic-with-carries
      - quotes line 4490: "This produces the radix-$2^b$ digits $a_0,\ldots,a_{q-1}$ in increasing coefficient order."
    - **S14** (infer, page `exact-recovery`): After removing the scales $S$ and $2^{2b+4}S$, the coefficient error is below $1/2$, so rounding gives the exact integers, and carry propagation yields the $2n$-bit product.
      - relies on: radix-digit-convolution, fixed-point-precision, rounding-to-the-nearest-integer, schoolbook-integer-arithmetic-with-carries
      - quotes line 4730: "The strict error below $1/2$ makes the nearest integer to that value the unique correct coefficient."
    - **S15** (infer, page `parameter-choice`): With $\tau=\sigma=1-2^{-50}$, $\lambda'=1-2^{-54}$, $\epsilon=2^{-75}$, every normalized cost in the table is at most $p^{1-2\kappa}$, so the total time is $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$.
      - relies on: fast-address-interchange, simultaneous-butterfly-layer, gaussian-resampling, big-o, logarithms-and-exponents
      - quotes line 4854: "Thus $\min_i g_i=2^{-181}=2\kappa$."
