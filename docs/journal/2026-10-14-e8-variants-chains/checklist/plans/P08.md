The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): There is one deterministic fixed-alphabet, fixed-tape Turing machine multiplying $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.
  - method: Reduce the product to a digit convolution evaluated by Fourier-type transforms on power-of-two grids over a polynomial ring, speed up the two dominant tape operations, address interchange and butterfly layers, by a power of their width, build the polynomial-ring convolution from them, express the coprime-length transforms through these by Gaussian resampling, check exactness, and balance the parameters so every cost row is at most $(\lg n)^{1-2\kappa}$ per unit volume.
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning the data at each of the logarithmically many transform levels already costs $n\log n$, so both movement and arithmetic must be made cheaper than a pass per level.
  - relies on: fixed-tape-model, nlogn-conjecture, nlogn-multiplier, int-as-convolution, cyclic-convolution, tape-machines, big-o
