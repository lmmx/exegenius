The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps. Its step ids are this plan's own.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): A single deterministic Turing machine with a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed finite number of one-dimensional tapes outputs $\operatorname{bin}_{2n}(\operatorname{val}(x)\operatorname{val}(y))$ for every pair of $n$-bit inputs $x,y$ in worst-case time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: Build two fast tape procedures (address-chunk interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers) from fixed linear networks with a rank saving, use them inside Harvey and van der Hoeven's Gaussian-resampling reduction of the digit convolution to synthetic polynomial transforms over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, recover the integer coefficients exactly, and bound every row of a cost table by $p^{1-2\kappa}$ times $Tp=\Theta(n)$.
  - motivation (explicit): Transform-based multiplication scans $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ levels, so beating $n\log n$ requires saving on both data movement and butterfly arithmetic; the two tape procedures provide these savings and the reduction carries them into exact multiplication.
  - relies on: n-log-n-conjecture, fixed-tape-model, big-o, turing-machine-tapes, binary-representation-of-integers
  - **S1** (show): Two tape procedures save a power of their parameter: interchanging two disjoint $u$-bit address chunks of an array of volume $V$ costs $O(Vu^\tau)$, and the normalized butterfly $H_0$ on one selected bit of each of $D\le d$ address chunks of polynomial records costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, with fixed $\tau,\lambda'<1$.
    - method: Derive each procedure from a fixed linear network that exchanges two banks and restores scratch values, run the network on $W$ role streams of volume $V/W$ with frames chosen so that every edge costs as many smaller recursive calls as its label-dimension change, and solve the volume-normalized recurrence using $s<Wm$.
