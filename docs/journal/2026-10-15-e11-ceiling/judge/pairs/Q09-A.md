# Plan A of pair Q09

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps. Its step ids are this plan's own.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): One deterministic multitape Turing machine with a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed number of tapes computes the exact $2n$-bit product of two $n$-bit integers in worst-case time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, disproving the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture of Schonhage and Strassen in this model.
  - method: Build two tape procedures (address-chunk interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers) whose volume-normalized costs save a power of their parameter through a finite linear network with a rank deficit, embed them in a chain of reductions from integer multiplication to convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, recover the exact product, and divide every cost by the volume $V=Tp=\Theta(n)$.
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning the $\Theta(n)$ stored bits once per transform level over $\Theta(\log n)$ levels already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so beating $n\log n$ requires saving on both data movement and butterfly arithmetic.
  - relies on: integer-multiplication-conjecture, multiplication-algorithm, time-accounting, address-chunk-interchange, fast-butterfly-layer, multitape-tm, big-o
  - **S1** (infer, page `integer-multiplication-conjecture`): To refute the conjecture in the fixed-tape model it suffices to exhibit one machine $A$ with $\liminf_{n\to\infty}T_A(n)/(n\lg n)=0$, and a bound $T_A(n)=O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa>0$ makes this ratio tend to zero through all input lengths.
    - relies on: big-o, multitape-tm, logarithms-and-iterated-logarithm-notation, big-omega-theta-and-little-o-notation
    - quotes line 386: "The power saving proved here gives a limit of zero through all input lengths, which is a stronger conclusion."
