# Plan A of pair Q12

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps. Its step ids are this plan's own.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): One deterministic Turing machine with a fixed finite alphabet and a fixed finite number of one-dimensional tapes outputs $\operatorname{bin}_{2n}(\operatorname{val}(x)\operatorname{val}(y))$ on every input $x\#y$ with $x,y\in\{0,1\}^n$, $n\ge1$, in worst-case time $T_A(n)=O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, which refutes an eventual $\Omega(n\lg n)$ lower bound for multiplication in this model.
  - method: Reduce the product to a convolution of radix-$2^b$ digits, carry that convolution through exact changes of problem (Chinese-remainder axes, Gaussian resampling with chirps, a phase twist into $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$) that end in synthetic transforms, make the address movement and butterfly layers of those transforms cheaper with two recursive tape procedures built on fixed linear networks with a rank deficit, then recover the integer product exactly and sum all costs divided by the volume $Tp$.
  - motivation (explicit): A transform-based multiplier scans $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ levels, so going below $n\log n$ requires both the data movement and the butterfly arithmetic of a level to cost less than a full scan; the two network-based tape procedures supply exactly those two savings.
  - relies on: time-bound, multiplication-algorithm, schonhage-strassen-conjecture, multitape-turing-machine
