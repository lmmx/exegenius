The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps. Its step ids are this plan's own.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ worst-case time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: Reduce the product to a convolution of short integer coefficients, evaluate the convolution through power-of-two transforms over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$, and replace the two tape operations that each cost a factor $\log n$ (address rearrangement and butterfly layers) by procedures whose cost per bit is a power of their parameter below one; then total the costs.
  - motivation (explicit): The sources note that scanning $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so both movement and arithmetic need savings.
  - relies on: multitape-tm, big-o, logarithms-and-iterated-logarithm-notation, integer-multiplication-conjecture
  - **S1** (infer, page `integer-multiplication-conjecture`): Setting: Schönhage and Strassen proposed $n\log n$ as the optimal order of multitape-Turing-machine time for multiplying two $n$-bit integers, Harvey and van der Hoeven reached $O(n\log n)$ from above, and a strict power saving in the logarithm is obtained in the same fixed finite-tape model.
    - relies on: multitape-tm, big-o, logarithms-and-iterated-logarithm-notation
    - quotes line 41: "machine, and proposed $n\log n$ as the optimal order of growth"
