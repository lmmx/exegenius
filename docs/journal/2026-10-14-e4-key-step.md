# 2026-10-14: E4, asking the argument task for the key step

**The result, by the preregistered rule: not supported, on one of three criteria.**
- **The central count rose in 4 of 5 pairs**, with one tie. The median paired difference was +3 steps. The plan reports 4 of 5 as suggestive (exact one-sided sign test, p = 0.19), not distinguished.
- **The variant plans made 2 major errors in total, the baseline 0.** Both are in one plan (V1), inside the derivation the instruction asked for. The rule required the variant to make no more major errors or wrong statements than the baseline, so it fails.

**What the measures show beyond the rule.** The instruction changes plans a great deal:
- **Depth:** core results derived rose in 5 of 5 pairs (median +6). Faithful derivations also rose in 5 of 5 (median +5), each p = 0.031.
- **The central gate:** 3 of 5 variant plans passed it, against 0 of 5 baseline plans. Sonnet at high effort had never passed it before (E3: 0 of 5).
- **Rank as dimension change (C4)** was reconstructed in 3 of 5 variant plans, against 0 of 5.

These are secondary measures. They do not override the rule; they say what a confirmation should test.

**The cost:** about 1.6 times the output and the seconds of the argument task.

**The decision.** No change to the argument task on `master`. The instruction is the most effective intervention on plan depth measured so far. Its risk is errors in the derivations it elicits. The next test is a confirmation with an error check (below), and with E5's model comparison in hand, since the two may combine.

## Design (preregistered: `calibration/plans/E4.md`, ec4a2b6)

**The pairs:**
- **Source:** each of E1's high-effort projects P1–P5 (`exegenius-planner`, Sonnet, high), two copies, each reset to just before the argument task.
- **B1–B5:** the argument task rendered by the baseline binary (master af36d6d).
- **V1–V5:** the same task rendered by the variant (`131026-exp-key-step`, 0c29556).
- **What differs:** in each pair the task files differ only in the key-step paragraph and the project directory's path, checked by diff.

**The subagents:** each task went word for word to a fresh `general-purpose` subagent, `model: "sonnet"`, `effort: "high"`, run from the development session.

**Contamination guard:** E1's original argument answers and its write tasks, which embed E1's plans, were moved out of the copies before any run.

**The evidence:** wiki branch `141026-exp-e4-key-step` (2032c37): the ten runs and all 18 argument transcripts.

**Judging, batch J5** (`2026-10-14-e4-key-step/judge/`):
- **Plans:** 12, blind, shuffled with seed 20261019. The anchors are E1-P4 and E3-M3.
- **Instructions:** J3's, unchanged, with the content judge and the central rubric (`calibration/rubrics/nlogn-central-saving.md`, f9ecb08).
- **Second judgements:** three, drawn by seed. They fell on V4, B3 and B2.

**The analysis:** `calibration/tools/paired.py`, new here. It holds the per-pair differences, the exact sign test, and the per-step and gate counts.

**Deviations:**
- **The rate limit.** Eight of the ten first attempts (all but B2 and B3) were killed by the account's five-hour rate limit (HTTP 429) before submitting. After the limit reset, each was retried with the same prompt and parameters by a fresh subagent. B2's subagent also hit the limit, after its answer had been accepted.
- **Reuse of a killed attempt's files.** Only one killed attempt (B4) had written files. They were a quote-checking script and a list of candidate quotes in its task's scratch directory, and the retry read and used them. B4 is a baseline run, so any advantage went to the baseline. No other retry found earlier files.
- **The orchestrator differs from E1's runner.** It is the development session (Opus, high) against E1's runner session, and the subagents had this session's permissions, not the wiki's `.claude/settings.json`. The orchestrator only passed prompts.

**The two major errors** (V1, in the derivation of the saving):
1. **The number of decreasing edges.** V1 says only one central-wire edge per invocation loses dimension. In the paper, each of the invocation's $c$ central wires loses $h$ on its edge from time 3 to 4, so $L=Ich$ (lines 997–1003, 1070–1071). With one such edge, $L/N$ would not be $100/539$.
2. **The source edges.** V1 says each edge's rank is its change in label dimension. It leaves out the $N$ source edges: each has matrix $2P_{U_a}$ of rank one but no change in dimension. That is why the budget is $Wm-N+2L$, not $Wm-2N+2L$ (lines 1117–1118, 1162–1171).

Both are errors a plan can only make by going into the counting core. Baseline plans that only mention these steps cannot be wrong about them. The criterion counts errors, not errors per claim, so it penalises depth. That was intended: an error in a plan reaches every page written from it.

## Interpretation, not shown

- **What the instruction does.** It makes Sonnet at high effort develop the derivation it was asked for, and with it most of the plan: derived and faithful counts rose in every pair, not only on the central chain. It is a general instruction that does not name this paper. It produced the first Sonnet plans to pass the central gate.
- **The remaining step.** C4, rank as dimension change with the $N$ rank-one exceptions, is the step that decides the gate. It is also where V1 went wrong.
- **Depth exposes errors.** One plan in five erred in the derivation the instruction elicited. Five plans cannot estimate how often that happens. The confirmation should measure it directly.
