# Plan P08

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-result`): A deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time, $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: Reduce multiplication to convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ evaluated by synthetic transforms, and speed up the two bottleneck tape operations (address interchange, butterfly layers) by a recursive power saving
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so savings are needed in both data movement and arithmetic.
  - relies on: nlogn-conjecture, multitape-tm, binary-representation-of-integers, asymptotic-constants-and-small-epsilon-exponents
  - **S1** (infer, page `nlogn-conjecture`): Schönhage and Strassen proposed $n\log n$ as optimal and Harvey and van der Hoeven reached $O(n\log n)$; the goal is a power saving below it.
    - relies on: big-o, karatsuba-and-schoolbook-multiplication
    - quotes line 43: "Harvey and van der Hoeven reached the unconditional $O(n\log n)$ upper"
  - **S2** (show): Address interchange and simultaneous butterfly layers can be done with a power saving in the logarithmic factor.
    - method: Build fixed linear networks with frames whose total number of smaller calls satisfies $s<Wm$, then recurse
    - motivation (recoverable): Both procedures arise from their own fixed linear network, and a strict inequality between calls and roles gives a power rather than linear growth.
    - **S2.1** (infer, page `linear-networks-frames`): A fixed linear network exchanging two banks of values and restoring auxiliary values lets an array gate act pointwise, with frames cancelling so the endpoint operator is the desired one.
      - relies on: array-streams, matrices-linear-maps-and-rank, permutations, linear-time-scanning-of-tapes
      - quotes line 643: "We construct two fixed linear networks that exchange two banks of values"
    - **S2.2** (infer, page `subset-networks`): Subspaces from three-element subsets, gather, scatter and scratch wires make the total number $s$ of smaller calls less than $Wm$.
      - relies on: linear-networks-frames, sets-products-and-tuples, matrices-linear-maps-and-rank
      - quotes line 853: "The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing"
    - **S2.3** (infer, page `recursive-saving`): Since $s<Wm$, a recursion with $s$ calls on volume $V/W$ costs $O(V\cdot\text{parameter}^{\tau})$ with $\tau<1$ (explicitly $\tau=1-2^{-50}$).
      - relies on: subset-networks, recurrences-and-divide-and-conquer, logarithms-and-exponents, asymptotic-constants-and-small-epsilon-exponents
      - quotes line 1331: "Both exact deficits displayed above are greater than $20\cdot2^{-50}$."
    - **S2.4** (show, page `address-interchange`): Two disjoint address fields of $u$ bits in an array of volume $V$ can be interchanged in time $O(Vu^\tau)$, $\tau<1$.
      - method: Realize the bit network's edge operators as address shears done in tape order, then recurse with the saving
      - motivation (recoverable): Edge operators must be done in tape order at cost scaling with rank, so that rank sums below $Wm$ give a power saving.
      - relies on: array-streams, linear-networks-frames, subset-networks, recursive-saving, permutations
      - **S2.4.1** (infer, page `lower-triangular-shear`): Any rational matrix factors as lower triangular times partial permutation times lower triangular, so address shears are done in tape order at cost scaling with rank.
        - relies on: matrices-linear-maps-and-rank, modular-arithmetic-and-residue-rings, linear-time-scanning-of-tapes
        - quotes line 1360: "is the Bruhat factorization for arbitrary matrices, with coordinate order"
      - **S2.4.2** (infer): Chunks of $e$ digits are interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time.
        - relies on: recursive-saving
        - quotes line 1486: "can be interchanged in $O(Ve^\tau)$ time, preserving every"
    - **S2.5** (infer, page `fast-butterfly-layers`): Simultaneous butterfly layers on same bit position of up to $d$ address chunks cost $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with $\lambda'<1$, instead of $d$ whole-array passes.
      - relies on: butterfly, ring-ymr, fixed-point-exactness, linear-networks-frames, subset-networks, recursive-saving, address-interchange, complex-numbers, floating-point-and-rounding-errors
      - quotes line 1792: "We will obtain cost $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, with"
  - **S3** (show): Convolution over $\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ can be computed by synthetic transforms and exact packed polynomial products.
    - method: Evaluate synthetic Fourier transforms by fast butterfly layers and multiply entries by signed packing
    - motivation (explicit): Powers of $y$ act as roots of unity so root multiplications are signed shifts, leaving only butterflies and polynomial products.
    - **S3.1** (infer, page `synthetic-transforms`): Synthetic transforms over the ring use powers of $y$ as roots of unity, with butterfly layers and a retained frequency order.
      - relies on: ring-ymr, butterfly, dft-convolution, fast-butterfly-layers, address-interchange
      - quotes line 2764: "where the required roots of unity act by exact signed permutations"
    - **S3.2** (infer, page `signed-packing`): Ring polynomials are multiplied exactly by signed Kronecker packing into the Harvey–van der Hoeven integer multiplier.
      - relies on: hh-multiplier, ring-ymr, fixed-point-exactness, binary-representation-of-integers
      - quotes line 3217: "reduction is a signed form of Kronecker substitution; compare"
  - **S4** (show, page `multiplication-algorithm`): The integer product is obtained from the convolution over the ring by a pipeline of reductions with controlled error.
    - method: Chain radix-digit convolution, prime axes, Gaussian resampling, chirp convolution and phase twist, then rescale, round and carry
    - motivation (recoverable): Each reduction changes the problem into one nearer to power-of-two axes where synthetic transforms apply.
    - relies on: synthetic-transforms, signed-packing, ring-ymr, fixed-point-exactness, hh-multiplier, nlogn-conjecture
    - **S4.1** (infer, page `radix-convolution`, reported out of order): Splitting into radix digits turns integer multiplication into convolution of short integer coefficient lists.
      - relies on: dft-convolution, binary-representation-of-integers
      - quotes line 4489: "Read each stored operand backward from"
    - **S4.2** (infer, page `crt-prime-axes`, reported out of order): A Chinese-remainder map places the coefficient list on distinct prime cyclic axes, preserving convolution.
      - relies on: prime-numbers-and-coprimality, modular-arithmetic-and-residue-rings, radix-convolution
      - quotes line 4199: "theorem to convert one cyclic convolution into multidimensional cyclic"
    - **S4.3** (infer, page `gaussian-resampling`): Gaussian resampling expresses each Fourier transform through a power-of-two transform, keeping the two permutations in the formula.
      - relies on: dft-convolution, crt-prime-axes, permutations, fixed-point-exactness
      - quotes line 3548: "[Resampling without executing the two permutations]"
    - **S4.4** (infer, page `bluestein-chirp`): Bluestein's chirp identity converts the middle transform with its permutation into cyclic convolution, and the retained permutations cancel.
      - relies on: dft-convolution, permutations
      - quotes line 3911: "[Chirp and permutation cancellation]"
    - **S4.5** (infer): Error stays below $1/2$ so rounding recovers the coefficients exactly.
      - relies on: fixed-point-exactness, floating-point-and-rounding-errors
      - quotes line 4546: "\subsection{Precision and exact recovery}"
    - **S4.6** (infer): With compatible parameters every term of the cost table is within $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$.
      - relies on: address-interchange, fast-butterfly-layers, big-o
      - quotes line 4810: "For the cost calculation put $V=Tp=\Theta(n)$."
