# Plan P01

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `target`): Two $n$-bit integers can be multiplied exactly by a deterministic multitape machine in time $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: reduce multiplication through a chain of convolution problems down to polynomial-ring convolution, evaluated with the fast tape procedures
  - motivation (recoverable): The standard transform-based approach costs n log n at best; the chain of reductions puts the problem in a form where transforms are made only of address moves, shifts and butterflies, which the two fast procedures speed up by a power of log n.
  - relies on: multitape-model, nlogn-conjecture, convolution, big-o, binary-representation-and-bit-operations, floors-ceilings-and-logarithms, asymptotic-notation-beyond-big-o
  - **S1** (show): Two fast tape procedures exist: interchange of two address fields of $u$ bits in time $O(Vu^\tau)$ and a simultaneous butterfly layer in time $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$, with $\tau,\lambda'<1$ and $V$ the stored bit volume.
    - method: construct each procedure from a linear network and extract a power saving
    - motivation (explicit): Scanning the array at each of the Theta(log n) transform levels already costs n log n, so savings are needed in both movement and arithmetic.
    - relies on: turing-machine
    - **S1.1** (show, page `address-interchange`): Address interchange of two $u$-bit fields of an array of stored bit volume $V$ costs $O(Vu^\tau)$ for a fixed $\tau<1$, using XOR combinations of data.
      - method: build a fixed linear network with frames, restore auxiliaries, and recurse on a strict call-count inequality
      - motivation (recoverable): A direct scan costs a full pass per level; a network whose edges are smaller calls of the same procedure lets a strict inequality on call counts compound into a power saving.
      - relies on: tape-streams
      - **S1.1.1** (infer, page `linear-network`): A fixed linear network exchanges two banks of values; wires are roles and each gate has an invertible frame, so intermediate frames cancel and leave the endpoint operator on each of the $W$ role arrays.
        - relies on: matrices-invertibility-and-linear-maps, tape-streams
        - quotes line 643: "We construct two fixed linear networks that exchange two banks of values"
      - **S1.1.2** (infer, page `restored-auxiliaries`): Auxiliary scalar values are subtracted, used and added back, so the result is exact for any initial auxiliary contents.
        - relies on: linear-network
        - quotes line 729: "For arbitrary initial values of its auxiliary wires, a forward"
      - **S1.1.4** (infer, page `subset-network`): Subspaces of a fixed $m$-dimensional space attached to gates, built from three-element subsets with gather, scatter and auxiliary wires, make the total number $s$ of smaller calls satisfy $s<Wm$.
        - relies on: vector-spaces-subspaces-and-dimension, linear-network, restored-auxiliaries
        - quotes line 847: "The scalar network already has the required signed exchange. We now"
      - **S1.1.3** (infer, page `address-shear`): The endpoint operator for the bit procedure is the address shear $(H,D)\mapsto(H+D,D)$, which with two linear-cost updates completes an interchange.
        - relies on: matrices-invertibility-and-linear-maps, modular-arithmetic-and-congruences, linear-network
        - quotes line 1366: "Every rational $m\times m$ matrix $A$ of rank $a$ has a factorization"
      - **S1.1.5** (infer, page `recursive-saving`): Since $s/W<m$, multiplying the parameter by $m$ raises the per-volume cost by less than a factor $m$, so the cost is the volume times a power of the parameter below 1.
        - relies on: mathematical-induction-and-recurrences, subset-network, address-shear
        - quotes line 1481: "Choose a rational number $\tau\in(0,1)$ with $s/W<m^\tau$."
    - **S1.2** (infer, page `fast-butterfly-layer`): Applying $H_0$ simultaneously at the same bit position in $d$ address chunks of the numerical arrays costs $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ with $\lambda'<1$, with a single truncation after the layer.
      - relies on: butterfly, fixed-point-precision, address-interchange, linear-network, recursive-saving
      - quotes line 2588: "Simultaneous normalized butterfly layer"
  - **S2** (infer, page `crt-address-map`, reported out of order): The radix-digit coefficient list is placed on a product of distinct prime cyclic axes by a Chinese-remainder map that preserves convolution.
    - relies on: convolution, prime-numbers-and-coprimality, modular-arithmetic-and-congruences, cyclic-and-negacyclic-indexing, cartesian-products-and-index-tuples
    - quotes line 4198: "Agarwal and Cooley used the Chinese remainder"
  - **S3** (infer, page `gaussian-resampling`): Gaussian resampling expresses each source Fourier transform through a power-of-two tensor transform, with source and target permutations retained in the identity rather than executed as sorts.
    - relies on: fourier-transform, multidimensional-transform, permutations-and-bijections
    - quotes line 3548: "Resampling without executing the two permutations"
  - **S4** (infer, page `bluestein-chirp`): Bluestein's chirp identity converts the middle transform, including its target permutation, into normalized cyclic convolution.
    - relies on: fourier-transform, cyclic-and-negacyclic-indexing
    - quotes line 3911: "Chirp and permutation cancellation"
  - **S5** (infer, page `phase-twist`): A phase twist turns the last cyclic coordinate into a polynomial modulo $y^r+1$ whose negative wrap gives the original cyclic wrap after untwisting.
    - relies on: polynomial-ring, cyclic-and-negacyclic-indexing
    - quotes line 4301: "obtained from the synthetic convolution by a coefficient twist."
  - **S6** (show): Convolution over the polynomial ring on the remaining axes is computed with synthetic transforms in retained frequency order and packed polynomial products.
    - method: use synthetic transforms for the evaluation and signed packing for the pointwise products
    - motivation (recoverable): Powers of y act as roots of unity so root multiplications are signed shifts, and the pointwise polynomial products reduce to integer multiplication.
    - relies on: synthetic-transforms, polynomial-ring
    - **S6.1** (infer, page `transform-layout`, reported out of order): The two forward synthetic transforms retain the same frequency order through their pointwise product, and the opposite transform consumes that order.
      - relies on: synthetic-transforms, polynomial-ring, address-interchange, fast-butterfly-layer
      - quotes line 2929: "Transform layout and cost"
    - **S6.2** (infer, page `signed-packing`, reported out of order): Polynomial products are computed by signed packing into one integer and the established $O(n\log n)$ multiplier as an ordinary subroutine.
      - relies on: polynomial-ring, nlogn-conjecture
      - quotes line 3221: "Signed packing and normalized ring multiplication"
  - **S7** (infer, page `carry-propagation`): Known normalizations and digit scales are removed, the recovered coefficient error is below $1/2$ so rounding is exact, and carries yield the exact binary product.
    - relies on: fixed-point-precision, dyadic-rationals-and-fixed-point-numbers, sup-norm-and-rounding-error-bounds, schoolbook-multiplication-and-linear-time-addition, convolution
    - quotes line 4771: "To propagate carries, let $Q_0=2^b$."
