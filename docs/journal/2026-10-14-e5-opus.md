# 2026-10-14: E5, Opus at medium effort against Sonnet at high, for planning

**The result: Opus at medium is not preferred.** By the preregistered rule, five fresh Opus-medium plans did not beat E1's five Sonnet-high plans on the central derivation:
- **Central count:** median 6 against 5 (exact one-sided Mann–Whitney p = 0.33).
- **The central gate:** 0 of 5 Opus plans passed it, against 0 of 5 Sonnet plans.

Opus at medium also derived fewer core results (median 6 against 11) and passed the Technical calibre in 0 of 5 plans, against 2 of 5. Its plans are cheaper in output and time.

**S1's screening signal did not hold.** S1's two Opus-medium plans passed the gate once and reconstructed all of C3–C6 once. None of these five reconstructs C4, rank as dimension change. This is what screening is for (`calibration/PROTOCOL.md` §6): two runs advance an arm, and only an estimate decides.

**The decision,** as preregistered: report, and stop spending on Opus for planning this paper. The planning default stays Sonnet at high effort. The strongest lever measured on the central derivation remains the key-step instruction (E4).

## Design (preregistered: `calibration/plans/E5.md`, b03e7a3)

**The Opus arm:**
- **Runs:** N1–N5, fresh planning-only runs with E1's configuration, byte for byte, and the same paper.
- **Binary:** af36d6d, which differs from E1's cc70355 only in a status message (`git diff --stat`: `main.rs`, 4 lines).
- **The subagents:** each task's prompt as `exegenius next` printed it went word for word to a fresh `general-purpose` subagent with `model: "opus"`, `effort: "medium"`. The development session did the orchestrating.
- **Recorded:** every one of the 15 subagents ran on `claude-opus-5-5` at `medium` on every turn. There were no restarts, and every run stopped when `next` handed out write tasks.

**The comparison arm:** E1's G1–G5 (`general-purpose`, Sonnet, high, binary cc70355), re-judged.

**The evidence:** wiki branch `141026-exp-e5-opus` (904a3c6), with runs and transcripts.

**Judging, batch J6** (`2026-10-14-e5-opus/judge/`):
- **Plans:** 12, blind, shuffled with seed 20261020. The anchors are E1-P4 and E3-M3.
- **Instructions:** J5's, unchanged, which are J3's.
- **Second judgements:** three, drawn by seed. They fell on G5, M3 and P4.

**Deviations:**
- **The orchestrator:** the development session (Opus, high) with its own permissions, against E1's runner. It only passed prompts.
- **Timing:** the arms ran a day apart.

## Interpretation, not shown

- **A stronger model at lower effort does not buy the central derivation on this task.** Opus at medium wrote shorter arguments, about a third of Sonnet-high's output, and developed fewer derivations. Its plans name the chain's steps (stated 17–19) but rarely carry the counting core. The argument task's own instruction lets deeper steps stop at an `infer` quote.
- **What the S1 screen had suggested.** It suggested that the counting core needs model capability. With these five plans, Opus at medium reaches C4 in 1 of 7 plans overall (S1's O1–O2, and N1–N5), against 3 of 5 for Sonnet-high plans given the key-step instruction (E4).
- **The evidence now points at the task's instructions, not the model,** for this gap. This is a hypothesis drawn across batches and arm designs, not a measured comparison.
- **One confound in the S1 comparison.** S1 used `exegenius-planner` and E5 `general-purpose`. E1 found no definition effect at Sonnet high. None has been measured at Opus.
