A fixed-point Gaussian dyadic record is the number format in which the transform layers keep their polynomial data, chosen so that a whole layer is computed without rounding and rounded once at its end.

A Gaussian dyadic is a complex number whose real and imaginary parts are integer multiples of a negative power of two. Let $p$ be the number of fractional bits, so stored values lie on the grid $2^{-p}\mathbb Z[i]$. Each record is a polynomial in the [Polynomial ring modulo y^r + 1](ring-y.md); each of its $r$ coefficients is stored as two signed two's-complement integers, one per real and imaginary part, each read as the integer divided by $2^p$. A record is laid out on the tapes of the [Fixed finite-tape machine model](machine-model.md) as $2r$ consecutive fixed-width integers, so it is read and moved by ordinary tape scans.

Inside a layer, the operations are additions, subtractions, multiplications by $\pm1$ and $\pm i$, and halving, as in $H_0(u,v)=((u+v)/2,(u-v)/2)$. The layer's inputs are disk-valued: each coefficient lies in $2^{-p}\mathbb Z[i]$ and has modulus at most $1$. Let a value computed with $k$ operations in sequence since the layer began be said to have depth $k$. A value of depth $k$ lies in $2^{-(p+k)}\mathbb Z[i]$ and has modulus at most $2^k$. This follows by induction on $k$. At depth $0$ the value is an input, so the claim is the disk-valued hypothesis. Addition or subtraction of values of depths at most $k$ gives a value of depth at most $k+1$, and at most doubles the largest modulus. Halving adds at most one denominator bit. Signs and multiplication by $i$ change neither bound. For example, with $p=2$ and $u=\tfrac34$, the value $u/2=\tfrac38$ needs one more fractional bit than the grid, and loses nothing if that bit is kept.

So the components can be given room in advance. Let $\Delta$ be the number of extra bits reserved, at least the maximal depth reached in the layer. Reserving $p+\Delta$ fractional bits and $\Delta+3$ integer and sign bits per real component represents every intermediate coefficient exactly <cite data-source="paper">represents every intermediate coefficient exactly</cite>, because the denominator and modulus bounds above then hold at every depth. The parameters keep $\Delta$ small compared with $p$, so each component still has $O(p)$ bits. The intermediate values are exact Gaussian dyadics <cite data-source="paper">Intermediate values are exact Gaussian dyadics, whose real and imaginary parts have power-of-two denominators.</cite>

Only after the completed layer is one truncation $Q_p$ applied <cite data-source="paper">A single truncation follows the completed contractive layer.</cite> The truncation $Q_p$ drops the bits below $2^{-p}$ in each real and imaginary part, returning the record to the $p$-bit grid, with error below $\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ per complex coefficient. Rounding at every step would add one such error per step; here there is one per layer.
