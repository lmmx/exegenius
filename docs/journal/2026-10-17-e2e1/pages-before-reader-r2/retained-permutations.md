Retained permutation cancellation computes the prime-length Fourier transforms of the integer product through power-of-two ones without executing any coordinate permutation as a sort: the permutations of Gaussian resampling stay inside the transform identity, where one is absorbed by a convolution and the other cancels.

Each prime-length transform is computed through a power-of-two one by [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md). Its usual implementation sorts twice on each coordinate. A tape machine cannot jump to an arbitrary position, so it carries out a permutation of records by labelling each with its destination and sorting, which costs a $\log$ factor beyond a linear pass: each sort costs $O(tp\log t)$ <cite data-source="paper">each costing $O(tp\log t)$</cite> per line. A sort on every line would undo the saving of a power of $\log n$, so the permutations stay inside the formula.

## One axis, then all axes

Take $d$ axes with pairwise coprime prime lengths $s_i$ and power-of-two lengths $t_i>s_i$. Write $\boldsymbol s=(s_1,\dots,s_d)$, $\boldsymbol t=(t_1,\dots,t_d)$, $S=\prod_is_i$ (the number of entries of the source array) and $T=\prod_it_i$ (the number of entries of the padded box). For the normalized [discrete Fourier transform](dft.md), $(F_qz)_k=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}z_je^{-2\pi ijk/q}$; $F_q^+$, the opposite transform, reverses the sign of the exponent, and $F_q^+F_q=I/q$. For axis lengths $\boldsymbol q$, $F_{\boldsymbol q}=\bigotimes_iF_{q_i}$ applies $F_{q_i}$ along every line of axis $i$, as in [multidimensional transforms](multidim-transform.md), and likewise $F_{\boldsymbol q}^+$. Hence $F_{\boldsymbol s}^+F_{\boldsymbol s}=I/S$.

For one axis, with $\alpha$ and $\delta$ as on the resampling page, let $(P_su)_j=u_{tj}$ and $(P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}$, where $u$ and $v$ are complex arrays here, not the integers being multiplied. The resampling page gives linear contractions $A$ and $B_0$, the input and output maps ($B_0$ is unrelated to the packing base of [signed packing](signed-packing.md)), with $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$, computed in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ without executing $P_s$ or $P_t$.

Operators on different axes touch different indices, so $\bigl(\bigotimes_iX_i\bigr)\bigl(\bigotimes_iY_i\bigr)=\bigotimes_iX_iY_i$. Put $R=\bigotimes_iP_{s_i}$ (the source permutation, not a ring), $Q=\bigotimes_iP_{t_i}$, $\mathcal A=\bigotimes_iA_i$, $\mathcal B_0=\bigotimes_iB_{0,i}$. Tensoring the one-axis identity gives $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ with $\gamma=2d\alpha^2$ <cite data-source="paper">=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A,</cite>. Both maps are applied one line at a time, so errors and times add over the $d$ axes: error below $dp^2$, total time $O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ with a constant independent of $d$ <cite data-source="paper">$O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$, with a constant independent of $d$.</cite>

The [fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md) makes each axis the innermost field before its lines are processed. With $\ell$ the number of address bits of an axis of length $r=2^\ell$, $\tau<1$ the interchange exponent, each $t_i\in\{r/2,r\}$ and each entry of $O(p)$ bits, exposing and restoring all lines costs $O\bigl(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau)\bigr)$ <cite data-source="paper">then exposing and restoring all the lines costs</cite>. Since $t_i\ge r/2$, each tensor map invokes at most $\sum_iT/t_i\le 2dT/r$ lines, so a setup of $O(p^\nu)$ per line, with $\nu$ a fixed exponent, totals $O(dTp^\nu/r)$. Divided by $Tp$ this is $O(dp^{\nu-1}/r)\to0$ when $r$ outgrows every power of $p$ and $d$ is polynomially bounded in $p$, so setup is $o(Tp)$.

## Absorbing $Q$

The target permutation $Q$ sits between $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B_0$ and is absorbed by the [Bluestein chirp identity](chirp.md). $Q$ becomes part of a convolution formula, so no step reorders the array. For even $t_i$ and $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_is_ij_i^2/t_i)$ one has $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot\bigl(a*(\overline a\cdot u)\bigr)/T$, where $*$ is the [cyclic convolution](convolution.md) of length $T$ and a dot is coordinatewise multiplication.

## Cancelling $R$

Conjugating input and output turns a routine for $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$ into one for $RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+$, since conjugation flips the exponent's sign and commutes with the real permutation $R$. So both the forward and the opposite transform are available only in the form with $R$ in front. The product then reads $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+)[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)]=u*v/S^2$, with no $R$ left to execute.

For a unit $\beta$ modulo $q$ let $(R_\beta z)_j=z_{\beta j}$. Then $(F_q^+R_\beta z)_j=\frac1q\sum_w z_{\beta w}e^{2\pi i wj/q}$, with $w$ the summation index. Substituting $w'=\beta w$ gives $\frac1q\sum_{w'}z_{w'}e^{2\pi i w'\beta^{-1}j/q}=(R_\beta^{-1}F_q^+z)_j$, so $F_q^+R_\beta=R_\beta^{-1}F_q^+$. Each $t_i$ is a unit modulo $s_i$ and $R$ applies $R_{t_i}$ on axis $i$, so tensoring gives $RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+R=F_{\boldsymbol s}^+$. A permutation commutes with pointwise products, so the left side of the product identity equals $F_{\boldsymbol s}^+(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$. The normalized convolution rule is $F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=S(F_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$, and $F_{\boldsymbol s}^+F_{\boldsymbol s}=I/S$, so this equals $\frac1SF_{\boldsymbol s}^+F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=u*v/S^2$.
