Retained permutation cancellation computes the prime-length Fourier transforms of the integer product through power-of-two ones without executing any coordinate permutation as a sort: the permutations of Gaussian resampling stay inside the transform identity, where one is absorbed by a convolution and the other cancels.

Take $d$ axes with pairwise coprime prime lengths $s_i$ and power-of-two lengths $t_i>s_i$ (so $\gcd(s_i,t_i)=1$ for odd $s_i$). Write $\boldsymbol s=(s_1,\dots,s_d)$, $\boldsymbol t=(t_1,\dots,t_d)$, $S=\prod_is_i$, $T=\prod_it_i$. For operators $X_i$ acting on vectors of length $q_i$, $\bigotimes_iX_i$ applies $X_i$ along every line of axis $i$. Operators on different axes touch different indices, so $\bigl(\bigotimes_iX_i\bigr)\bigl(\bigotimes_iY_i\bigr)=\bigotimes_iX_iY_i$ for further operators $Y_i$.

For a vector $z$ of $q$ entries, the normalized [discrete Fourier transform](dft.md) is $(F_qz)_k=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}z_je^{-2\pi ijk/q}$, and $F_q^+$ is the same with the exponent's sign reversed: $(F_q^+z)_k=\frac1q\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}z_je^{2\pi ijk/q}$. Since $\sum_k e^{2\pi i k(j-j')/q}$ is $q$ if $j=j'$ and $0$ otherwise, $F_q^+F_q=I/q$. For a tuple $\boldsymbol q$ of axis lengths, $F_{\boldsymbol q}=\bigotimes_iF_{q_i}$ and $F_{\boldsymbol q}^+=\bigotimes_iF_{q_i}^+$, so $F_{\boldsymbol s}^+F_{\boldsymbol s}=I/S$.

Each prime-length transform is computed through a power-of-two one by [Gaussian resampling](gaussian-resampling.md), whose usual implementation sorts twice on each coordinate <cite data-source="paper">Its usual implementation sorts twice on each</cite>. For one axis, with $p$ and $\alpha$ as there and $\delta$ the fixed exponent in its time bound, let $(P_su)_j=u_{tj}$ and $(P_tv)_k=v_{-sk}$ ($u,v$ are complex arrays here, not integers). That page gives linear contractions $A$, $B_0$ with $P_sF_s=2^{2\alpha^2}B_0P_tF_tA$, computed in time $O(tp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ without executing $P_s$ or $P_t$.

Tensor this identity over the axes. Put $R=\bigotimes_iP_{s_i}$ (a permutation, not a ring), $Q=\bigotimes_iP_{t_i}$, $\mathcal A=\bigotimes_iA_i$, $\mathcal B_0=\bigotimes_iB_{0,i}$. By the product rule the left sides combine to $RF_{\boldsymbol s}$, and the right sides to $\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ times $d$ factors $2^{2\alpha^2}$, so $RF_{\boldsymbol s}=2^\gamma\mathcal B_0QF_{\boldsymbol t}\mathcal A$ with $\gamma=2d\alpha^2$. Both $\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal B_0$ are contractions, applied one line at a time in a padded box of $T$ entries. Errors and times add over the $d$ passes: error below $dp^2$, time $O(dTp^{3/2+\delta}\alpha)$ with a constant independent of $d$. Each axis is exposed as the innermost field by the [fast address-chunk interchange](address-interchange.md). With $t_i\in\{t_{\max}/2,t_{\max}\}$, $t_{\max}=2^\ell$, at most $2dT/t_{\max}$ lines occur, since $t_i\ge t_{\max}/2$, so setup $O(p^g)$ per line ($g$ a fixed exponent) totals $O(dTp^g/t_{\max})=o(Tp)$ when $t_{\max}$ outgrows every power of $p$ and $d$ is polynomially bounded in $p$. With entries of $O(p)$ bits, exposing and restoring all lines costs $O(Tp\,d^2(1+\ell^\tau))$ <cite data-source="paper">then exposing and restoring all the lines costs</cite>.

The target permutation $Q$ is absorbed by the [Bluestein chirp identity](chirp.md): for even $t_i$ and $a_{\boldsymbol j}=\exp(-\pi i\sum_is_ij_i^2/t_i)$, where $\boldsymbol j=(j_1,\dots,j_d)$ indexes the entries, $QF_{\boldsymbol t}u=\overline a\cdot\bigl(a*(\overline a\cdot u)\bigr)/T$, with $*$ the [cyclic convolution](convolution.md) and a dot coordinatewise multiplication. So $Q$ is part of a convolution of length $T$ and never executed.

The source permutation $R$ cancels: $(RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+)[(RF_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(RF_{\boldsymbol s}v)]=u*v/S^2$. For a unit $c$ modulo $q$, let $(R_cz)_j=z_{cj}$. Substituting $h=ck$ in the Fourier sum, with $h$ the summation index, gives $F_q^+R_c=R_c^{-1}F_q^+$. Each $t_i$ is a unit modulo $s_i$ by the coprimality assumed, and $R$ applies $R_{t_i}$ on axis $i$, so tensoring gives $RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+R=F_{\boldsymbol s}^+$. A permutation commutes with pointwise products, so the left side is $RF_{\boldsymbol s}^+R(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)=F_{\boldsymbol s}^+(F_{\boldsymbol s}u\cdot F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$. The normalized convolution rule is $F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=S(F_{\boldsymbol s}u)\cdot(F_{\boldsymbol s}v)$ and $F_{\boldsymbol s}^+F_{\boldsymbol s}=I/S$, so this equals $\frac1SF_{\boldsymbol s}^+F_{\boldsymbol s}(u*v)=u*v/S^2$.
