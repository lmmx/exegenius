Convolution from the retained transform layout computes the normalized convolution over $\mathcal R_r=\mathbb C[y]/(y^r+1)$ on the remaining axes: transform both operands, multiply records pointwise, transform back. It supplies the ring convolution that the multiplication algorithm needs, using three synthetic transforms and short polynomial products.

Arrays are indexed by $\prod_i\mathbb Z/t_i\mathbb Z$ over the $d-1$ remaining axes, where $t_i$ is the length of axis $i$, with values in $\mathcal R_r$; each value is a record of $r$ coefficients. Let $M=\prod_i t_i$ be the number of records and $T=rM$ the number of complex coefficients. $\|\cdot\|_\infty$ is the largest coefficient modulus in an array. A disk array has norm at most $1$; a disk grid array also has real and imaginary parts that are multiples of $2^{-p}$. Tuples $\boldsymbol j,\boldsymbol k,\boldsymbol l$ are positions in the cyclic axes, with components $j_i,k_i,l_i$. The convolution $f*g$ of [Cyclic convolution](convolution.md) adds the ring product $f(\boldsymbol k)g(\boldsymbol l)$ into position $\boldsymbol k+\boldsymbol l$, each coordinate taken modulo $t_i$.

## The normalized convolution

For arrays over $\mathcal R_r$ define $\mathcal M_r(f,g)=\frac{f*g}{rM}=\frac{f*g}{T}$. A fixed output coefficient averages $rM$ signed scalar products <cite data-source="paper">$rM$ signed scalar products, and $\mathcal M_r$ is a bilinear</cite>, so $\mathcal M_r$ is a bilinear contraction: $\|\mathcal M_r(f,g)\|_\infty\le\|f\|_\infty\|g\|_\infty$.

Let $F^-$ and $F^+$ be the forward and opposite synthetic transforms on all $d-1$ axes ([Synthetic polynomial transform](synthetic-transform.md)). Put $\omega_t=y^{2r/t}$, a power of $y$ that plays a $t$-th root of unity, and $\chi_{\boldsymbol j}(\boldsymbol k)=\prod_i\omega_{t_i}^{-j_ik_i}$. Then
$$(F^-f)(\boldsymbol j)=\frac1M\sum_{\boldsymbol k}\chi_{\boldsymbol j}(\boldsymbol k)f(\boldsymbol k),$$
and $F^+$ is the same with the exponent signs reversed. Since $\omega_{t_i}^{t_i}=1$, $\chi_{\boldsymbol j}(\boldsymbol k+\boldsymbol l)=\chi_{\boldsymbol j}(\boldsymbol k)\chi_{\boldsymbol j}(\boldsymbol l)$. Expanding $f*g$ as the sum of its products $f(\boldsymbol k)g(\boldsymbol l)$ placed at $\boldsymbol k+\boldsymbol l$, and using that the ring is commutative,
$$(F^-(f*g))(\boldsymbol j)=\frac1M\sum_{\boldsymbol k,\boldsymbol l}\chi_{\boldsymbol j}(\boldsymbol k)f(\boldsymbol k)\,\chi_{\boldsymbol j}(\boldsymbol l)g(\boldsymbol l)=\frac1M\bigl(M(F^-f)(\boldsymbol j)\bigr)\bigl(M(F^-g)(\boldsymbol j)\bigr).$$
So $F^-(f*g)=M(F^-f)(F^-g)$, the product taken record by record. The per-axis identity $F_t^+F_t^-=I/t$ of the synthetic-transform page, tensored over the axes, gives $F^+F^-=I/M$. Hence $F^+$ applied to both sides gives $\frac{f*g}{M}=MF^+\bigl((F^-f)(F^-g)\bigr)$, and dividing by $r$,
$$MF^+\Bigl(\frac{(F^-f)(F^-g)}r\Bigr)=\frac{f*g}{rM}=\mathcal M_r(f,g).$$
Write $P(u,v)=uv/r$ for the record-by-record product. Each coefficient of $uv$ is a signed sum of $r$ products of coefficients, so $P$ is bilinear and $\|P(u,v)\|_\infty\le\|u\|_\infty\|v\|_\infty$. Here $P$, $u$, $v$ denote arrays, which differs from their meanings on earlier pages.

## Keeping the stored order

The forward procedure does not store $F^-f$ in natural order. Let $B$ be the bit-reversal order in which the butterflies produce frequencies ([Retained frequency order](frequency-order.md)), and $L$ the layout permutation of records that the tape layout imposes. Both move whole records and leave each record's polynomial intact. The stored output is $LBF^-f$, with frequency $\boldsymbol j$ at position $LB\boldsymbol j$. The opposite procedure consumes that order; its exact counterpart is $\mathcal T_+=F^+B^{-1}L^{-1}$. The transforms are laid out as in [Tape layout for synthetic transforms](transform-layout.md), which makes each round one simultaneous layer. Perform both forward transforms and retain their common $LB$ order.

Since $P$ acts on each record separately, $P(LBu,LBv)=LBP(u,v)$. Because $\mathcal T_+LB=F^+$, applying $\mathcal T_+$ to the pointwise product of the stored forward outputs equals $F^+P(F^-f,F^-g)$, and scaling it by $M$ gives $\mathcal M_r(f,g)$. The procedure keeps the retained order of [Retained frequency order](frequency-order.md), passing the product to the opposite transform without reordering. Also $F^+$ averages signed shifts and $B^{-1}L^{-1}$ only permutes records, so $\mathcal T_+$ is a contraction.

## Cost

Each record product is formed by [Signed packing](signed-packing.md) in $O(rp\log(rp))$ time and truncated by $Q_p$ to the $2^{-p}$ grid, which keeps it in the disk. Each of the three transforms costs $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}])$, with $\ell=\log_2r$, $K$ the chunk width of the layout, and $\tau,\lambda'<1$ the exponents of the interchange and layer costs; the final scaling by $M$ is linear in $Tp$, and the $M=T/r$ pointwise products cost $O(Tp\log(rp))$ in total <cite data-source="paper">Their total cost is $O(Tp\log(rp))$</cite>. The sum is $O(Tp[dK+pK^{\tau-1}+\ell d^{\lambda'}+\log(rp)])$.

## Error

Put $\delta=\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$ for the error of each transform procedure and $\eta=\sqrt2\,2^{-p}$ for one truncation $Q_p$. Each transform procedure maps disk grid arrays to disk grid arrays with error less than $\delta$ <cite data-source="paper">$\sqrt2\ell\,2^{-p}$, returns disk grid coefficients in the same</cite>. Let $u,v$ be the exact stored forward outputs and $\widetilde u,\widetilde v$ the returned ones, all disk arrays. By bilinearity, $P(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)-P(u,v)=P(\widetilde u-u,\widetilde v)+P(u,\widetilde v-v)$. Each term is less than $\delta$ by the bound on $P$ and norm at most $1$ of $\widetilde v$ and $u$, so $\|P(\widetilde u,\widetilde v)-P(u,v)\|_\infty<2\delta$. With $\widetilde z=Q_p(P(\widetilde u,\widetilde v))$ and $z_*=P(u,v)$, truncation gives $\|\widetilde z-z_*\|_\infty<2\delta+\eta$. The opposite procedure returns $\widetilde h$ with $\|\widetilde h-\mathcal T_+\widetilde z\|_\infty<\delta$, and $\mathcal T_+$ is a contraction, so the triangle inequality gives
$$\|\widetilde h-\mathcal T_+z_*\|_\infty\le\|\widetilde h-\mathcal T_+\widetilde z\|_\infty+\|\mathcal T_+(\widetilde z-z_*)\|_\infty<3\delta+\eta.$$
The exact multiplication by $M$ gives total error less than $M(3\delta+\eta)=\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$ <cite data-source="paper">$M(3\delta+\eta)=\sqrt2\,M(3\ell+1)2^{-p}$, as claimed.</cite>
