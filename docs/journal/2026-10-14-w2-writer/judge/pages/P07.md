Subspace labels give each network vertex a subspace, so an edge costs its label dimension change. The saving comes from keeping the dimension lost on decreasing edges small relative to the number $N$ of data roles <cite data-source="paper">The saving will come from keeping the total dimension lost on decreasing</cite>.

The labels sit on vertices of the [linear exchange network with restored scratch wires](linear-network.md), which has $W$ wires, and price each edge by its change of frame, as in [frames on wire arrays](common-frame-principle.md). A nested edge's orthogonal residual, as in [rank, subspaces and orthogonal complements](linear-algebra-basics.md), has dimension equal to its rank.

Let $h=100$ be the size of the ground set $\{1,\dots,h\}$, $\mathcal T$ its 3-element subsets, and index data roles $X_a,Y_a$ by $a\in\mathcal T^3$, so $N=\binom h3^3$ counts the roles $X_a$. Bit network: $F=\mathbb Q^h$ with $\langle x,y\rangle=x^{\mathsf T}(I-J/9)y$ ($J$ all-ones); complex network: $F=\mathbb F_2^h$ with the dot product. With $t_T$ the indicator of $T$, over $\mathbb Q$
$$\langle t_S,t_T\rangle=|S\cap T|-1,\qquad \langle t_T,t_T\rangle=2,$$
<cite data-source="paper">\langle t_S,t_T\rangle=|S\cap T|-1,</cite> so each line $\langle t_T\rangle$ is nondegenerate and neighbors span orthogonal lines. Labels live in $\mathcal F=F^{\otimes3}$, of dimension $m=h^3$; let $u_a=t_{a_1}\otimes t_{a_2}\otimes t_{a_3}$, $U_a=\langle u_a\rangle$.

Terminals (source, sink): $(U_a,\mathcal F)$ for $X_a$, $(0,U_a^\perp)$ for $Y_a$, $(0,\mathcal F)$ for scratch roles. Source and sink dimensions total $N$ and $Wm-N$ <cite data-source="paper">The source dimensions sum to $N$ and the sink dimensions to $Wm-N$.</cite>

With $A=F^{\otimes(j-1)}$, $P=\langle t_{a_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes t_{a_{j-1}}\rangle$, $B=P^\perp\subset A$, and $t$ the indicator of the wire's $j$th triple, $X$ labels grow from $A\otimes\langle t\rangle$ to $A\otimes F$, $Y$ labels from $B\otimes\langle t\rangle$ to $(P\otimes\langle t\rangle)^\perp_{A\otimes F}$. Next, $A'=A\otimes F$, $P'=P\otimes\langle t\rangle$, $B'=(P')^\perp_{A'}$, so outgoing labels equal next incoming labels.

Only the central edge from time 3 to 4 decreases, losing $P\otimes F$ (dimension $h$) <cite data-source="paper">Label dimension is nondecreasing along every edge except center $3\to4$, which removes</cite>. With $K=3\binom h3^2$ invocations of $c$ central wires each, the dimension lost is $L=Kch$. Signed changes telescope at each gate to $Wm-2N$; absolute values add $2L$. As $L/N=c/539$ with $c=100$ (bit) or $101$ (complex), $L_{\rm b}/N=100/539$ and $L_{\rm c}/N=101/539$ <cite data-source="paper">\frac{L_{\rm b}}{N}=\frac{100}{539},\qquad</cite>, below $1/2$.

Bit network: vertices get projections $P_U$ onto labels, and $-P_{U_a}$ at the source of $X_a$. For nested $U\subset V$, $P_V-P_U$ is the identity on $V\cap U^\perp$ and zero elsewhere, so has rank $\dim V-\dim U$. The first edge at each $X_a$ is $2P_{U_a}$, rank one despite zero dimension change, adding $N$: $s_{\rm b}=Wm-N+2L_{\rm b}<Wm$.

Complex network: $q_U(x)=\operatorname{wt}(P_Ux)\bmod4$ (Hamming weight $\operatorname{wt}$), $C=H\operatorname{diag}(1,i)H=aI+bX$, $a=\tfrac{1+i}2$, $b=\tfrac{1-i}2$, and frame $\mathcal C_U=H^{\otimes m}\operatorname{diag}_x(i^{q_U(x)})H^{\otimes m}$. Weight is additive mod 4 on orthogonal vectors: $\operatorname{wt}(z+w)=\operatorname{wt}(z)+\operatorname{wt}(w)-2|\operatorname{supp}z\cap\operatorname{supp}w|$. A residual has an orthonormal basis $\mathcal B$ (it has a norm-one coordinate vector):
$$q_V-q_U=\sum_{v\in\mathcal B}\operatorname{wt}(v)[v\cdot x]\pmod4,$$
with $\operatorname{wt}(v)$ odd. Each term $i^{\varepsilon[v\cdot x]}$, $\varepsilon=\pm1$, equals $\frac{1+i^\varepsilon}2+\frac{1-i^\varepsilon}2(-1)^{v\cdot x}$, and $H\operatorname{diag}(1,-1)H=X$ makes it the kernel $aI+bX_v$ or its inverse. One kernel per basis vector gives $s_{\rm c}=Wm-2N+2L_{\rm c}<Wm$ <cite data-source="paper">s_{\rm c}=W_{\rm c}m-2N+2L_{\rm c}</cite>. Using $C=iZC^{-1}Z$ on $X_a\to Y_a$, every role receives $C^{\otimes m}$.
