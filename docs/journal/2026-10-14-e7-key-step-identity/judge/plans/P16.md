# Plan P16

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **S0** (show, page `main-theorem`): A single deterministic multitape Turing machine multiplies $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$, refuting the $n\log n$ optimality conjecture in this model.
  - method: Build the multiplier as a reduction of the product to a convolution evaluated by synthetic polynomial transforms, give its two cost drivers (address rearrangement and butterfly arithmetic) tape procedures that cost a power of the logarithm less than a plain pass by a rank-saving recursion, then prove exactness and add up the costs.
  - motivation (explicit): A plain transform scans $\Theta(n)$ bits at each of $\Theta(\log n)$ levels, already $\Theta(n\log n)$, so both the data movement and the arithmetic must be made cheaper than a full pass per level.
  - relies on: schonhage-strassen-conjecture, hh-multiplier, multitape-turing-machine, big-o-notation, limits-and-limit-inferior, asymptotic-notation-beyond-big-o

    - method: Prove the bound first for widths that are powers of $m$ with enough rows (the recursion on the finite bit network), then handle arbitrary widths by moving a few high digits to supply rows, splitting the remaining width in base $m$, and padding binary widths to radix $q$.
    - motivation (explicit): The power-width recursion needs a row field divisible by $W^k$ and a width of the form $m^k$; both are supplied for general arrays by the chunk's own high digits and a base-$m$ decomposition.
