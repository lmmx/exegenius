# Plan P14

The plan of the argument for the main theorem, as a tree of steps.

- **R** (show, page `main-theorem`): One deterministic fixed-tape Turing machine multiplies two $n$-bit integers exactly in $O(n(\lg n)^{1-\kappa})$ time with $\kappa=2^{-182}$.
  - method: Reduce the product to a convolution evaluated by Gaussian-resampling, Bluestein-chirp and synthetic-transform steps, speed up the address movement and the butterfly layers of those transforms with two recursive tape procedures that each save a power of the logarithm, and then fix parameters, recover the integers and add up the cost.
  - motivation (explicit): Scanning the whole array at each of the $\Theta(\log n)$ transform levels already costs $\Theta(n\log n)$, so both movement and arithmetic need savings.
  - relies on: nlogn-history, fixed-tape-model, established-multiplier, big-o, turing-machine, worst-case-running-time-and-subroutines, schoolbook-integer-arithmetic
  - **T** (show): There are fixed-tape procedures that interchange two address chunks of $u$ bits in $O(Vu^{\tau})$ time and apply a butterfly layer to $D\le d$ address chunks in $O(Vd^{\lambda'})$ time, for fixed $\tau,\lambda'<1$ and data of volume $V$.
    - method: Obtain each procedure from a fixed linear network that exchanges two banks of values and restores its scratch wires, whose changes of representation along its edges are smaller calls of the same procedure, strictly fewer than $Wm$ in total.
    - motivation (explicit): A strict inequality on the number $s$ of smaller calls makes the recursive contribution grow by a factor below $m$ per level, and this strict inequality is the source of the power saving.
