## Question

With the binary (`cc70355`), configuration, definition (`exegenius-planner`), model (Sonnet), prompts and runner protocol held fixed, how do plans made at **medium** effort compare with plans made at **high**?

## Arms

| Arm | Runs | Configuration | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| medium | M1–M5 | `exegenius-planner`, `model: "sonnet"`, `effort: "medium"` | wiki branch `131026-exp-e3-effort` |
| high | P1–P5 | the same, with `effort: "high"` | E1's P arm, wiki branch `131026-exp-e1-config` (85611ce) |

**Why the high arm is reused.** E1's P arm ran under this protocol on the same binary, an hour earlier. Re-running it would cost about 28 USD, by E1's runner report, and would control only the time of day and the runner session. Those differences remain, and are stated as limitations.

**Checking the effort actually applied.** The transcripts are checked with `calibration/tools/effort.py`. A run whose subagents did not all run at the arm's effort is excluded and reported.

## Judging

**One blind batch, J3,** with 12 plans:
- M1–M5 and P1–P5;
- the anchors `sonnet1` and `C2`, already judged in J1 and J2.

**How:**
- the same pack (byte-identical to J1's and J2's) and the same instructions as J2, plus the section of `calibration/rubrics/nlogn-central-saving.md`;
- `prepare.py` with seed 20261015;
- general-purpose Opus judges, with the key held outside the judges' directory;
- second, independent judgements of three plans, drawn with `random.Random(20261015).sample(range(1, 13), 3)`.

## Measures

**Primary,** per plan, from the content judge (as in 2026-10-13-content-judge): core results derived, and derived faithfully, of 19.

**Secondary:**
- core results stated in substance, and stated fully;
- statements judged wrong;
- major and minor errors;
- **the central-derivation count (0–9) and gate** (the rubric), reported separately and never combined with the item counts.

**Resources,** per run:
- seconds per planning subagent;
- model calls, input, cache-read and cache-write tokens (measured), and output tokens (estimated at 4 characters per token: transcripts undercount output);
- rejected submits;
- the runner session's reported cost, whose basis includes orchestration.

## Decision rules, fixed now

**R1 (effort, primary).** High effort is preferred for planning on this paper when the high arm exceeds the medium arm by **at least 4** in median core results derived **and at least 2** in median core results derived faithfully.
- The exact one-sided Mann–Whitney test on each per-plan count is reported beside it, with $p<0.05$ (5 against 5) taken as distinguishing.
- **If one condition holds and the other does not,** the result is **inconclusive**. The sequential rule of `calibration/PROTOCOL.md` then decides whether more runs are worth their cost.

**R2 (the central derivation).** Reported separately from R1:
- the count's medians and ranges per arm;
- how many plans in each arm pass the gate.

R1 can hold while R2 shows that neither arm reconstructs the saving. In that case the report says so in its first lines, and it does not call either arm research-grade on this paper.

**R3 (cost).** The ratio of high to medium per planning run, reported on:
- tokens read from cache;
- estimated output;
- seconds.

The recommendation states the quality gained per unit of these. No monetary or subscription-quota figure is stated except as reported by a session, with its basis.

**What R1 changes.** If R1 holds, the recommendation is that planning on papers of this kind should run at high effort when affordable. Changing exegenius's defaults remains the author's decision. If R1 fails or is inconclusive, no change is recommended.

## Known limitations, stated in advance

- The arms ran in different runner sessions, an hour apart.
- The reference is an unreviewed draft. These are comparisons between configurations, not certifications of correctness.
- One paper. The result is scoped to a frontier mathematics paper of this kind and to `claude-sonnet-5-5` as served on 2026-10-13.

## Addendum, before any E3 plan existed

Added after review by the author (relayed from ChatGPT), and before any medium-effort plan existed. Nothing above is changed.

**A1. The first runner's failure is an operational deviation, not a result.**
- **What happened:** the first runner (session started 21:01:48 UTC) was refused the exegenius build (`Code from External`). It had registered the clone in the same turn as the build. It made no run.
- **The record:** its report is kept as `results-attempt-1.md` on the branch, and the registry spec lists it under `deviations`.
- **Counting:** no run is counted as failed because of it.

**A2. Material differences between the arms are verified before interpretation.** These are checked mechanically and recorded in the E3 report, as identical or different, before any comparison is read:
- **Source:** the sha256 of `template/sources/paper.tex` on both branches.
- **Configuration:** `template/exegenius.toml` on both branches, and each run's `exegenius.toml`.
- **Definitions and settings:** the sha256 of every file under `.claude/`.
- **Task prompts and task files:** each planning task file (`exegenius/tasks/000N-*.md`) of M1 against P1, compared with run-directory paths normalised. Each task's prompt as the runner passed it, from the subagents' first messages, also normalised.
- **Binary:** the commit each runner built, from `results/versions.txt`.
- **Runner protocol:** a diff of `EXPERIMENT.md` between the branches.
- **The recorded model and effort** of every subagent, and of each runner's orchestrator.
- **Time and session:** when the runs ran, and in which runner session.

Any difference other than effort, time and session is reported. The report says how it could bear on the result. The report does not call the effort effect "isolated". It calls the comparison sequential and partly re-used, with these differences documented.

**A3. Earlier verdicts stay separate.**
- **The batch:** J3 re-judges E1's P plans in its own directory.
- **J2 is kept apart:** its verdicts remain in `docs/journal/2026-10-13-e1-config/judge/`, unchanged, and are never mixed with J3's in an analysis.
- **The R1 comparison** uses J3 alone.

**A4. Does the rubric discriminate?** This is secondary and descriptive, reported beside R2:
- **Agreement between judges:** per step, for the three plans judged twice.
- **The count's spread within each arm:** a rubric on which every plan scores the same tells nothing.
- **Item counts against the rubric:** whether plans with similar content-judge counts (core derived) differ on the central-derivation count, and the reverse.
- **The anchors:** the two anchor plans' central-derivation results.

A rubric that does not separate plans, or on which judges disagree on most steps, is reported as not yet a valid instrument, whatever R2 shows.

**A5. The rubric stays fixed.** It is changed only if a genuine defect is found, such as a wrong line reference or a step the paper does not contain. In that case it is versioned (`nlogn-central-saving.v2.md`), the change is explained, and verdicts under each version are kept apart.

**A6. Evidence for R1 is provisional** (stage 2 of `PROTOCOL.md` §6). It supports a scoped recommendation, not a confirmatory claim. Confirmation, if a default is to change, needs fresh runs of both arms in one runner.
