## The decision it informs

**The question it answers.** In `policy.md`, the argument-planning row's open question (3) is whether a stronger model is better *above* the ceiling. The judging row says the instrument is "adequate below the ceiling; blind above it".

**Why the current measures cannot answer it:**
- **The primary rubric saturates.** With the combined instruction, every judgement of Sonnet `high` plans scores 19/19 faithful and 9/9 central (E9: F2, F4, F8).
- **The checklist is near its own top.** Y plans score 24–26 of 26 (J20). E9F8Y against E8F8X is already "within noise" there.

**What K3 decides:**
1. **Validation:** whether pairwise preference judging tracks known orderings without position or order effects. If it does, it is adopted as the measure for comparisons at the ceiling.
2. **Use:** the comparison it then makes possible, Opus `xhigh` against Sonnet `high`, both with the combined instruction. That comparison is preregistered separately; its design is sketched below so that the validation is sized for it.

## The instrument

**What a judge sees** (the prompt in full is in `rubrics/pairwise-judge-prompt.md`):
- the reference pack;
- the mechanism checklist;
- the paper;
- two plans of the same extraction, labelled A and B.

The labels and the order are assigned by `pairwise.py` from a recorded seed, and each pair is judged in both orders.

**What it returns:**
- for each of the 26 reference items, and for the chain to the main theorem: a preference (A, B or tie), its strength (slight or clear), and the concrete differences behind it, each located in both plans and in the paper;
- errors in either plan;
- an overall preference, with its reason.

**How it compares:**
- **The six discriminating results** of the checklist are compared sub-step by sub-step, so a preference names the sub-steps that differ.
- **What counts, in order:** correctness, then the paper's essential steps with their defining content, then completeness (cases, constants, conditions, dependencies).
- **What does not count:** length, restatement, wording and quote choice.
- **Ties** are allowed and must be chosen whenever no concrete difference can be named.

**Why a comparison should resolve where the rubrics do not:**
- Absolute categories have a top. Two plans that both derive a result "faithfully", or both carry 25 of 26 sub-steps, are indistinguishable to them.
- A comparison has no top. It can register one case, one constant, one condition or one dependency that one plan carries and the other lacks.

**The risks that come with it:**

| Risk | What guards against it |
|---|---|
| inventing differences | identical pairs |
| order and position bias | both orders; identical pairs |
| rewarding length | the prompt; the length-matched pair; reading the reasons |
| judge noise | two judges per file |

**A known confound: length.** In three of the four known pairs, the better plan is also the longer one. E9F2Y against E8F2X (95.5 against 96.7 KB rendered) is length-matched, so it carries the length-neutral test.

**Totals:** 12 pair files and 20 judgements, with Opus judges (`general-purpose`, `model: "opus"`), as in J18.

**What each pair tests:**
- **The wide pairs (F4, F8):** gaps of 14 or more faithful, and 14 and 17 items. They check that the instrument is not broken, so one judge per order is enough.
- **The two F2 pairs:** gaps of 3 faithful, and two to four items. They are the finest known orderings, and they carry the judge-to-judge test.
- **The identical pairs:** any non-tie verdict on them is a false preference, so they measure invented differences and the position those differences favour.
  - A judge may notice that the two files are identical. So the identical pairs bound false preferences, and position bias is read mainly from the swapped orders.
- **The ablation pair** is the only known ordering at the ceiling. It tests whether a difference of one sub-step is resolved.
  - The removed step is a leaf, and no step refers to it.
  - The gap it leaves in the step ids is not distinctive: ids in real plans are irregular. 59 of E9F2Y's 258 ids are off the regular scheme, and E9F4Y skips S4.5.2.6. No plan in `scratchpad/e6` refers to step ids.

**Each pair in both orders.** `pairwise.py prepare` writes a pair in both orders (X as A, then Y as A). An identical pair has one file. All files are numbered in an order shuffled by the seed.

## (b) Acceptance criteria (proposed; fixed at preregistration)

**1. Agreement with the known ordering:**
- **(a)** All 4 overall verdicts on the wide pairs prefer the E8 plan.
- **(b)** On each F2 pair, at least 3 of its 4 verdicts prefer the known-better plan, and none prefers the worse.
- **(c) Items.** The 37 items whose primary verdicts differ beyond noise (14 + 17 + 4 + 2) are compared with the pairwise majority on each item.
  - Met if the pairwise majority agrees on at least 0.80 of them and reverses on at most 2.

**2. Position bias:**
- **(a) Identical pairs:**
  - at most 1 of the 4 overall verdicts is a preference, and none is `clear`;
  - item false preferences are at most 0.10 of item verdicts (at most 10 of 108).
- **(b) Different plans:** the share of non-tie *item* verdicts given to position A is within 0.40–0.60.
  - The share of overall verdicts is reported only: the balanced design makes it 0.5 for any judge consistent across orders.

**3. Consistency across swapped order**, read on couples of verdicts from the two orders of a pair, mapped to plan names:
- **(a) Overall:**
  - no reversal on any known pair;
  - agreement on at least 0.80 of couples.
- **(b) Items:**
  - reversals on at most 0.05 of item couples;
  - cross-order item agreement no more than 0.10 below the judge-to-judge item agreement. The gap isolates the effect of order from ordinary judge noise.

**4. Judge-to-judge agreement**, on the 6 double-judged files: the four F2 files and the two identical ones.
- **(a) Overall:** the two judges give the same overall verdict on at least 5 of 6 files, and never reverse.
- **(b) Items:** reversals on at most 0.05 of item couples. Raw agreement and Cohen's κ are reported.

**5. Resolution at the ceiling** (reported, not a condition of adoption): on the ablation pair,
- the intact plan is preferred in at least 3 of 4 verdicts, and the ablated plan in none;
- and the deciding difference is located at `label-residual-budget` (L5) or `rank-saving` (R1).

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| 1–4 met | adopted as the measure for comparisons above the ceiling. The ceiling comparison (c) may be preregistered. Criterion 5 sets the smallest difference a result may claim |
| 1 met; 2 or 3 not met | order-dependent: never used in one order. One prompt revision (for example, reading the two plans in separate passes) and one revalidation, in a fresh directory; if it fails again, dropped |
| 4 not met; 1–3 met | adopted with two judges per order in every use (twice the cost) |
| 1 not met | not adopted. The question "is a stronger planner better above the ceiling" goes to downstream outcomes (W2's page fidelity and simulated readers) or to the second document |
| 5 not resolved | reported. A ceiling result may claim only differences larger than one sub-step |

## (c) Use at the ceiling: Opus `xhigh` against Sonnet `high`, both with the combined instruction (sketch, for a separate preregistration)

**Preconditions:**
- E10 fixes the instruction (runbook §9, P0);
- K3 is adopted;
- the decision is live: a proposal or default turns on it.

**The design:**
- **Runs:** six Opus `xhigh` runs with the combined instruction, one on each extraction where a Sonnet `high` Y plan already exists:

  | Extraction | Sonnet `high` Y plan |
  |---|---|
  | F2 | E9F2Y |
  | F3 | E10F3Y |
  | F4 | E9F4Y |
  | F5 | E10F5Y |
  | F6 | E10F6Y |
  | F8 | E9F8Y |

  Six extractions make a sign test able to reach p < 0.05.
- **A sanity screen:** a checklist screen of the six Opus plans, with Opus and Sonnet judges. Primary judging only if the pairwise result says Opus is worse and the size of that loss matters.
- **The control:** E9F4Y against E10F4Y2, two Sonnet `high` Y runs on F4. It measures run-to-run variance on the pairwise scale.
- **Judging:**
  - 7 pairs, both orders, one judge per order: 14 judgements;
  - **sequential second judges:** where a pair's two verdicts disagree, a second judge in each order (+2 per such pair).

**The reading per extraction.** Net = (verdicts preferring O) − (verdicts preferring S).
- **O is favoured** when net ≥ 2 and O wins at least one verdict in each order: with 2 verdicts, both; with 4, net ≥ 2 and a win in each order.
- **S is favoured** under the symmetric rule.
- **Otherwise the extraction is tied** and dropped from the sign test. That includes any extraction with a reversal across orders.

**The sign test** is one-sided, with H1 "O better" fixed in advance. P = P(Bin(n, ½) ≥ k) over the n untied extractions, of which O is favoured on k.

| O favoured on | Ties | One-sided p | Shown |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 of 6 | 0 | 0.016 | yes |
| 5 of 5 | 1 | 0.031 | yes |
| 5 of 6 (S once) | 0 | 0.109 | no |
| 4 of 4 | 2 | 0.0625 | no |

So "O is better above the ceiling" needs O favoured on at least 5 extractions and S on none.

**Run variance.** Each extraction contributes one run per configuration, so the sign test is valid under run variance. Runbook §6's proposal rule, however, also needs the gain to exceed run variance. So a *proposal* additionally needs the control pair tied (|net| ≤ 1). If the control is not tied, two runs of one configuration differ as visibly as O and S, and a second control (an Opus repeat) is preregistered before any proposal.

**The Bradley–Terry reading** (`pairwise.py score --arm O=… --arm S=…`):
- **The model:** strengths π, with P(i preferred to j) = π_i / (π_i + π_j). Ties count as half a win, and one pseudo-tie per compared couple keeps the estimates finite.
- **The interval:** the extractions (pairs) are the bootstrap units, giving an interval for log(π_O/π_S).
- **With two arms,** it adds only an effect size, P(O preferred), and its interval. The decision stays with the sign test: six units make a coarse bootstrap.
- **It decides when three or more configurations share the scale,** for example Opus `max`, or S6's Sonnet `medium` and Haiku `high` if they hold the ceiling. Each is run on the same extractions and paired with the Sonnet `high` Y plan in both orders. Configuration i is above j when the 95% bootstrap interval of log(π_i/π_j) excludes 0.

**Outcomes:**

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| O better, control tied | `policy.md` records that a stronger model is better above the ceiling, at its measured cost per run. A proposal goes to the author, who weighs the gain against Opus `xhigh`'s cost |
| O not shown better | Sonnet `high` with the combined instruction stays the candidate. Opus is not worth its cost on this paper's argument task |
| S favoured on ≥ 5 extractions | reported, as evidence for Sonnet `high` |

**Same-model bias.** With Opus judges, the Opus plan has the judge's own model. K2 found no bias on the checklist (+0.43), but a direct comparison may invite more self-preference. The Sonnet-judge variant (e) is the cross-check: it is flagged if the Opus and Sonnet judges' net preference for O differs in sign on 2 or more extractions.

## (d) Cost, in cache-read tokens

**The basis is measured.** It comes from the subagent transcripts of J18's 50 Opus primary judgements, with plans of 11–280 KB rendered:
- cache read ≈ **3.28 M + 1.53 M per 100 KB of rendered plan**, with mean 4.82 M, 27 calls and a peak context of 257 k;
- J16's 20 judgements give the same line (3.38 M + 1.50 M);
- the intercept is the pack, the paper's lines and the verdict; the slope is reading and re-reading the plan.

**For a pairwise judgement:**
- the same intercept, since the pack and the paper's lines serve both plans;
- plus the slope times the *sum* of the two plans;
- plus the checklist (6.5 KB, about 0.1 M);
- plus an overhead for comparing, assumed to be 0–30% and measured by the pilot. J18's own estimate was 3 M, and the measured cost was 4.8 M.

`pairwise.py prepare` prints this estimate for each pair file.

## (e) Cheaper variants

**1. Sonnet judges, once Opus has validated the instrument** (as K2 did for the checklist):
- **The check:** Sonnet judges the stage-B F2 files and the ablation files once each, 6 judgements, about 40–55 M.
- **Adopted if** Sonnet's overall verdict matches the Opus judges' majority on at least 5 of 6 with no reversal, and its item reversals against Opus are at most 0.05.
- **Its use at the ceiling:** Sonnet judges in both orders, with one Opus judge per pair as a check. The bias reading in (c) applies.
- **Caveat: it saves price, not tokens.** On the same plans, Sonnet checklist judges read *more* from cache than Opus judges:
  - K2: 0.81 M against 0.63 M (J11);
  - J20: 1.12 M against 0.91 M.

  The saving is in price per token, which this programme does not convert (runbook §5). In cache-read tokens this variant is about the same, or about 20% more.

**2. Focused pairwise, on the six discriminating results and errors only, with no pack:**
- **What it saves:** this one does save tokens. From J20's checklist fit (0.62 M + 0.30 M per 100 KB), a 200 KB pair would cost about 1.2–1.6 M, roughly a quarter of the full judgement.
- **Its blind spot:** it cannot see differences outside the six results.
  - On the F2 pairs, 2 of E9F2Y's 4 differing items are among the six (`label-residual-budget`, `matrix-shear`), and 1 of E8F2X's 2 (`complex-phase-interface`). So the same pair files would show what it misses.
  - It needs its own prompt (not written): section 1 of this one, restricted to the six.

**3. Sequential judging:** one judge per order, and a second only where the two disagree. It is already in the designs above, and saves up to half against two judges per order.

**4. One order per pair:** only if the validation shows no order effect at all (no reversals; item position share within 0.45–0.55). The orders are then balanced across pairs (half with O as A), which halves the cost. Not before.
