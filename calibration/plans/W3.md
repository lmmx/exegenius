## Reading (fixed at preregistration)

**Per key-step page:** W2b's rule. Opus is better on a page if:
- its three faithful values are all at least Sonnet's median;
- its median exceeds Sonnet's;
- and it has no more major errors by median.

Sonnet is better under the symmetric rule. Otherwise the page is tied.

**Per control page:** Opus is better if its median major errors are lower, or if they are equal and its median minor errors are lower. Sonnet is better under the symmetric rule.

| Outcome | Action |
|---|---|
| Opus better on ≥ 5 of the 6 key-step pages, and on at most 1 of 3 controls | the advantage generalises to key-step pages. Propose the split with its measured cost; the author decides |
| Opus better on ≥ 5 of 6 key-step pages and on ≥ 2 of 3 controls | Opus writes better in general, not only on key-step pages. Reported with its cost; the author decides |
| Opus better on 2–4 of the 6 key-step pages | the advantage is page-specific. No rollout. The exception stays at 0015 and 0016, or is withdrawn by the author |
| Opus better on ≤ 1 of the 6 key-step pages | W2b's result does not generalise. Recommend withdrawing the exception |

**The rows are read on k, the number of key-step pages on which Opus is better.** Every k from 0 to 6 falls in exactly one row. Pages where Sonnet is better are reported with the outcome; they do not change the row.

**Notes on the reading:**
- A one-sided sign test on 5 of 6 gives p ≈ 0.11, and on 6 of 6 p ≈ 0.016. The ≥ 5-of-6 bar is a screen, not proof. The result is read together with W2b's 2 of 2.
- **Run variance:** W2b saw a 2-point spread between two Sonnet runs of one page. With one run per page, pages are the units and that variance stays in the per-page noise.

## Can the table decide? A check before any run, with a secondary analysis added (11:06 UTC, 2026-10-15; no run has started)

**Asked by the author at about 11:00:** will W3's preregistered outcomes distinguish a useful selective-writing policy from an inconclusive result?

**The check.** Treat each key-step page as an independent trial on which Opus is better with probability p (W2b's per-page rule; ties and Sonnet wins otherwise).

| p (Opus better per page) | P(k ≥ 5): "generalises" | P(2 ≤ k ≤ 4): "page-specific" | P(k ≤ 1): "does not generalise" |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.25 | 0.005 | 0.46 | 0.53 |
| 0.35 | 0.02 | 0.66 | 0.32 |
| 0.50 | 0.11 | 0.78 | 0.11 |
| 0.70 | 0.42 | 0.57 | 0.01 |
| 0.80 | 0.66 | 0.34 | 0.002 |
| 0.90 | 0.89 | 0.11 | 0 |

**What it shows:**
- **"Generalises" is well protected against false positives:** under 0.03 when Opus wins on at most a third of pages.
- **The middle row is the likely outcome over a wide range** (p from 0.25 to 0.70). It cannot tell "no general advantage" from "a real but moderate one". Its action (no rollout) is the status quo, so that outcome mostly confirms the current policy.
- **Ceiling pages cut the chance of "generalises".** Two of the six key-step pages score few items (0021: 3 ids; 0033: 2 ids), where both writers may be perfect and tie. If they tie with probability 0.8, P(k ≥ 5) falls to 0.13 at p = 0.8 and 0.22 at p = 0.9.
- **The k ≤ 1 row** recommends withdrawing the exception. But the exception for 0015 and 0016 rests on direct evidence (W2b) that W3 does not retest. At the reading, the session will report that alongside the row's action, which stays as written.

**The secondary analysis, added before any run (it does not change the primary table above).** The policy question behind W3 is whether Sonnet `high` meets the fidelity standard on key-step pages, and whether Opus fixes the pages where it does not. Item-count wins answer that only indirectly.

- **The standard, per run and page:** the median major-error count over the three judges is 0, and the median faithful count is at least ⌈0.9 n⌉ of the page's n scored ids (n from `docs/journal/2026-10-15-w3-setup/assigned.json`). That is all of them for n ≤ 9; one may be missed at n = 13–19.
- **Counts:**
  - F_S: key-step pages where Sonnet fails the standard;
  - F_O: those where Opus fails it;
  - R: pages where Sonnet fails and Opus meets it.

| Secondary outcome | Reading |
|---|---|
| F_S ≤ 1 | Sonnet `high` meets the fidelity standard on at least 5 of 6 key-step pages. Opus routing beyond 0015 and 0016 is not needed for fidelity, whatever k says |
| R ≥ 2 and F_O = 0 | Sonnet fails the standard on key-step pages where Opus meets it. **This is the fidelity case for routing key-step pages to Opus.** It is reported with its measured cost; the author decides |
| otherwise | inconclusive on fidelity; reported as such |

**Reporting.** Both the primary and the secondary outcome are reported. Where they disagree, the result says so, and the author decides.

**An option for the author, not adopted:** a second Sonnet run on each of the six key-step pages, judged in the same batch.
- **What it adds:** it doubles the sample for Sonnet's failure rate, which is what the fidelity question turns on: one of two Sonnet runs on 0015 had a major error, and one run per page cannot tell a 10% failure rate from a 50% one.
- **Cost:** about 5–25 M for the runs plus about 8–10 M for 18 more judgements.

## Amendment 2: a second Sonnet run on each key-step page, and a stricter fidelity rule (2026-10-17 06:49 UTC; no W3 run has started)

**Why.** The author asked (2026-10-17) for the smallest design that can tell a useful selective-Opus policy from ordinary run-to-run variation. The session simulated each design under fixed rules (`docs/journal/2026-10-17-w3-design/`).
- **The model:** pages differ in difficulty (Beta, concentration 4).
- **A failure:** a page-run that fails the fidelity standard above.
- **Four worlds:**
  - A, routing useful: Sonnet fails 30%, Opus 3%;
  - B, moderate: Sonnet 20%, Opus 3%;
  - C, not needed: Sonnet 5%, Opus 3%;
  - D, run variance only: both fail 20%.

| Design and rule | A: route / adequate / inconclusive | B | C | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Current: 1 Sonnet run, the 11:06 secondary** (adequate if F_S ≤ 1 of 6) | 0.49 / **0.42** / 0.10 | 0.28 / **0.66** / 0.06 | 0.03 / 0.97 / 0.00 | 0.07 / 0.65 / 0.28 |
| **2 Sonnet runs per key-step page, rule R2 below** | 0.60 / 0.11 / 0.29 | 0.37 / 0.30 / 0.33 | 0.03 / 0.87 / 0.11 | 0.08 / 0.31 / 0.61 |
| 2 Sonnet runs, adequate only at 0 of 12 | 0.60 / 0.02 / 0.38 | 0.37 / 0.09 / 0.54 | 0.03 / 0.58 / 0.40 | 0.08 / 0.09 / 0.82 |
| 2 Sonnet and 2 Opus runs per page | 0.67 / 0.11 / 0.22 | 0.41 / 0.30 / 0.29 | 0.03 / 0.86 / 0.11 | 0.08 / 0.30 / 0.61 |
| 9 key-step pages, 1 run each | 0.40 / 0.04 / 0.55 | 0.20 / 0.14 / 0.67 | 0.01 / 0.63 / 0.36 | 0.02 / 0.13 / 0.85 |

**What it shows:**
- **The 11:06 secondary rule is unsafe.** It clears Sonnet as adequate 42% of the time when Sonnet fails 30% of key-step page-runs, and 66% of the time at 20%. One run per page cannot separate a page Sonnet always handles from a lucky run.
- **A second Sonnet run** cuts those wrong clearances to 0.11 and 0.30. It keeps the correct clearance in world C at 0.87 and false routing at 3–8%.
- **Second Opus runs** add little: Opus failures are rare in the evidence so far (0 of 4 key-step page-runs).
- **More pages at one run each** clears less often, wrongly and rightly alike, and leaves most outcomes inconclusive.

**The design, fixed now.**
- **Added runs:** six Sonnet `high` runs, **WS2**, one on each key-step page (0018, 0021, 0031, 0032, 0033, 0036), in fresh copies. They are judged in J26 alongside the others, blind, by the same page judge. Controls get no second run.
- **The primary analysis is unchanged.** It uses the preregistered run of each arm (WS and WO) and the table under "Reading". WS2 enters only the fidelity analysis.
- **The fidelity analysis is replaced by rule R2.** The 11:06 rule is kept in the record above but superseded, because it is unsafe. Over the six key-step pages, with 12 Sonnet page-runs and 6 Opus page-runs, judged by the standard above:

| R2 outcome | Reading |
|---|---|
| at most 1 of the 12 Sonnet page-runs fails | Sonnet `high` meets the fidelity standard on key-step pages at this sample. Opus routing beyond 0015 and 0016 is not needed for fidelity |
| at least 3 of 12 fail, on at least 2 pages where Opus met the standard, and no Opus page-run fails | **the fidelity case for routing key-step pages to Opus.** Reported with its measured cost; the author decides |
| otherwise | inconclusive on fidelity |

**Remaining weaknesses, stated rather than assumed solved:**
- **Wrong clearances:** R2 still clears Sonnet wrongly 11% of the time in world A and 30% in world B.
- **Inconclusive outcomes:** about a third of outcomes are inconclusive in A and B.
- **Scope:** six pages of one paper. The result supports a screen and a provisional rule, not a general default.
- **Model assumptions:** the simulation's difficulty model and its failure rates are assumptions, anchored only on W2 and W2b (Sonnet failed 1 of 4 key-step page-runs; Opus 0 of 4).

**Cost:** about +10–35 M (six Sonnet runs at 0.34–4.06 M each, plus 18 judgements at 0.43–0.54 M). W3 is now **~80–175 M** in all.

**Why the thresholds are set this way:**
- **No conversion is possible.** The meter weighs components differently, and the two intervals measured so far disagree: about 318 M for 65% on 2026-10-14, and about 175 M for 80% on 2026-10-15. A window has held anything from about 220 M to about 490 M of cache read.
- **The thresholds assume the lower figure** (~2.2 M per 1%). On that assumption, stage 1 needs about 70% of a window and stage 2 about 27%.
- **The meter is authoritative.** If the reading is higher than these limits, the stage waits for the reset.
- **Splitting is safe.** Accepted pages persist in their copies, so judging can follow in a later window without loss or deviation.

## Stopping and deviations

- One run per configuration and page; no second stage. A tied page is reported as tied.
- A run that fails to submit an accepted page is rerun once in a fresh copy; a second failure is reported, and that page counts as tied.
- Judging uses J22's page judge unchanged. A judge that returns no verdict is rerun once.
- Any deviation is recorded in the ledger below with its time, before the result is read.
